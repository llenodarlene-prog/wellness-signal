Botox is a brand of botulinum toxin, a medicine that relaxes the muscles it's injected into. In cosmetic use, that softens lines caused by repeated facial movement. It's one of the most popular cosmetic treatments, and also one where the setting and the injector matter a great deal.

These answers draw on the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) and MedlinePlus from the National Library of Medicine. They cover what Botox does, how long it lasts, side effects and safety.

## The Basics

What Botox is and what it's approved to treat.

### What is Botox?

Botox is the brand name for onabotulinumtoxinA, a purified form of botulinum toxin. It works as a neurotoxin. Injected in tiny amounts, it temporarily relaxes the targeted muscles. When those muscles can't contract as strongly, the lines they create soften.

### What is Botox approved to treat?

MedlinePlus says Botox Cosmetic is used in adults 18 and older to temporarily smooth:

- frown lines between the eyebrows
- crow's feet near the outer corners of the eyes
- forehead lines
- vertical bands connecting the jaw and neck

MedlinePlus lists other uses of the same medicine for medical conditions, including severe underarm sweating, chronic migraine and some eye, muscle and bladder disorders.

### Will I lose my facial expressions?

You shouldn't, if it's done well. The AAD says dermatologists inject just enough to weaken and relax the targeted muscles without affecting others. That lets you keep natural expressions. Botox also doesn't affect your ability to feel in the treated area.

## Results and Aftercare

What to expect after your appointment.

### How long does Botox last?

The AAD says the effect on lines and wrinkles lasts about 3 to 4 months, and sometimes longer. For excessive underarm sweating, one treatment can give up to 6 months of relief. You can be treated again when the lines or sweating return. With repeat treatments, the treated muscle may thin, so results can last longer.

### Is there any downtime after Botox?

Usually not. The AAD says most people go back to everyday activities right away and can apply makeup as soon as they leave the treatment room. Some people get mild bruising at the injection sites, which can last about a week.

### What should I avoid after Botox?

The AAD gives a few rules to stop the medicine spreading where it isn't wanted:

1. Don't rub or massage the injected areas.
2. If you wear makeup, don't press or rub it in.
3. Wait 2 hours before strenuous activity, including heavy lifting, workouts or anything that raises your heart rate.

## Side Effects and Safety

Most side effects are mild, but the setting matters.

### What are the side effects of Botox?

The AAD says side effects tend to be mild and temporary, and most come from the injections rather than the toxin. People have had swelling, redness, soreness and bruising. Mild headache is uncommon. Rarely, a nearby muscle weakens, causing a temporarily droopy brow or eyelid. Problems breathing, swallowing or speaking are very rare.

### Can Botox spread beyond where it's injected?

It can. MedlinePlus carries an important warning that the medicine may spread from the injection site and affect other muscles. If it affects the muscles for breathing or swallowing, the problems can be serious. MedlinePlus notes this hasn't been seen at the recommended doses used to treat wrinkles. Get urgent help for trouble breathing, swallowing or speaking after treatment.

### Can I get Botox while pregnant?

The AAD says it isn't known whether botulinum toxin therapy can harm an unborn child. Tell your provider if you're pregnant or planning to become pregnant.

### Where should I not get Botox injections?

The AAD warns against getting injections at a non-medical spa, a party, a salon or someone's home. It also warns never to buy botulinum toxin online. A product you can buy without a medical license differs greatly from what a dermatologist uses, and has caused serious complications.

## Cost and Choosing a Provider

What you'll pay and who should inject you.

### How much does Botox cost?

Prices vary by city, provider, product and how many units you need. The AAD notes that most insurance plans don't pay for cosmetic treatment. Some patients pay part of the cost when Botox treats excessive sweating, so check your coverage first.

### How do I choose a Botox injector?

Look for a licensed medical professional with training in facial anatomy, working in a medical setting, using product from a legitimate source. The AAD advises that when medical staff give the injections, the dermatologist should be in the office and immediately available.
