A computer workstation that doesn't fit you makes you reach, hunch or tilt your head for hours. Small adjustments to your desk, chair and screen can make a big difference to comfort. OSHA notes that there's no single correct posture for everyone, so the goal is a setup that fits your body and lets you change position.

These answers draw on OSHA's computer workstation guidance and a Cochrane review of ways to cut sitting at work. To work out heights for your own body, use the [Desk Height Calculator](/desk-height-calculator/).

## Desk and Keyboard

Get your hands and arms in the right place first.

### What is the correct desk height?

The right height depends on your arms, not a standard number. OSHA says your keyboard should sit at about elbow height, with your elbows close to your body and your shoulders relaxed. So your desk or keyboard-tray surface should be roughly your elbow height minus your keyboard's thickness.

### What height should my keyboard be?

About elbow height. OSHA's guidance says elbows should be about the same height as the keyboard and hang comfortably at your sides. Its purchasing guide says keyboard height should adjust from 22 to 30 inches for seated work and 36 to 46.5 inches for standing work.

### What if my desk is too high and doesn't adjust?

OSHA suggests raising your chair until your elbows reach keyboard height, then using a footrest so your feet stay supported. If the desk or chair can't be adjusted properly, a keyboard tray can bring the keyboard down to the right height.

## Monitor and Chair

Set your screen and seat to keep your neck and back comfortable.

### How high should my monitor be?

OSHA says the top line of the screen should be at or slightly below eye level. Put the monitor directly in front of you, at least 20 inches away. OSHA says the preferred viewing distance is generally 20 to 40 inches from your eyes to the screen.

### What if I wear bifocals?

OSHA suggests lowering the monitor below the usual recommendation for bifocal wearers. That way you can read through the lower part of your lenses without tilting your head back.

### How should my chair be set up?

OSHA says to adjust your chair so your feet and back are well supported. The seat should let your feet rest flat on the floor or a footrest. If you can't lower the seat because it would put your keyboard or monitor too high, use a footrest.

## Sitting, Standing and Moving

How to sit less during the workday.

### Do standing desks reduce sitting?

Yes, at least in the short term. A 2018 Cochrane review of 34 studies with 3,397 people found that sit-stand desks cut sitting at work by about 100 minutes a day for up to 3 months, compared with regular desks. The effect was smaller, about 57 minutes a day, at 3 to 12 months. The evidence was rated low quality.

### Is standing all day better than sitting all day?

Not necessarily. OSHA's point that no single posture fits everyone applies here too. The aim is to move between positions rather than swap one long static posture for another. If you use a standing desk, readjust your keyboard and monitor heights each time you switch.

### Do treadmill desks or walking pads help?

The Cochrane review found that active workstations, such as treadmill and cycling desks, had unclear or inconsistent effects on sitting time. Some people like them for calls or reading. Our [walking pad vs treadmill](/fitness/walking-pad-vs-treadmill/) comparison covers what treadmill-desk research shows.

### What else helps reduce sitting at work?

The Cochrane review also looked at policies, information and counseling, with mixed results. Simple changes such as regular breaks to stand or walk are easy to try.
