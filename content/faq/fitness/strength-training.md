Strength training is any exercise that makes your muscles work against resistance: weights, bands, machines or your own body weight. US guidelines call for it at least twice a week, yet it gets far less attention than cardio.

These answers draw on the US Physical Activity Guidelines, the CDC and the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM). They cover how often to train, how many sets and reps to do and how to keep progressing.

## Getting Started

How much you need and how to begin.

### How often should I strength train?

The CDC says adults need muscle-strengthening activity on at least 2 days a week, working all the major muscle groups. Those are your legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms. The ACSM suggests 2 to 3 days a week for beginners, 3 to 4 days for intermediate lifters and 4 to 5 days for advanced lifters.

### What counts as strength training?

Anything that makes your muscles work harder than usual against resistance. The US guidelines say resistance can come from weights, elastic bands or your own body weight, as in push-ups. Weight machines count too.

### How long should a strength workout be?

There's no set time. The US guidelines don't give a minimum number of minutes for muscle strengthening. What matters is working each major muscle group hard enough that another repetition would be difficult.

## Sets, Reps and Rest

How to structure each session.

### How many sets and reps should I do?

The US guidelines say one set of 8 to 12 repetitions of each exercise builds strength, though 2 or 3 sets may work better. Each set should take you close to the point where another rep would be hard. The ACSM recommends beginners use loads they can lift 8 to 12 times.

### What's the difference between training for strength and for muscle size?

The ACSM recommends a mix of loads for both, but the emphasis differs. For maximum strength, experienced lifters eventually focus on heavy loads they can lift 1 to 6 times. For muscle size, the ACSM emphasizes loads you can lift 6 to 12 times. Our [Pilates vs strength training](/fitness/pilates-vs-strength-training/) comparison explains where other methods fit.

### How long should I rest between sets?

It depends on your goal. For heavy strength work, the ACSM recommends 3 to 5 minutes between sets so you can keep lifting heavy. For muscle-size training at moderate loads, it suggests 1 to 2 minutes.

## Progress and Recovery

How to keep improving without overdoing it.

### What is progressive overload?

It means gradually making your training harder so your muscles keep adapting. The US guidelines say strength improves when you increase the weight, sets, reps or days you train. The ACSM suggests adding 2% to 10% to the load once you can do 1 or 2 reps more than your target.

### Do I need rest days between strength workouts?

The ACSM's frequency advice builds in recovery. Beginners train 2 to 3 days a week, which leaves days off between sessions. Lifters who train more often typically spread different muscle groups across the week, so the same muscles aren't worked hard every day.

### What is a one-rep max, and do I need to test it?

A one-rep max (1RM) is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. You don't need to test it to make progress. You can estimate it from a lighter set with the [One Rep Max Calculator](/one-rep-max-calculator/), then use it to plan your training loads.

### Can I build strength without weights?

Yes. The US guidelines count body-weight exercises and elastic bands as muscle-strengthening activity. The key, they say, is working to the point where another repetition would be difficult, and making it gradually harder over time.
