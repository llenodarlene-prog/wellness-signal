# Reformer Pilates vs Mat Pilates: 8 Differences in Resistance, Cost and Results

Reformer Pilates and mat Pilates use the same method. What changes is how resistance and support reach your body. On a mat, you work against your own weight and gravity. A reformer adds a sliding carriage, springs, straps and a footbar.

That doesn't make the reformer harder or better by default. Springs can add resistance, but they can also help you through a move.

So the reformer Pilates vs mat Pilates choice depends on your goals, budget and access. Here's how they compare.

## Reformer Pilates vs Mat Pilates at a Glance

| Factor | Mat Pilates | Reformer Pilates |
|---|---|---|
| Resistance | Body weight, gravity, position | Springs plus body weight |
| Equipment | A mat, optional small props | A reformer machine |
| Portability | Excellent | Low |
| Home use | Easy | Needs a machine and floor space |
| Class size | Usually larger | Limited by machines |
| Ways to assist a move | Fewer | More |
| Ways to adjust resistance | Limited | Broad |
| Typical cost | Lower | Higher |
| Proven better overall | No | No |

## 1. Springs Can Resist or Assist

A reformer's springs and carriage change how a move feels. Depending on the exercise, the springs may make it harder by adding resistance or easier by supporting your weight.

That's why reformer classes can suit very different ability levels. An instructor can change the springs, your position or your range of motion while keeping the same Pilates principle. ACE Fitness describes the reformer as offering easier progressions, while mat work is more portable and cost-effective.

## 2. Mat Pilates Isn't the Beginner Version

Mat work can be surprisingly hard. With no springs or straps to help, you have to control your whole body against gravity. A simple-looking move gets tough when your position changes or you have to stay controlled through a bigger range.

That's why reformer experience doesn't make mat classes easy.

## 3. Head-to-Head Trials Show Both Work

A handful of trials compare the two directly, but they're small and mostly involve specific health conditions.

- **Multiple sclerosis:** in a randomized study of 38 patients, both mat and reformer groups improved balance, mobility, core stability, fatigue and quality of life over 8 weeks. The reformer group gained more trunk flexor strength. Other gains were similar.
- **Fibromyalgia:** a 2023 trial of 28 women compared supervised reformer classes with home mat sessions for 6 weeks. Both groups improved on different measures, with no significant difference between groups. The different settings make it hard to credit the equipment alone.
- **Dysmenorrhea and soccer:** more recent trials compared the two in women with painful periods and in soccer players. The dysmenorrhea trial had 45 women, with 15 in each group.

Together these suggest both formats work. None shows that one is better for healthy adults in general.

## 4. The Reformer Makes Progression Easier

Changing spring settings gives the reformer a clear way to add or reduce load. That can make progressive training simpler than on a mat.

Mat Pilates still builds strength, especially for beginners or when the instructor progresses your position well. Its load ceiling is just different from a machine with adjustable resistance. Neither replaces heavy strength training if maximum strength is your goal. Our [Pilates vs strength training](/fitness/pilates-vs-strength-training/) comparison covers that tradeoff.

## 5. Beginners Can Start With Either

A reformer can make some moves easier to learn, because the straps, springs and carriage give support and feedback. It also means learning how to set up and use the machine.

A mat class has less setup but fewer ways to assist you. A skilled instructor matters more than the equipment.

## 6. Both Can Train Mobility

The reformer lets you explore a range of motion with spring support. Mat work builds control through body-weight positions that need no equipment.

If pain, a health condition or a recent injury limits how you move, the right modification matters more than the format.

## 7. Reformer Classes Usually Cost More

The economics are simple. A mat studio can fit many people in one room with cheap equipment. A reformer studio needs a large machine for each person, more floor space and a cap on class size.

That usually pushes reformer prices higher, though the gap varies by city and membership. Use the [Pilates Class Cost Calculator](/pilates-class-cost-calculator/) to compare packs and memberships against how often you'll actually go.

## 8. Mat Pilates Wins at Home

A mat takes little space, needs almost no setup and travels easily. Home reformers exist, but they take real floor space, need upkeep and cost a lot more upfront. ACE notes reformers typically cost thousands of dollars.

If consistency matters more to you than equipment variety, the mat may be the smarter long-term choice. App-based classes can help, and our [Pilates apps](/fitness/pilates-apps/) comparison covers mat and reformer options.

### Does Reformer Pilates Burn More Calories?

There isn't enough evidence to say. Energy use depends on the exercises, spring tension, your body size, the pace and rest breaks. A fast reformer class can be demanding, but so can a hard mat session. Calories are a poor way to choose between them.

## Which Should You Choose?

| If you want... | Choose |
|---|---|
| Adjustable resistance and support | Reformer |
| Easier ways to progress or regress moves | Reformer |
| The lowest cost | Mat |
| A workout you can do anywhere | Mat |

You don't have to pick one forever. Mat work teaches control with minimal equipment, and reformer work adds more ways to resist, assist and give feedback. ## FAQs

### Is Reformer Pilates More Effective Than Mat Pilates?

Current evidence doesn't show that one is better across the board. Small head-to-head trials find both formats improve meaningful outcomes, with differences that depend on the group and the measure. The reformer's springs add more options, but that doesn't guarantee better results for every goal.

### Is Mat Pilates Harder Than Reformer Pilates?

It can be. Some reformer moves use springs to help you, while mat moves make you control your full body weight without help. How hard either feels depends on the exercise, your position, the spring setting, the pace and your experience.

### How Many Reformer Classes Should a Beginner Take?

There's no set number. A beginner needs enough classes to learn the setup, breathing, control and common moves safely. How often you go should fit around your other activity and recovery, not a studio's sales schedule.

### Can Mat Pilates Build the Same Strength as Reformer Pilates?

They overlap, but they don't offer the same loading options. Mat work builds strength and control, especially for newer students. The reformer gives an instructor more ways to change resistance, which can make steady progression easier for some exercises.
