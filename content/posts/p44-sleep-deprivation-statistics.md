# Sleep Deprivation Statistics: 2024 US Data on Short Sleep and Sleep Problems

Nearly 1 in 3 US adults reported sleeping less than 7 hours in 2024. That's an important number, but it isn't the same as the rate of insomnia, chronic sleep deprivation or a diagnosed sleep disorder. Each measures something different.

The CDC published its analysis of the 2024 National Health Interview Survey in April 2026. It separates four measures: short sleep, trouble falling asleep, trouble staying asleep and waking up rested. That makes it a strong source for sleep deprivation statistics, as long as you keep the measures apart.

## US Sleep Statistics at a Glance

| Measure, adults 18 and older | 2024 |
|---|---|
| Sleep less than 7 hours | 30.5% |
| Trouble falling asleep most days or every day | 15.4% |
| Trouble staying asleep most days or every day | 18.1% |
| Wake up well-rested most days or every day | 54.8% |

These come from survey questions, and they overlap. Many people have more than one problem, so the percentages can't be added together.

## Women Report More Sleep Problems, Not Less Sleep

Men and women were almost identical on short sleep, at 30.6% and 30.4%. The difference showed up in sleep quality.

| Measure | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep less than 7 hours | 30.6% | 30.4% |
| Trouble falling asleep | 12.2% | 18.5% |
| Trouble staying asleep | 14.6% | 21.4% |
| Wake up well-rested | 58.2% | 51.7% |

A story about sleep based only on hours would miss much of what women report. For how short sleep varies by age, see our [average sleep hours by age](/mind-sleep/average-sleep-hours-by-age/) guide.

## Sleep Problems Change With Age

The two kinds of sleep trouble move in opposite directions as people get older.

| Age | Trouble falling asleep | Trouble staying asleep |
|---|---|---|
| 18 to 34 | 18.3% | 12.7% |
| 35 to 49 | 15.4% | 16.9% |
| 50 to 64 | 14.5% | 22.3% |
| 65 and older | 12.8% | 21.7% |

Young adults had the most trouble falling asleep. Adults 50 and older had the most trouble staying asleep. That's a useful reminder that "sleep problems" isn't one thing.

## Differences by Race and Ethnicity

| Group | Sleep less than 7 hours |
|---|---|
| Black adults | 40.2% |
| Hispanic adults | 29.0% |
| White adults | 28.9% |
| Asian adults | 27.9% |

Black adults were the most likely to report short sleep. White adults were the most likely to report trouble staying asleep, at 20.7%, compared with 9.2% of Asian adults. These are survey descriptions, not evidence that race itself causes sleep problems. Work conditions, health care access, neighborhoods and many other factors may play a part, and this data doesn't show which ones.

## Short Sleep Isn't the Same as Insomnia

Short sleep describes hours. Insomnia means ongoing trouble falling or staying asleep, usually with daytime effects, and diagnosing it involves how long and how often it happens. Someone who chooses a short schedule may not have insomnia. Someone with insomnia may spend enough time in bed and still feel unrefreshed.

## Reading Sleep Statistics Carefully

- **Check the survey year.** This report was published in 2026 but uses 2024 interviews.
- **Check the question.** Changes in wording can create trends that aren't real changes in behavior.
- **Don't assume cause.** A one-time survey can't prove short sleep caused a health problem.

Small differences between groups may not be statistically meaningful, and the CDC's methods account for its survey design.

## FAQs

### What Percentage of US Adults Are Sleep Deprived?

The CDC found that 30.5% of US adults reported sleeping less than 7 hours on average in 2024. That measures short sleep, not a clinical diagnosis. Other definitions, such as trouble falling or staying asleep, give different figures and should be reported separately.

### Which Age Group Is Most Sleep Deprived?

Adults aged 50 to 64 were the most likely to sleep less than 7 hours, at 34.5%. Young adults aged 18 to 34 had the most trouble falling asleep, at 18.3%. Which group looks worst depends on the measure you use.

### Are Women More Sleep Deprived Than Men?

Not on hours. In 2024, 30.4% of women and 30.6% of men slept less than 7 hours. Women were more likely to report trouble falling asleep and staying asleep, and less likely to wake up feeling rested.

### Does Poor Sleep Always Mean Insomnia?

No. Poor sleep can come from work schedules, your environment, illness and many other things. Insomnia is diagnosed against specific criteria by a clinician, not from an occasional bad night or a single sleep tracker score.
