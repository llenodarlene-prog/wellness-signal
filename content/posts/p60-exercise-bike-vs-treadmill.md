# Exercise Bike vs Treadmill: Calories, Knees and Training Compared

The exercise bike vs treadmill question has no single winner. A bike lets you train seated with no foot impact. A treadmill trains walking and running, the movements you use every day and in races. Both build cardio fitness when you work hard enough, so the better choice depends on your goal, your joints and which one you'll actually use.

**Quick answer:** Pick a treadmill if you want to walk more, run or train for a running race. Pick an exercise bike if you want low-impact cardio, find balance on a moving belt hard, or want to cycle outdoors.

## Exercise Bike vs Treadmill at a Glance

| Factor | Exercise bike | Treadmill |
|---|---|---|
| Body position | Seated | Upright, walking or running |
| What makes it harder | Resistance and cadence | Speed and incline |
| Impact | No foot landing | Low when walking; landing impact when running |
| Running preparation | Limited | Specific |
| Cycling preparation | Specific | Limited |
| Balance demand | Lower | Higher, especially when running |
| Home checks | Seat fit, adjustability, footprint | Belt size, noise, clearance, safety stop |

## Does the Treadmill Burn More Calories?

In one lab study, yes. A 2024 study in *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* compared seven indoor machines in 30 recreationally active men, average age about 42, at a hard self-selected effort and at maximum effort. The treadmill produced the highest energy expenditure and oxygen use. The spin, upright and recumbent bikes all came lower, with the recumbent bike lowest.

The chart title reads RPE 20 while the caption says RPE 17. Captured November 2026.")

Don't turn that into a fixed ratio. A hard cycling interval can burn more than a relaxed treadmill walk. And a bike may let someone keep going longer when walking is uncomfortable. Track how long you can sustain a steady, repeatable effort, not just the console's calorie number.

## Choose the Type of Bike First

"Exercise bike" covers several machines:

- **Upright bike:** a regular cycling position.
- **Recumbent bike:** a reclined seat with back support and pedals out in front, easier to get on and off.
- **Indoor cycling or spin bike:** built for higher-cadence and standing efforts.

Their fit, comfort and resistance differ, and in the 2024 study they didn't produce the same energy expenditure. Someone with balance concerns may prefer a recumbent. Someone training for road cycling may want a spin bike with cadence or power data.

## What About Your Knees?

A bike removes the repeated foot landings of running and supports your body weight, which some people find easier when returning to exercise. But knees can still get irritated if the seat is set wrong, resistance is too high or pedaling itself causes pain. A seat that's too low makes your knee bend more on every stroke.

Treadmill walking is another low-impact option, since walking has no flight phase. If pain gets worse, or your knee swells or feels unstable, see a clinician or physical therapist.

## Which Builds Better Cardio Fitness?

Both do, if you work hard enough and stay consistent. The CDC recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle strengthening on 2 days. The CDC's intensity examples list bicycling slower than 10 mph as moderate and faster than 10 mph as vigorous, and brisk walking at 2.5 mph or faster as moderate.

If your goal is a 5K, the treadmill trains your pace and running form. If you want to ride farther outdoors, the bike trains your pedaling. General fitness overlaps, but sport-specific fitness doesn't fully transfer.

## Buying One for Home

For a bike, check seat and handlebar adjustment, maximum user weight, resistance type and warranty. For a treadmill, check belt length, motor, top speed and incline, emergency stop and space behind the deck. Noise varies by model, so don't assume a bike is always silent or a treadmill always loud.

Before buying, answer these: What's my main goal? Which movement can I repeat without pain? How long can I realistically train? Will others in the house use it? If both fit, choose the one you'll use more.

## FAQs

### Is 20 Minutes of Cycling Better Than 20 Minutes on a Treadmill?

There's no general winner. A hard cycling session can be more work than an easy walk, and a brisk uphill walk can be more work than gentle pedaling. Time alone doesn't settle it.

### Is a Stationary Bike Enough Exercise?

It can count toward weekly aerobic activity if the intensity is high enough. The CDC also recommends muscle strengthening on 2 days a week, which a bike doesn't replace.

### Is an Exercise Bike Better Than a Treadmill for Bad Knees?

It may feel better because there's no foot impact. Set the seat height correctly and start with low resistance. Treadmill walking is another low-impact choice. A clinician can advise if pain persists.

### Which Is Better for Beginners?

A bike makes it easy to control intensity without balancing on a moving belt. A slow treadmill walk is also approachable. Comfort, access and safety features should decide.
