# 9 Fitness Apps for Women Compared by Training Style and Cost

Women's fitness apps cover a much wider market than programmes designed exclusively for women. Some platforms focus on structured strength training or Pilates, while others offer broad libraries of yoga, HIIT, cardio, and home workouts that can suit any user.

The best choice among fitness apps for women depends on the kind of training someone will actually follow. A person looking for a progressive strength programme needs different features from someone who wants short home workouts, Pilates, or a large on-demand class library.

The category is commercially significant. Grand View Research estimates the global women's health app market at **$5.3 billion in 2025**, with North America representing 38% of revenue. Its broader category includes fitness and nutrition, menstrual health, pregnancy, menopause, and other health applications, so the figure should not be treated as the size of women's fitness apps alone.

## Key Takeaways

- Sweat and EvolveYou are explicitly designed for women, while Alive also centres female trainers and programmes.
- Sweat currently lists more than **60 programmes**, more than 13,000 workouts, and published US pricing of **$24.99 monthly or $134.99 annually**.
- EvolveYou publishes US pricing of **$22.99 monthly or $119.99 annually** and offers more than 1,500 workouts and 20 programmes on its annual plan.
- Alive costs **$14.99 monthly or $119.99 annually** after a seven-day trial.
- Ladder is built around progressive strength programming and costs **$29.99 monthly or $179.99 annually**.
- Nike Training Club remains free and provides more than 185 free workouts across strength, yoga, mobility, and cardio.
- Apple Fitness+ costs **$9.99 monthly or $79.99 annually** in the US, while Peloton App One costs **$12.99 monthly**.
- App pricing does not indicate programme quality. Training style, progression, coaching, equipment requirements, and adherence matter more than the number of workouts in a library.

## How We Compared Fitness Apps for Women

We reviewed current official app pages, pricing pages, membership terms, programme descriptions, and support documentation. The comparison focuses on workout structure, coaching, training style, equipment needs, pricing, and whether the platform provides a free option or trial.

Not every app in this article is marketed only to women. We included several general fitness platforms because women may prefer them for strength, Pilates, yoga, or home training, but we do not assume that women need fundamentally different exercise methods.

## 1. Sweat

Sweat is one of the most established fitness apps built specifically for women. The company says the platform has more than **60 programmes** and more than 13,000 workouts.

Training styles include strength, HIIT, Pilates, yoga, barre, low-impact workouts, pregnancy, and post-pregnancy programmes. This makes Sweat suitable for users who want a broad training library rather than one narrowly defined method.

### Sweat Pricing and Programme Structure

Sweat currently publishes standard US pricing of **$24.99 per month** or **$134.99 per year**, with a seven-day free trial on eligible signups. Its annual pricing works out to approximately $11.25 per month before taxes.

The platform is programme-led rather than simply a video library. Users can select structured training plans and access nutrition, recovery, and wellness content alongside workouts.

### Best Fit

Sweat is strongest for women who want a large choice of structured programmes across several stages of life. It may be less suitable for someone who only wants progressive barbell training or a very low-cost fitness app.

## 2. EvolveYou

EvolveYou describes itself as a strength training and wellbeing app made by women for women. It was co-founded in 2019 by trainer Krissy Cela and offers training at home, in the gym, or with limited equipment.

The platform currently advertises more than **1,500 workouts**, 20+ structured programmes on both monthly and annual plans, and about 1,000 recipes. Training includes conventional strength work, Pilates, hybrid programmes, and structured progression.

### EvolveYou Pricing

Published US pricing is **$22.99 per month** or **$119.99 per year**. The annual plan currently includes a seven-day trial, while the monthly plan does not always carry the same trial terms.

EvolveYou says its structured plans use weekly blocks and periodised progression. That design may appeal to users who want more direction than an open workout library provides.

### Best Fit

EvolveYou is a strong option for women who want progressive strength training combined with Pilates or wellness content. Both billing options unlock the full programme library, although the free trial applies to the annual plan.

## 3. Alive by Whitney Simmons

Alive is a female-led training app associated with Whitney Simmons and other women coaches. Its programming includes strength, hypertrophy, Pilates, endurance, pregnancy and postpartum, and beginner-to-advanced training.

The company currently advertises more than **10 programmes**, 100+ daily workouts, video demonstrations, weight and rep tracking, progress photos, and Apple Watch support.

### Alive Pricing

Alive costs **$14.99 per month** or **$119.99 per year** after a seven-day free trial. The annual plan therefore costs less per month than the month-to-month option.

The platform also includes journaling and wellness features, which separates it from apps focused only on training metrics.

### Best Fit

Alive suits users who prefer female coaches and structured strength-oriented programming. It also offers Pilates and pregnancy-related options, giving it broader appeal than a conventional bodybuilding app.

## 4. Ladder

Ladder is built around progressive strength training rather than a large on-demand class catalogue. Members join coaching teams and receive new programmed workouts each week.

The app includes in-ear coaching, video demonstrations, weight and rep tracking, team chat, and integration with Apple Music and Spotify. Ladder also offers Pilates-strength hybrid teams alongside more conventional lifting programmes.

### Ladder Pricing

Ladder Pro costs **$29.99 per month** or **$179.99 annually**, billed upfront and advertised as $14.99 per month (about $15.00 when divided by 12). New users can try all training teams for seven days without providing payment information until the trial ends.

This makes Ladder one of the more expensive apps in the comparison, but its model is closer to ongoing programming and coaching than a simple workout library.

### Best Fit

Ladder is strongest for users who already intend to train consistently and want a progressive plan. It is less appropriate for people who mainly want occasional yoga, meditation, or short casual workouts.

## 5. Peloton App

Peloton's app provides fitness programming without requiring Peloton equipment. App One includes unlimited strength, yoga, meditation, outdoor running, gym, Pilates, HIIT, and related floor workouts, plus limited cardio-equipment classes.

Peloton currently advertises more than 15 workout types and more than 55 instructors. Users can follow live or on-demand sessions and use scheduling and progress-tracking features.

### Peloton App Pricing

Peloton App One costs **$12.99 per month**, while App+ costs **$28.99 per month** for broader access to cardio-equipment classes. A 30-day trial is currently available to eligible new members.

For people who do not own a Peloton Bike, Tread, or Row, App One is the more relevant comparison with general fitness subscriptions.

### Best Fit

Peloton works well for people who enjoy instructor-led classes and variety. It is less specialised than EvolveYou or Ladder for progressive strength programming but offers a much broader mix of class formats.

## 6. Apple Fitness+

Apple Fitness+ is a general fitness subscription built into Apple's ecosystem. It offers 12 workout types, such as Strength, HIIT, Yoga, Pilates, Cycling and Dance, plus Meditation.

Sessions run from five to 45 minutes, and users can access custom plans and personalised recommendations. Supported devices can also display real-time heart-rate and activity metrics.

### Apple Fitness+ Pricing

In the United States, Fitness+ costs **$9.99 per month** or **$79.99 annually** after the standard free trial. It can be shared with up to five family members.

That makes it one of the lower-cost paid options in this comparison, particularly for households already using Apple devices.

### Best Fit

Apple Fitness+ is suitable for people who want guided classes across many formats without paying for a specialised coaching platform. It is less appropriate for users seeking detailed lifting progression or women-specific programming.

## 7. Nike Training Club

Nike Training Club provides guided workouts, training programmes, and wellness content without a paid subscription. Nike states that the app includes more than **185 free workouts** across strength, endurance, yoga, mobility, HIIT, and bodyweight training.

Workout duration ranges from approximately five to 50 minutes. Some sessions require no equipment, while others are designed for gym environments.

### Why the Free Model Is Notable

Nike removed the Premium label from its workout content and made those programmes free to Nike members. That makes it one of the most substantial [free fitness apps](/fitness/best-free-fitness-apps/) among mainstream options.

Users do not receive the same community and coach-team structure offered by paid apps such as Ladder or EvolveYou.

### Best Fit

Nike Training Club is a practical starting point for people who want a variety of free workouts. It is particularly useful for beginners who are not yet ready to pay for a specialist programme.

## 8. FitOn

FitOn offers free workouts covering strength, cardio, Pilates, yoga, barre, dance, and meditation. The company continues to describe core workouts as free while selling a paid PRO subscription for additional features.

PRO adds structured fitness and nutrition programmes, premium music, meal plans, more than 500 recipes, offline downloads, and supported device integrations.

### Pricing Limitation

FitOn's official support pages describe PRO features but do not consistently publish one universal current price because app-store and promotional pricing can vary. Users should confirm the amount presented in their account before subscribing.

This is an example of why comparing apps by advertised price alone can be misleading. App-store billing, promotions, and location can change the final cost.

### Best Fit

FitOn works well for users who want a large free class library. People who need downloadable workouts, advanced nutrition content, or structured premium programmes may prefer PRO.

## 9. Centr

Centr combines fitness programming with nutrition and wellness content. Its current app includes strength, HIIT, Pilates, yoga, HYROX, boxing, MMA, meal plans, progress tracking, and personalised training.

The company provides monthly, quarterly, and annual memberships, all with the same core feature access. Centr directs users to its signup flow for current pricing rather than presenting one fixed price in its support documentation.

### Trial and Programme Access

New members currently receive a **seven-day free trial** with access to the paid membership experience. Programmes commonly run six to 12 weeks, which may suit users looking for structured training rather than isolated classes.

### Best Fit

Centr suits users who want workouts and nutrition within one platform. Because pricing is shown dynamically at signup, prospective members should verify the current offer before comparing it with fixed-price competitors.

## Market Data: Why Women's Fitness Apps Are Growing

Grand View Research estimates that the broader global women's health app market was worth **$5.3 billion in 2025** and could reach $23.4 billion by 2033. Fitness and nutrition is one part of that larger category.

The traditional fitness market is also expanding. The Health & Fitness Association reported **81 million [US fitness-facility members](/fitness/fitness-statistics-by-activity-and-age/) in 2025**, while more than 100 million Americans used a fitness facility when flexible and non-member access was included.

Digital fitness and physical facilities are therefore not mutually exclusive. Many consumers can use apps to supplement gym or studio training rather than replace it.

## How to Choose a Fitness App

The best app depends on how someone prefers to train. Before subscribing, compare:

1. Training style and exercise goals.
2. Structured programmes versus on-demand classes.
3. Home versus gym equipment requirements.
4. Strength progression and workout tracking.
5. Instructor-led video versus written programming.
6. Pregnancy, postpartum, or life-stage programming if relevant.
7. Free trial and cancellation terms.
8. Monthly and annual pricing.
9. Device and wearable support.
10. Whether nutrition features are useful or unnecessary.

A large workout library can look impressive but still be difficult to use consistently. A smaller programme that matches someone's schedule and goals may be more valuable.

## FAQs

### What Is the Best Fitness App for Women?

There is no single best app for every woman. Sweat and EvolveYou are specifically designed for women, Ladder is stronger for progressive strength programming, and Nike Training Club is a strong free option. The best choice depends on preferred training style, equipment, and budget.

### Which Fitness Apps for Women Are Free?

Nike Training Club provides a substantial free workout library, while FitOn keeps its core workouts free and charges for optional PRO features. Other apps in this comparison generally require paid subscriptions after introductory trials.

### Is Sweat Better Than EvolveYou?

Sweat offers a broader mix of programmes across strength, HIIT, Pilates, yoga, pregnancy, and postpartum. EvolveYou places more emphasis on structured strength and progressive training. Pricing is similar annually, so training style is the more important distinction.

### What Fitness App Is Best for Strength Training?

Ladder and EvolveYou are particularly focused on progressive strength programming. Alive also offers strength and hypertrophy programmes, while Peloton, Nike Training Club, and Apple Fitness+ provide strength classes within broader workout libraries.

### Are Women's Fitness Apps Different From General Fitness Apps?

Some are. Sweat and EvolveYou explicitly design programming and community around women, while apps such as Peloton or Apple Fitness+ are designed for general audiences. Exercise principles remain broadly applicable regardless of gender, although programme selection and life-stage considerations can differ.

### Are Fitness Apps Effective?

Fitness apps can help deliver structured workouts and support adherence, but effectiveness depends on whether users follow an appropriate programme consistently. App availability alone does not guarantee improvements in strength, fitness, body composition, or health outcomes.
