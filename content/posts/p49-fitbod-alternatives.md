# Fitbod Alternatives: 4 Strength Apps Compared by Price and Free Tier

Choosing between Fitbod alternatives starts with which part of Fitbod you actually use. Fitbod builds each workout for you from your equipment, goals and training history. Some people want that. Others already follow a program and just need a fast place to log sets, reps and weight. Those are two different jobs, and the apps below split along that line.

We compared four popular options using each publisher's own pricing and feature pages, checked in November 2026. We didn't run a hands-on test, and none of these apps paid to be included.

## Fitbod Alternatives at a Glance

| App | Best for | Free tier | Paid plan (US) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fitbod | Workouts generated for you | Trial only | $15.99/month or $95.99/year |
| Hevy | Logging with optional guided plans | Unlimited workouts, 4 routines | $2.99/month or $23.99/year |
| Strong | Fast logging of a set routine | Unlimited workouts, 3 routines | $4.99/month or $29.99/year |
| Boostcamp | Following a proven named program | 14,000+ free programs | Optional Pro |
| Alpha Progression | Planned progression and deloads | Unlimited logging, your own plans | Pro, with a 14-day trial on the yearly plan |

## What You're Replacing in Fitbod

Fitbod's help center lists two US plans: $15.99 a month or $95.99 a year, which works out to about $8 a month. For that, the app picks exercises, sets and weights for each session.

A cheaper logger can save money, but only if you don't need the app to plan for you. If you do, look for an alternative's guided or "generator" feature, which is usually the paid part.

## 1. Hevy: The Low-Cost All-Rounder

Hevy's free plan logs unlimited workouts. Its 2026 feature guide lists the limits: up to 4 routines, 7 custom exercises and 3 months of workout history and stats.

Hevy Pro costs $2.99 a month or $23.99 a year. It removes those limits and adds Hevy Trainer, which builds a personal program, suggests weights and reps, and gives progressive overload recommendations. That makes Pro the closest like-for-like swap for Fitbod at a quarter of the yearly price.

**Choose Hevy if** you want free logging now and the option of guided plans later.

![Hevy App Store artwork reading Log Workouts, Get Stronger, Stay Motivated](shot-hevy-app-store "Screenshot: Hevy listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

## 2. Strong: The Simplest Logger

Strong is built for people who already know their routine. Its App Store listing says the free version saves unlimited workouts but limits you to 3 custom routines. Strong PRO costs $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year and unlocks unlimited routines.

Strong doesn't write your program. It does make it quick to see what you lifted last time, and it works on Apple Watch.

**Choose Strong if** you run a set plan, such as a 5x5 barbell routine, and want minimal clutter.

![Strong app workout screen logging bench press and squat sets](shot-strong-app-store "Screenshot: Strong listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

## 3. Boostcamp: Follow a Program Instead of a Generator

Boostcamp takes a different approach from Fitbod. Instead of composing a fresh workout each day, it gives you a structured program to follow week by week. Its website lists more than 14,000 free programs, including plans from coaches such as Dr. Eric Helms. You can also build your own.

**Choose Boostcamp if** you'd rather run a proven plan than get a new session every visit.

![Boostcamp app screen listing science-based programs by named strength coaches](shot-boostcamp-app-store "Screenshot: Boostcamp listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

## 4. Alpha Progression: Best for Planned Progression

Alpha Progression's free version is generous. Its App Store listing includes unlimited logging, 795 exercise videos, your own plans, records and CSV export, with no ads and no account required.

Pro adds the parts that overlap most with Fitbod: a plan and workout generator, weight and rep recommendations for every set, periodization and deloads. The yearly plan has a 14-day free trial.

**Choose Alpha Progression if** you care about planned increases in load and volume more than daily variety.

![Alpha Progression app push workout with bench press and shoulder press sets](shot-alpha-progression-app-store "Screenshot: Alpha Progression listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

## Three-Year Cost: Fitbod vs Hevy

At list prices, assuming no change, three years of Fitbod's annual plan costs $287.97 ($95.99 × 3). Three years of Hevy Pro costs $71.97 ($23.99 × 3). That's a difference of $216.

The saving is real, but it isn't proof of equal value. Hevy Trainer and Fitbod's generator work differently, so try a full training week in the new app before you cancel.

## How to Switch Without Losing Your Data

- **Export first.** Check whether your current app offers a CSV or data export before you cancel.
- **Check custom exercises.** Renamed or custom lifts may not map cleanly to a new app's library.
- **Watch the units.** Some apps log dumbbell weight per hand and others log the combined total, which can make old and new numbers look different.
- **Test one full week.** Make sure the free tier covers your whole split before you rely on it.

## FAQs

### Is There a Free Alternative to Fitbod?

Yes. Hevy, Strong and Alpha Progression all log unlimited workouts for free, and Boostcamp offers more than 14,000 free programs. The catch is that free plans log or follow workouts. Fitbod-style automatic workout generation is usually a paid feature.

### Is Hevy Better Than Fitbod?

It depends on the job. Hevy is far cheaper at $23.99 a year for Pro versus Fitbod's $95.99, and its free tier is strong for logging. Fitbod is built around generating each session, while Hevy's guided plans come through the paid Hevy Trainer feature.

### Which Fitbod Alternative Is Best for Progressive Overload?

Alpha Progression Pro and Hevy Trainer both recommend weights and reps based on your past sessions. Alpha Progression also plans periodization and deloads. Strong and the free tiers let you see past loads, but you decide the next increase yourself.

### Should Beginners Use a Workout Generator?

A generator can make early decisions easier. A simple, repeatable beginner program, like many free ones on Boostcamp, also works well. What matters most is consistent training, good technique and gradual progress.
