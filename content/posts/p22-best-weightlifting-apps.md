# 8 Best Weightlifting Apps Compared for Logging, Progression and 1RM Tracking

The best weightlifting apps remember more than exercise names. After a few months of training, what matters is the record of progress. How many sets did you finish? What weight did you use? How close to failure was the last set? And what does the program expect next time?

That's why this list is narrower than a general workout app roundup. We focused on apps built for repeatable strength training, not video classes. We checked routine limits, progression tools, estimated one-rep max (1RM), effort tracking, program libraries, watch support and long-term cost.

If you're still choosing between apps that plan sessions for you, our [workout planner apps](/fitness/workout-planner-apps/) comparison covers that side. And for how lifting compares with other training, see [Pilates vs strength training](/fitness/pilates-vs-strength-training/).

## What a Lifting App Needs That a Planner Doesn't

The two categories overlap, but the jobs differ. A planner helps you build a session. A lifting app has to keep enough detail to make progressive overload measurable.

So we gave most weight to these features:

- weight, reps and sets for every exercise
- effort ratings, RPE (rate of perceived exertion) or RIR (reps in reserve)
- estimated 1RM and personal records
- supersets, warm-ups, drop sets and failure sets
- rest timers and plate calculators
- progression suggestions
- program history and data export
- logging from a watch

We didn't score "ease of use," because that would imply hands-on testing we didn't do.

## 1. Hevy: Best All-Round Lifting Log With Social Features

![Hevy App Store artwork reading Log Workouts, Get Stronger, Stay Motivated](shot-hevy-app-store "Screenshot: Hevy listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Hevy records weight, reps, RPE and several set types. Your previous numbers stay visible during each workout, which makes it easy to beat last week. It also tracks personal records, volume and exercise progress, and it works on Apple Watch and Wear OS.

The free version is capable, but it caps saved routines, custom exercises and how much history you can see. Pro removes those limits and adds Hevy Trainer for generated programming.

**Best fit:** Lifters who want detailed logging and a social side without paying straight away.

**Pro price:** $2.99 a month, $23.99 a year or $74.99 lifetime.

## 2. Strong: Best for a Clean Manual Log

![Strong app workout screen logging bench press and squat sets](shot-strong-app-store "Screenshot: Strong listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Strong keeps the gym log simple. It handles reusable routines, custom exercises, supersets, warm-up and failure sets, rest timers, volume charts and estimated 1RM trends.

Free accounts save unlimited workouts but only three custom routines. PRO lifts that cap.

**Best fit:** Experienced lifters who know their program and want the app to record it, not rewrite it.

**PRO price:** $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year, with a lifetime option also listed.

## 3. Boostcamp: Best for Following Established Programs

![Boostcamp app screen listing science-based programs by named strength coaches](shot-boostcamp-app-store "Screenshot: Boostcamp listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Boostcamp pairs a lifting log with a very large library of strength and muscle-building programs. It supports RPE and RIR, estimated 1RM, rest timers, plate math, exercise swaps and program-specific progression.

The library is what sets it apart from blank-template loggers. You can follow known systems such as 5/3/1 variants or GZCLP without rebuilding every week by hand.

**Best fit:** Lifters who want proven programs and detailed logging in one place.

**Pro price:** $14.99 a month or $59.99 a year in standard App Store options.

## 4. JEFIT: Best for a Large Exercise Database

![JEFIT app logging dumbbell sets on iPhone and Apple Watch](shot-jefit-app-store "Screenshot: JEFIT listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

JEFIT combines a large exercise library with custom routines, set logging, notes, photos, supersets and detailed progress charts. It also offers adaptive planning and progressive overload suggestions.

Its analytics cover training volume, muscle focus and estimated 1RM. That makes it more data-heavy than a notebook-style app.

**Best fit:** Lifters who enjoy digging into training history and want most exercises already documented.

**Elite price:** $12.99 a month or $69.99 a year.

## 5. Alpha Progression: Best for Set-by-Set Overload Tips

![Alpha Progression app push workout with bench press and shoulder press sets](shot-alpha-progression-app-store "Screenshot: Alpha Progression listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Alpha Progression's free version supports unlimited logging and custom plans. Its standout feature is in Pro. The app suggests weight and reps for each set, based on past performance and the work you've already done that session.

It also tracks estimated 1RM, volume, body measurements and personal records.

**Best fit:** Muscle-building lifters who want progression suggestions inside a normal lifting log.

**Pro price:** $12.99 a month or $79.99 a year.

## 6. Caliber: Best Free Option for Unlimited Custom Workouts

![Caliber App Store artwork headlined Level up your workouts with science-based strength training](shot-caliber-app-store "Screenshot: Caliber listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Caliber's free tier allows unlimited custom workouts and hundreds of exercise demos. It adds workout groups and Apple Health integration.

Caliber Plus brings coach-designed plans and extra tracking. Premium Coaching is a separate one-to-one service. Plus currently appears in the App Store at $12 a month or $72 a year, with lower promotional offers at times.

**Best fit:** Lifters who want as many custom routines as they like, free.

## 7. StrongLifts: Best for Simple Barbell Progression

![StrongLifts app showing today’s 5x5 squat, bench press and barbell row workout](shot-stronglifts-app-store "Screenshot: StrongLifts listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

StrongLifts is more prescriptive than the others. Its core is a set barbell program with automatic weight increases, failure handling and deloads.

That simplicity helps beginners who don't want to design their own progression. It's restrictive if you run a bodybuilding split or a coach's program that looks different.

**Best fit:** New or returning barbell lifters who want clear progression with few decisions.

**Price:** $11.99 a month or $59.99 a year.

## 8. Liftin': Best for Apple Watch and Custom Progression Rules

![Liftin App Store artwork calling it the number one gym tracker with automatic progression](shot-liftin-app-store "Screenshot: Liftin' listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Liftin' is built around Apple devices. It supports custom programs, built-in templates, Apple Watch logging, Apple Health, Strava and CSV export. Progression rules include linear progression, double progression, deloads and 5/3/1-style training max cycles.

The free version allows five workouts a month. That's enough to try it, but not enough for most regular lifters to use for free long term.

**Best fit:** Apple users who want automatic progression rules and easy data export.

**Unlimited price:** $2.99 a month, $24.99 a year or $99.99 one-time.

## FAQs

### What Is the Best Free Weightlifting App?

Caliber has one of the least restrictive free models, because it allows unlimited custom workouts. Hevy, Boostcamp and Alpha Progression also offer strong free features, with paid tiers that remove limits or add programming.

### Which Weightlifting App Tracks RPE or RIR?

Hevy supports RPE, and Boostcamp supports both RPE and RIR. Alpha Progression also uses effort information in its progression model. Fields can change, so check the exercise log screen if one metric is essential to you.

### Which Lifting App Is Best for Progressive Overload?

StrongLifts gives the most explicit built-in progression for its own barbell program. Alpha Progression Pro offers set-level suggestions, and Boostcamp can automate progression in supported programs. Hevy Trainer and JEFIT also offer adaptive planning.

### Do These Apps Calculate One-Rep Max?

Yes. Strong, Boostcamp, JEFIT, Alpha Progression and Liftin' all document estimated 1RM or related strength tracking. Estimates are useful for spotting trends, but they aren't the same as a tested maximum.

### Which Lifting App Works Best With Apple Watch?

Hevy, Strong, StrongLifts and Liftin' all have Apple Watch apps. Liftin' is the most Apple-focused here, with iCloud sync, a Mac app and wider Apple ecosystem support.
