# How Many Steps per Day Do You Need? What the 7,000-Step Research Shows

The 10,000-step goal is easy to remember, but it isn't a medical rule. Newer research asks a better question: compared with very little walking, how many daily steps are linked to real health benefits?

A large 2025 review in *Lancet Public Health* found that 7,000 steps a day was a useful, realistic benchmark. The authors didn't say everyone must hit exactly 7,000, or that benefits stop there.

So how many steps per day should you aim for? Here's what the research shows and how to set a goal that fits you.

## What the 7,000-Step Review Found

The review covered 57 studies from 35 groups of people, with 31 studies pooled in its main analysis. It compared people walking 7,000 steps a day with those walking 2,000.

| Outcome | Risk at 7,000 vs 2,000 steps a day |
|---|---|
| Death from any cause | 47% lower |
| Death from heart disease | 47% lower |
| New heart disease | 25% lower |
| Death from cancer | 37% lower |
| Type 2 diabetes | 14% lower |
| Dementia | 38% lower |
| Depressive symptoms | 22% lower |
| Falls | 28% lower |

These are links found in observational studies. They aren't guaranteed results for any one person, and they don't prove that walking alone caused the difference. How certain the evidence was varied by outcome.

## Why the Comparison Matters

"47% lower risk of death" sounds dramatic until you notice the comparison is 2,000 steps a day, which is very little walking. It doesn't mean going from 6,000 to 7,000 cuts your risk in half.

For death from any cause, heart disease, dementia and falls, the benefits began to level off at around 5,000 to 7,000 steps. For other outcomes, such as type 2 diabetes and depressive symptoms, the link kept rising steadily. There's no single magic number that applies to every condition.

## Is 10,000 Steps Still Worth It?

If you enjoy walking and can keep it up, 10,000 steps is a fine personal goal. The bigger point is for people starting far below it. Walking 4,000 or 6,000 steps isn't worthless just because it falls short of 10,000. The research doesn't support an all-or-nothing view.

## What If You Can't Walk Much Every Day?

You don't have to hit the same number daily. A 2023 study in *JAMA Network Open* followed 3,101 US adults who wore step trackers for a week. Over 10 years, people who reached 8,000 steps on just 1 or 2 days a week had a 14.9 percentage point lower risk of death than those who never did. People who hit 8,000 steps on 3 to 7 days had a 16.5 point lower risk.

That's an observational link, not proof that weekend walking alone is enough. Still, it's encouraging if shift work, caregiving, pain or low energy make daily targets hard.

## Steps Aren't the Whole Picture

US guidelines call for at least 150 minutes of moderate or 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week, plus muscle strengthening on 2 or more days. Steps can count toward the aerobic part, but they don't show how hard you worked or capture strength training.

Swimming, cycling and lifting all help your health, and a step counter barely notices them. Our [StairMaster vs treadmill](/fitness/stairmaster-vs-treadmill/) guide explains why machine effort isn't the same as a step count.

## How to Set a Realistic Step Goal

1. **Find your baseline.** Wear the same device for a week without changing your routine.
2. **Add a little.** Pick a modest increase, such as 1,000 more steps a day.
3. **Think weekly.** Aim for a steady weekly pattern rather than a perfect daily number.
4. **Listen to your body.** Don't push through pain or exhaustion to hit a target.

## FAQs

### Is 7,000 Steps Better Than 10,000?

Neither is better for everyone. The 2025 review found meaningful links at 7,000 steps compared with 2,000, and walking more may still help. Your goal should fit your starting point, your ability and why you're walking.

### Are 5,000 Steps Enough for Health Benefits?

The research shows a sliding scale, not a pass-or-fail cutoff. For several outcomes, benefits began to level off between 5,000 and 7,000 steps, and some benefits rose even below 7,000. That doesn't promise a specific benefit to everyone at 5,000.

### Does Walking Only on Weekends Count?

Yes, weekly movement counts. A 2023 US study linked reaching 8,000 steps on just 1 or 2 days a week with lower risk of death over 10 years. That doesn't mean being inactive the rest of the week is ideal.

### Should I Walk 10,000 Steps If I Have Joint Pain?

Don't force a fixed step count through worsening pain. Plans should account for your symptoms, mobility and medical advice. Lower-impact activities such as swimming or cycling may suit you better, and step totals aren't the only measure of progress.
