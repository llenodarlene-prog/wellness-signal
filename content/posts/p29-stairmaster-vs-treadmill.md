# StairMaster vs Treadmill: 8 Differences in Cardio, Muscles and Calories

The StairMaster vs treadmill question usually gets framed as a calorie contest. That misses the point. Both machines raise your heart rate fast, but they ask your body for different things.

A stair machine makes you lift your own weight, step after step. A treadmill lets you walk, jog or run, and change the speed and slope as you go. So the better machine is the one that fits your goal and the effort you can keep up week after week.

Here's how the two compare on fitness, muscles, calories, joints and home use.

## StairMaster vs Treadmill at a Glance

This table shows the main differences before we get into each one.

| Factor | StairMaster | Treadmill |
|---|---|---|
| Main movement | Repeated stair climbing | Walking, jogging or running |
| How you set the pace | Step rate | Belt speed |
| Incline | Built into the stairs | Adjustable on most models |
| Easiest starting level | Still fairly demanding | A very slow walk |
| Running practice | No | Yes |
| Intervals | Yes | Yes |
| Best fit | Climbing-focused cardio | Walking-to-running progression |

## 1. Both Train Your Heart and Lungs

Either machine counts toward your weekly cardio. US activity guidelines call for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity or 75 minutes of vigorous activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening work on 2 or more days. The guidelines don't favor any one machine.

Stair climbing really does build aerobic fitness. In a 9-week randomized trial, active college-age women trained 4 days a week on either a stair machine or by running. VO2max rose 12% in the stair group and 16% in the running group. Their 2,414-meter run times improved by 8% and 11%.

The trial was small, with 23 women, and dates from 1993. Still, the authors concluded that stair climbing worked as an alternative to running, with similar results.

## 2. The Treadmill Has a Wider Range

A treadmill covers more ground, literally. You can stroll, add incline, move up to a jog and then run intervals, all on one machine. That range helps beginners build up slowly. It also helps runners practice exact speeds and grades.

The StairMaster's range starts higher. Even a slow step rate means lifting your body weight over and over. That's part of its appeal, and part of why it's harder to ease into.

## 3. Calories Depend on Effort, Not the Machine

There's no fair way to name a universal winner. You burn more when you do more work. On a treadmill that means more speed, more incline or both. On a stair machine it means faster stepping and less weight on the handrails.

Console calorie counts are estimates. A hard treadmill run can beat an easy stair session, and steady climbing can beat a casual walk. If calories matter to you, compare sessions by heart rate, effort and time rather than the number on the screen. The [Heart Rate Zone Calculator](/heart-rate-zone-calculator/) can help you set targets that work on either machine.

## 4. They Work Your Legs Differently

Both machines work the lower body, but in different ways.

- **StairMaster:** each step drives your hips and knees through extension as you climb. That puts steady work on your quads, glutes and calves.
- **Treadmill:** walking and running move your hips, knees and ankles forward rather than up. Adding incline brings in more calf and hip work.

Leaning hard on the handrails changes the picture. It shifts weight off your legs and cuts how much they have to do. Running also adds a flight phase and more impact than walking, which makes it specific practice for runners.

### Is the StairMaster Better for Glutes?

It can give your glutes plenty of work, because every step needs hip extension. A steep treadmill walk works them too. Neither machine burns fat from one body part, though. Where you lose fat isn't decided by which muscle you train.

## 5. Neither Is Automatically Easier on Joints

"Low impact" doesn't mean comfortable for everyone. Treadmill walking avoids the flight phase of running. Stair climbing bends and straightens your knees and hips under load with every step.

Some people find one pattern easier than the other, depending on their build, past injuries or technique. If something hurts, change the setup or switch machines. Pain that persists or gets worse is worth having checked.

## 6. The Treadmill Is Easier for Beginners

You can slow a treadmill to a gentle walk. A stair machine gets hard sooner, because every step still means lifting your weight.

Beginners can still use a StairMaster. Start with short bouts at a slow step rate, stand tall without hanging on the rails and add one thing at a time, such as a few more minutes. New runners who'd rather build up outdoors can start with our [best free running apps](/fitness/best-free-running-apps/) for guided plans.

## 7. The Treadmill Wins for Running Goals

If you want to run faster or prepare for a race, practice the activity itself. Treadmill running builds pace judgment, sustained speed and hill strength in a way stair climbing can't copy. Stair work still helps general fitness and leg endurance, but it doesn't train running form. Our [running FAQ](/faq/fitness/running/) covers how to build up safely.

## 8. Home Use Depends on the Model

Full-size stair machines are tall, so check your ceiling height first. Treadmills range from compact walking models to long running decks with big motors.

Before you buy either, compare:

- dimensions in use and in storage
- maximum user weight
- speed range and incline system
- power requirements
- warranty and service options

If you mostly plan to walk while you work, our [walking pad vs treadmill](/fitness/walking-pad-vs-treadmill/) comparison covers that choice.

## Which Should You Choose?

Pick by what you want to train, not by which machine burns more.

| If you want... | Start with |
|---|---|
| Climbing work and leg-heavy cardio without running | StairMaster |
| Intervals built around step rate | StairMaster |
| Walking through running on one machine | Treadmill |
| Race or pace practice | Treadmill |
| A gentle starting point | Treadmill |
| A machine for several people at different levels | Treadmill |

Many gyms have both, so you don't have to choose forever. Our [fitness statistics](/fitness/fitness-statistics-by-activity-and-age/) show the treadmill is the most-used piece of gym equipment among US facility members.

## FAQs

### Is 20 Minutes on the StairMaster Better Than 30 Minutes on a Treadmill?

Not automatically. Minutes alone don't tell you how hard a session was. A 20-minute climb can beat a 30-minute easy walk, while a 30-minute run can beat both. Compare effort, heart rate and how recovered you feel rather than time on the clock.

### Does the StairMaster Burn More Fat Than the Treadmill?

No machine targets fat directly. Fat loss comes from your overall energy balance over time. Both machines raise how much energy you use, and the amount depends on how hard and how long you go. Claims that one machine melts fat faster oversimplify it.

### Should I Use the Treadmill or StairMaster After Lifting?

Either can work if the cardio fits the session before it. A hard stair workout adds a lot of leg fatigue on top of lifting. An easy treadmill walk is usually simpler to recover from. Base the choice on what else your week includes.

### Can the StairMaster Replace Running?

It can replace running as cardio if running itself isn't your goal. In the 1993 trial, stair training improved both aerobic fitness and run times. It can't fully replace running practice, pacing and impact tolerance if you're training to run faster or farther.
