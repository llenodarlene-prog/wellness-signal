# 7 Best Hiking Apps Compared for Trails, Offline Maps and Backcountry Navigation

The best hiking apps aren't all built for the same moment. The app that finds you a Saturday hike may not be the one you want when the trail vanishes under snow, your phone loses signal or the route crosses land where map detail really counts.

Most hiking apps do one of three jobs: **trail discovery**, **route planning and navigation**, or **specialist backcountry guidance**. AllTrails is the strongest trail directory. Gaia GPS and onX Backcountry go deeper on map layers and offline use. FarOut is built around long-distance guides, while Avenza lets you carry official maps from trusted publishers.

We compared seven apps by trail database, map depth, offline use, route planning, community data and price. We checked US App Store listings in early November 2026. We didn't field-test route accuracy. No phone app should replace route preparation, a check of local conditions or a backup way to navigate in serious terrain.

## 1. AllTrails: Best for Finding and Comparing Local Trails

![AllTrails App Store artwork reading Discover 500,000 plus trails](shot-alltrails-app-store "Screenshot: AllTrails listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

AllTrails works like a search engine for trails. It combines a huge catalog of hiking, walking, running and cycling routes with community photos, reviews, filters and recorded activities.

The free version is great for discovery. Offline maps, wrong-turn alerts and extra safety and planning tools need a paid plan. The US App Store lists **AllTrails Plus at $35.99 a year** and **AllTrails Peak at $79.99 a year**.

### Where It Fits

AllTrails is best when your question is "where should I hike?" It's less suited to "which map layer do I need for this remote valley?" Recent reviews can flag trail conditions, but check user-uploaded routes against official maps and local guidance.

**Best fit:** Day hikers, beginners and travelers looking for established trails.

## 2. Gaia GPS: Best for Layered Topographic Maps

![Gaia GPS App Store artwork of hikers on a ridge headlined One tap to your next adventure](shot-gaia-gps-app-store "Screenshot: Gaia GPS listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Gaia GPS is built around map depth. You can stack topographic, satellite, public land, weather and other layers. You can also plan routes and download maps for offline use.

Offline maps and the bigger map catalog come with paid plans. The App Store lists **Gaia GPS Premium at $59.90 a year**, or **$89.90 a year** bundled with Outside+.

### Where It Fits

Gaia makes most sense if you already read maps and want control over what they show. It's not a polished "best hikes near me" list. Its strength is planning and navigating with several layers at once.

**Best fit:** Backpackers and experienced hikers who want map control.

## 3. onX Backcountry: Best for 3D Terrain and Backcountry Layers

![onX Backcountry App Store artwork with a 3D terrain map and hike, bike, ski and climb filters](shot-onx-backcountry-app-store "Screenshot: onX Backcountry listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

onX Backcountry combines hiking and biking trails with 3D maps, offline downloads, route planning, slope data, recent satellite images and weather layers. It also includes climbing and ski routes, so it's broader than a hiking-only app.

In the App Store, Backcountry Premium is listed at **$29.99 a year** and Elite at **$99.99 a year**.

### Where It Fits

The 3D and slope tools help when elevation and steepness drive your route choice. They don't replace avalanche training, weather forecasts or local safety advice.

**Best fit:** Backcountry users who want terrain context and offline maps.

## 4. komoot: Best for Route Planning and Turn-by-Turn Directions

![komoot App Store artwork headlined Explore 7M plus routes to hike, ride and run](shot-komoot-app-store "Screenshot: komoot listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

komoot is built for planning your own hiking, running and cycling routes. It shows distance, elevation, surface and difficulty, then gives you turn-by-turn voice directions.

Offline maps are bought by region or as a World Pack, listed in the App Store at **$29.99**. komoot Premium adds more features for **$59.99 a year**.

### Where It Fits

komoot shines when you're building a route yourself instead of picking a fixed trail. Voice directions also help if you'd rather not keep checking your screen.

**Best fit:** Hikers and cyclists who enjoy planning custom routes.

## 5. HiiKER: Best for Multi-Day Hiking Plans

![HiiKER app topographic map with offline and 3D mode labels under the headline Plan, map and track your next hike](shot-hiiker-app-store "Screenshot: HiiKER listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

HiiKER focuses on hiking and trekking. Its route planner can split a long route into several days. It also offers topographic maps from national and independent map providers in several countries.

Offline downloads are part of HiiKER PRO+. The App Store lists a monthly PRO+ plan at **$4.99**, with annual plans too.

### Where It Fits

The multi-day planner sets HiiKER apart from apps aimed at short local walks. It's a stronger pick when campsites, daily sections and long-route organization matter.

**Best fit:** Multi-day hikers and trekkers.

## 6. Avenza Maps: Best for Official Maps and Custom Offline Files

![Avenza Maps app showing an offline topographic map](shot-avenza-maps-app-store "Screenshot: Avenza Maps listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Avenza Maps works differently from the trail directories. It displays official maps with GPS positioning from publishers such as the National Park Service, US Forest Service and USGS. It also supports your own GeoPDF and GeoTIFF files.

Many maps are free, and others are sold one at a time. Avenza Maps Plus is listed at **$34.99** and Pro at **$169.99** for custom imports and professional features.

### Where It Fits

Avenza is the pick when the map itself is what you care about. You can carry an official park or topo map offline and still see your position on it.

**Best fit:** Hikers who prefer official map products or need a specific published map.

## 7. FarOut: Best for Long-Distance Trail Guides

![FarOut app listing long-distance trail guides including the Pacific Crest Trail](shot-farout-app-store "Screenshot: FarOut listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

FarOut is best known for long-distance hiking and backpacking guides. Its route packages include trail data, water sources, resupply points, campsites and other waypoints that matter on multi-day trips.

You can subscribe to FarOut Unlimited, listed at **$15 a month**, or buy individual guides. The Appalachian Trail guide, for example, is listed at **$74.99**.

### Where It Fits

A thru-hiker on a named trail needs different things from a casual hiker looking for a nearby waterfall. FarOut is built for route-specific planning and offline reference.

**Best fit:** Thru-hikers and long-distance backpackers.

## FAQs

### What Is the Best Free Hiking App?

AllTrails is a strong free choice for finding trails and reading reviews. Avenza also gives access to many free official maps, though some are sold separately. The right pick depends on whether you need discovery or navigation.

### Which Hiking App Has the Best Offline Maps?

Gaia GPS, onX Backcountry, HiiKER, Avenza and FarOut all handle serious offline use in different ways. There's no single best map, because the answer depends on whether you need layered topography, an official map, a long-distance guide or a custom route.

### Is AllTrails Good Enough for Backcountry Hiking?

It can help, but relying on one community route isn't ideal where navigation really matters. For remote terrain, compare the route with official maps and land manager updates, and carry an offline backup.

### Which App Is Best for Thru-Hiking?

FarOut is built specifically around named long-distance trails and their waypoints. HiiKER is another strong option for multi-day planning, and Gaia GPS helps if you want more control over the maps themselves.

### Do Hiking Apps Work Without Cell Service?

Many do, if you download the maps or guides before you go. Offline access often needs a paid plan or a purchased map, so installing the app alone doesn't guarantee the map you need will work without signal.
