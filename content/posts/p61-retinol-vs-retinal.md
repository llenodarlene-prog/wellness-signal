# Retinol vs Retinal: What the Chemistry and Human Studies Show

In the retinol vs retinal comparison, the two aren't the same ingredient. Both are vitamin A derivatives used in skincare, but retinal, short for retinaldehyde, is a different chemical form. Retinal is one conversion step closer to retinoic acid, the form that acts on skin. That doesn't make every retinal product better than every retinol product. Formulation, stability, strength and how your skin tolerates it all matter.

**Quick answer:** Retinal is chemically one step closer to the active form, and one small trial found a specific retinal formula improved some aging measures more than retinol. But the evidence is limited to particular products. Pick one retinoid, start slowly and judge by how your skin responds.

## Retinol vs Retinal at a Glance

| | Retinol | Retinal (retinaldehyde) |
|---|---|---|
| Chemical type | Vitamin A alcohol | Vitamin A aldehyde |
| Steps to retinoic acid | Retinol → retinal → retinoic acid | Retinal → retinoic acid |
| Where you find it | Many over-the-counter products | Some over-the-counter products |
| Research | Broad evidence | Smaller evidence base |
| Can it irritate? | Yes | Yes |
| Proven better overall? | No | No |

## Why "One Step Closer" Isn't the Whole Story

Brands often call retinal "stronger" because it's closer to retinoic acid. But how much active ingredient actually reaches your skin depends on the formula, its stability and how it's delivered. A 0.1% retinal serum isn't directly comparable with a 0.1% retinol serum. The label percentage can't tell you how much reaches your skin, how irritating it will be or how it performs over months.

## What Clinical Studies Found

**A direct comparison.** A 2021 split-face study in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology* enrolled 23 Korean volunteers. Each applied a retinal cream on one side of the face and a retinol cream at the same strength on the other, for 8 weeks. Both sides improved on all objective measures, including wrinkle depth. The retinal side improved more on every measure except dermal density, with no adverse events reported. But the retinal was in a special delivery system (multilamellar vesicles), so the result may reflect that formula, not retinal in general.

**Retinal at two strengths.** A 2018 trial gave 40 Korean women a 0.1% or 0.05% retinal cream twice a day for 3 months. Both improved texture and hydration with no statistical difference between them. Only the 0.1% cream improved a melanin measure. Both were well tolerated.

**The bigger picture.** A 2025 network meta-analysis in *Scientific Reports* pooled 23 randomized trials with 3,905 participants. It found isotretinoin, retinol and tretinoin significantly improved fine wrinkles. It wasn't a head-to-head test of retinol vs retinal products.

## Which Causes Less Irritation?

Either can cause dryness, redness, peeling or stinging, especially if you start too often or combine it with other strong products. One retinal study reporting good tolerance doesn't prove retinal is gentler as a category.

A dermatologist quoted by the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) advises using the least intense retinoid you can find, every other night to start, and slowly building up. The AAD also stresses moisturizer and daily sun protection, and notes that in skin of color, irritation can trigger dark marks called hyperpigmentation.

## How to Start a Retinoid

1. **Pick one product.** Don't stack several retinoids.
2. **Start slowly.** Every other night, then build up as your skin allows.
3. **Moisturize.** A plain moisturizer helps with dryness.
4. **Use sunscreen daily.** Seek shade and cover up too.
5. **Skip harsh extras at first.** Avoid acids, scrubs and strong exfoliants on the same night until you know how your skin reacts.
6. **Ease off if it stings.** Reduce how often you use it, or stop and ask a dermatologist.

### Pregnancy

The AAD says retinoids should not be used during pregnancy. If you're pregnant or planning to be, check your skincare with your obstetric clinician or dermatologist.

## How to Compare Products

Look for the exact ingredient name, the concentration if listed, air-tight or opaque packaging, an expiry date and any other actives. A study on one delivery system doesn't validate a different product.

## FAQs

### Is Retinal the Same as Retinaldehyde?

Yes. In skincare, retinal means retinaldehyde. It's different from retinol, and both differ from prescription tretinoin, which is retinoic acid itself.

### Is Retinal Stronger Than Retinol?

It's one conversion step closer to retinoic acid, and one small study found a specific retinal formula outperformed retinol on several measures. That doesn't mean every retinal product beats every retinol product.

### Can I Use Retinol and Retinal Together?

Using two retinoids at once is likely to increase irritation. Choose one, and talk to a dermatologist about more complex routines.

### How Long Do Retinoids Take to Work?

The studies above measured results after 8 weeks to 3 months. Expect gradual change, not overnight results, and keep using sunscreen.
