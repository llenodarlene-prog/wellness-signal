# The WHOOP Business Model: A Wearable Sold as a Membership

WHOOP positions its wearable tracker as part of a membership rather than a conventional stand-alone device purchase. Customers pay for access to hardware and supported biometric analysis, with the service tied to recurring subscription terms.

This makes the WHOOP business model different from a company selling a smartwatch that retains extensive functionality after purchase. The long-term cost and the consequences of cancellation are central to the purchasing decision. Wearables sit alongside gyms in the wider picture of how Americans exercise, set out in our [fitness statistics by activity and age](/fitness/fitness-statistics-by-activity-and-age/).

## Key Takeaways

- WHOOP's published US annual plans include **One at $199**, **Peak at $239** and **Life at $359**.
- One and Peak include WHOOP 5.0; Life includes WHOOP MG under the described plan structure.
- Continued biometric collection and analysis depend on active membership.
- Public pricing does not reveal WHOOP's exact profits or subscriber retention.

### The Business Purpose of Tiered Access

Higher tiers provide additional supported features and associated hardware capabilities. A customer upgrading to a more expensive plan may pay more annually, but those payments cannot be assumed to convert entirely into profit because service and device costs also differ.

## How WHOOP Generates Recurring Revenue

Members pay for defined subscription periods, creating potential continuing revenue so long as they renew. Connected equipment uses a similar logic, as our [Peloton business model](/fitness/peloton-business-model-hardware-memberships/) analysis shows. WHOOP also bears the costs of supplying hardware and delivering software, support and updates.

### Long-Term Cost of Membership

At unchanged list prices, five annual payments would total $995 for One, $1,195 for Peak and $1,795 for Life. These are illustrative calculations rather than guaranteed prices or estimates of WHOOP's realised revenue.

## What Happens When Membership Ends

WHOOP's cancellation guidance states that the device stops collecting, uploading and analysing biometric data after membership access expires. Customers need to consider that restriction when comparing the service with wearables offering independent basic functionality.

### Contract Commitments and Monthly Billing

Some membership arrangements billed monthly can still involve a longer commitment. The cancellation date and remaining obligation depend on the specific agreement, so consumers should review the terms displayed during purchase.

## WHOOP Compared With Hardware-First Wearables

With a hardware-first device, the consumer may purchase equipment outright and optionally add software services. Our [Oura business model](/wellness/oura-business-model-ring-membership/) analysis covers a ring sold separately from its membership. Costs over three years are set side by side in our [WHOOP vs Oura](/longevity-recovery/whoop-vs-oura/) comparison. WHOOP combines these into a membership relationship, changing both the initial payment and continued access conditions.

## FAQs

### How does WHOOP make money?

WHOOP principally sells recurring access to a wearable tracking service that includes eligible hardware and software features. Membership pricing is public, but the privately held company does not provide complete audited figures showing profit per member or precise revenue by plan.

### Can a WHOOP device work without membership?

According to WHOOP's cancellation support materials, biometric collection, uploading and analysis stop after the subscription expires. Consumers should review current terms before purchase because continued functionality is substantially different from a stand-alone fitness watch.

### How much does WHOOP cost over five years?

Using unchanged published US annual rates, five years cost $995 for One, $1,195 for Peak or $1,795 for Life. Real spending may differ due to promotions, taxes, upgrades, regional pricing or changes to future plans.

## Conclusion

WHOOP's subscription-first approach turns device access into an ongoing service relationship. Its value depends on the features customers use, how long they retain membership and the financial implications of renewal or cancellation.
