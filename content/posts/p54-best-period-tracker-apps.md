# Best Period Tracker Apps: 4 Options Compared for Privacy and Features

The best period tracker apps do two jobs: they help you understand your cycle, and they protect some of the most personal data on your phone. Most comparisons focus on the first job. This one gives equal weight to the second: where your data is stored, who can read it and whether you can use the app without an account.

We compared four apps using Apple's support documents and each app's official App Store listing or website, checked in November 2026. This is a research-based comparison, not a hands-on test, and no app paid to be included.

## Period Tracker Apps at a Glance

| App | Best for | Where your data lives | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Apple Health Cycle Tracking | iPhone and Apple Watch owners | On device; end-to-end encrypted in iCloud under conditions | Free, built in |
| Clue | Detailed tracking under EU privacy law | Clue's servers, Berlin-based, GDPR | Free; Clue Plus subscription |
| Flo | Reminders and insights, with an anonymous option | Flo's servers; optional Anonymous Mode | Free; Flo Premium subscription |
| drip | Keeping everything on your phone | Only on your device | Free, open source |

## 1. Apple Health Cycle Tracking: Built Into iPhone

If you have an iPhone, you already have a period tracker. Apple's Cycle Tracking lives in the Health app and on Apple Watch. It can notify you when your next period or fertile window is approaching, and it covers pregnancy and menopause logging too.

Apple's support page explains its privacy protections. When your iPhone is locked with a passcode, Touch ID or Face ID, your Health data is encrypted. If you use the latest software with two-factor authentication, health data synced to iCloud is end-to-end encrypted, so Apple can't read it.

**Choose Apple Health if** you're on iPhone and want a private tracker with no extra app.

## 2. Clue: Detailed Tracking Under EU Privacy Law

Clue is one of the best-known cycle trackers. Its App Store listing says the company is headquartered in Berlin, so your data is protected by the EU's GDPR privacy law. The free app tracks periods, ovulation estimates and symptoms. A Clue Plus subscription adds deeper analysis, advanced predictions and personalized insights.

**Choose Clue if** you want detailed tracking with symptom logging and strong legal privacy protections.

![Clue App Store listing showing period prediction and symptom tracking screens](shot-clue-app-store "Screenshot: Clue listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

## 3. Flo: Insights With an Anonymous Mode

Flo is among the most popular period apps, and it's built around predictions, reminders and educational content. Its standout privacy feature is Anonymous Mode. Flo's App Store listing says that with it on, your name, email address and IP address aren't linked to your health data. Flo's privacy portal says it encrypts stored data with AES-256 and secures data in transit with TLS.

The core app is free, with a paid Flo Premium plan for more features.

**Choose Flo if** you want reminders and insights, and you'll turn on Anonymous Mode.

![Flo App Store listing showing period prediction, fertile days and symptom logging](shot-flo-app-store "Screenshot: Flo listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

## 4. Drip: Your Data Never Leaves Your Phone

drip takes the strictest approach. Its website says everything you enter stays on your device. It's open source, so anyone can check how it works, and it shows how it calculates predictions instead of hiding them. You can track just your period or use the symptothermal method to track fertility signs. It's available on iPhone, Google Play and F-Droid.

The trade-off: with no cloud account, you're responsible for backups. Lose your phone without one and you lose your history.

**Choose drip if** keeping cycle data off anyone's servers is your top priority.

## Period Apps Are Not Birth Control

All four apps say they shouldn't be used as contraception. Apple states that Cycle Tracking should not be used as a form of birth control. Clue's listing says it should not be used as a contraceptive, and Flo says it is not a method of birth control.

Predictions are estimates. Cycles shift with stress, illness, medications, postpartum changes and perimenopause. If your cycle changes a lot or you notice worrying symptoms, see a doctor rather than relying on app predictions.

## Seven Privacy Questions to Ask Before You Download

1. What does the app collect, and is any of it optional?
2. Is your data stored on your phone, the company's servers or both?
3. Is it encrypted, and can the company read it?
4. Does the app share data with advertisers or other third parties?
5. Can you use it without an account or your real name?
6. Can you export your history?
7. Does deleting the app also delete your account and server data? Often these are separate steps.

The right answer depends on your concerns. A cloud app helps you recover data from a lost phone. A local-only app keeps data off servers but needs you to back it up.

## FAQs

### What Is the Most Private Period Tracker App?

drip keeps everything on your device and is open source. Apple Health is also strong on privacy for iPhone users, with end-to-end encryption for iCloud health data when you use two-factor authentication. Flo offers an Anonymous Mode if you prefer its features.

### Is Apple Health Good for Tracking Periods?

Yes, for most iPhone users. It logs periods and symptoms, predicts your next period and fertile window, and sends notifications. It's free and built in, with strong privacy protections.

### Can a Period App Be Used as Birth Control?

No. Apple, Clue and Flo all state that their cycle tracking shouldn't be used as birth control. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist about contraception options.

### Are Period Tracker Apps Free?

All four have free versions. Apple Health and drip are fully free. Clue and Flo are free to download, with paid subscriptions, Clue Plus and Flo Premium, for extra features.
