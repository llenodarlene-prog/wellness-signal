# The Mindbody Business Model Beyond Booking Software

A fitness studio needs to schedule classes, manage memberships, process payments and communicate with customers. Mindbody combines many of those operations in one platform, which gives the Mindbody business model a reach beyond simple booking software.

Mindbody charges for access to management software and offers additional services involving transactions, marketing and customer discovery. Those fee categories need to be separated when assessing the total cost for a studio.

## Key Takeaways

- Mindbody's core offering is subscription business-management software.
- Mindbody's published US pricing starts at **$79 per month per location**.
- Integrated payments may incur processing fees in addition to the subscription.
- The Mindbody marketplace describes a **20% first-purchase acquisition fee capped at $30** for qualifying US transactions.
- These charges are different from ClassPass's membership-credit and partner-payout model.

## The Core Software Subscription

Mindbody supports scheduling, customer records, memberships and reporting. Its published US options include Starter, Accelerate, Ultimate and custom enterprise arrangements, with features increasing across tiers.

### Why Location-Based Pricing Is Significant

Mindbody's published pricing starts per location, making the total base subscription dependent partly on a business's footprint. Larger companies may also purchase advanced features or customised services, so advertised starting prices are not universal quotes.

## How Payments Add Another Fee Category

Mindbody Payments connects payment processing with appointments and customer records. Processing charges can depend on transaction method and contract terms, making transaction volume a relevant consideration.

### Subscription Fees Are Not Processing Fees

A studio may pay a monthly software charge and separate fees for each processed transaction. Comparing providers solely by their advertised subscription rates may therefore understate the full operating cost.

## Mindbody's Consumer Marketplace

The platform's consumer app can introduce new customers to participating wellness businesses. Its publicly described US acquisition policy applies 20% to qualifying first purchases, with a $30 cap, rather than charging that percentage to every subsequent reservation.

### When Customer Acquisition Fees May Be Worth Paying

A fee tied to an actual first purchase can be attractive compared with promotional spending that generates no reservations. Still, businesses should examine repeat visits and contribution after costs to determine whether acquired customers are financially valuable.

## Marketing and Customer Retention

Higher software tiers offer capabilities for automated communications, reporting and lead management. These features may reduce administrative work, but their value depends on whether the business uses them effectively. Retention matters because, as our [fitness statistics](/fitness/fitness-statistics-by-activity-and-age/) show, gym membership and regular exercise are not the same thing.

## Comparing Mindbody and ClassPass

Mindbody can be a studio's underlying management system, while [ClassPass](/fitness/classpass-business-model-studio-partners/) operates a member-credit marketplace for reserving participating services. Their commercial relationships overlap but should not be treated as interchangeable.

## FAQs

### How does Mindbody make money?

Mindbody charges for business-management software and offers payments, customer acquisition and other services that can create additional fees. The company is privately held, so published pricing does not establish how much revenue each channel actually contributes.

### Does Mindbody take a percentage of every booking?

Its published US marketplace policy describes a 20% fee, capped at $30, on qualifying first purchases from new customers. That is not a blanket commission on all reservations, although separate payment-processing charges may still apply.

### What should studios compare before selecting Mindbody?

Studios should assess the base plan, required features, processing rates, marketplace fees and migration needs. A cheaper subscription can become more expensive if the business depends heavily on paid extras or higher transaction charges.

## Conclusion

Mindbody's model connects recurring software access with commercial services supporting transactions and customer acquisition. Studios can assess its value only after comparing the full cost with the operational benefits they actually receive.
