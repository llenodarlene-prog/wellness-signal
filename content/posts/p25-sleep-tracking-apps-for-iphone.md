# 7 Sleep Tracking Apps for iPhone Compared by Tracking Method, Smart Alarm and Cost

Sleep tracking apps for iPhone can work without a watch, but how they track matters. Some listen for movement and night sounds from your bedside table. Some use the phone's motion sensors. Others can also pull in Apple Watch or Apple Health data when it's there.

So "best sleep tracker" is too broad to be useful. Someone without a watch needs a different app from someone who wants to record snoring, use a smart alarm or export a month of data for a doctor.

We compared seven iPhone apps by how they collect data, what works without a wearable, what costs extra and what the app can realistically tell you. We checked US App Store listings in early November 2026. We didn't test sleep stage accuracy ourselves. Results from consumer apps don't replace a sleep study or a medical diagnosis.

## What a Phone Can and Can't Measure

A phone estimates sleep from movement, sound, breathing-related signals or a mix of those. It can't measure brain waves, eye movements or muscle activity. Those are the signals a clinical sleep study (polysomnography) records.

That matters because many apps show neat stage charts and scores. They can reveal patterns, but a precise-looking chart doesn't mean the measurement behind it is precise. Our [sleep stages FAQ](/faq/mind-sleep/sleep-stages/) explains what each stage actually is.

## 1. Sleep Cycle: Best for Phone-Only Tracking Plus a Smart Alarm

![Sleep Cycle App Store artwork headlined Sleep Better Tonight with a smart alarm feature list](shot-sleep-cycle-app-store "Screenshot: Sleep Cycle listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Sleep Cycle tracks from your bedside without a wearable. It uses sound analysis or the phone's motion sensor to estimate your night. Its smart alarm aims to wake you during a lighter stretch of sleep.

It also records sounds such as snoring, coughing and sleep talking. Premium adds deeper trends, data export, sleep content and more analysis.

### Where It Fits

It's a practical choice if you don't want to wear anything overnight but still want a nightly record and a gentler alarm. The catch is placement. Results depend on where the phone sits and what the microphone or sensor can pick up.

**Best fit:** Phone-only sleepers who want tracking and a smart alarm in one app.

**Cost:** Free core app. The App Store shows several Premium prices, so check the live checkout.

## 2. ShutEye: Best for Sleep Sounds Plus Snore Recording

![ShutEye app showing a sleep score of 92 and a sleep stage chart](shot-shuteye-app-store "Screenshot: ShutEye listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

ShutEye combines sleep tracking with white noise, bedtime audio, a smart alarm and night sound recording. It can save sessions to Apple Health and doesn't need a watch for its core features.

The US App Store lists the standard annual Premium plan at **$59.99 a year**, with a $9.99 monthly option and other offers at times.

### Where It Fits

It's more of an all-in-one sleep app than a pure tracker. It suits someone who wants one app for bedtime, overnight and the morning, instead of a tracker plus a separate sound app.

**Best fit:** People who want soundscapes, snore recording and tracking together.

## 3. SleepScore: Best for Contact-Free Tracking From the Nightstand

![SleepScore App Store artwork of a phone on a nightstand under the words Get your best sleep](shot-sleepscore-app-store "Screenshot: SleepScore listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

SleepScore uses sonar from the phone to monitor movement and breathing patterns from your bedside table. The company says its technology has been checked against sleep lab testing in published validation studies.

The free app gives basic tracking. Premium adds history, trends, reports and guidance for **$9.99 a month or $59.99 a year**.

### Where It Fits

It's one of the more distinctive phone-only options, because the phone doesn't go in your bed. That suits people who dislike wearing a device or sleeping on top of their phone.

Its validation claims apply to its technology under study conditions. They don't mean a phone app can diagnose a sleep disorder.

**Best fit:** People who want tracking from the nightstand without a wearable.

## 4. Pillow: Best If You Switch Between iPhone and Apple Watch

![Pillow app sleep chart under the headline Sleeping better, made simple](shot-pillow-app-store "Screenshot: Pillow listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Pillow works on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, so it sits between phone-only and watch-first trackers. It offers stage estimates, a smart alarm, trends, heart rate data when available and audio recording of night sounds.

The app is free to download. Premium unlocks more history and analysis, with App Store options including **$39.99 a year**.

### Where It Fits

Pillow helps if you sometimes wear your watch to bed and sometimes don't. That flexibility makes it less specialized than AutoSleep, which centers on Watch data.

**Best fit:** Apple users who want one sleep app across iPhone and Apple Watch.

## 5. Sleepzy: Best for a Smart Alarm With Phone-Based Estimates

![Sleepzy App Store artwork calling it your personal sleep assistant, with an App of the Day badge](shot-sleepzy-app-store "Screenshot: Sleepzy listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Sleepzy pairs a sleep tracker with a smart alarm, sleep sounds and morning stats. It uses the phone's sensors and microphone, and it can record night sounds if you allow it.

Premium adds fuller reports and removes limits and ads. The App Store shows several plans and promotions, so the live subscription screen is your best guide to price.

**Best fit:** People who care most about the alarm and a simple morning summary.

## 6. SleepWatch: Best for Reports and Coaching

![SleepWatch App Store artwork listing sleep tracking, sound recording and smart alarm features](shot-sleepwatch-app-store "Screenshot: SleepWatch listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

SleepWatch can run from your iPhone or with an Apple Watch. The phone-only route gives you a report and sound features. Adding a watch brings more sensor data.

Premium includes personal coaching, richer reports and a smart alarm. The US price is **$4.99 a month or $39.99 a year**.

**Best fit:** People who want long-term reports and may add a watch later.

## 7. Sleep Time: Best for a Simple Smart Alarm

![Sleep Time App Store artwork of a phone alarm headlined Sleep Better and Wake Up More Refreshed](shot-sleep-time-app-store "Screenshot: Sleep Time listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Sleep Time uses a phone placed on your bed to estimate sleep cycles and offer a smart wake-up window. It also includes sleep sounds, daily and long-term graphs, history and Apple Health support.

The basic app is free. Premium adds more soundscapes and advanced reports.

**Best fit:** People who want a classic phone-on-bed tracker and alarm without a big wellness platform.

## FAQs

### Can an iPhone Track Sleep Without Apple Watch?

Yes. Sleep Cycle, ShutEye, SleepScore, Sleepzy and Sleep Time use the phone's microphone, motion sensors or sonar. Place the phone as each app instructs, and treat the stage results as consumer estimates rather than clinical testing.

### Which iPhone Sleep Tracker Works Without Putting the Phone in Bed?

SleepScore is designed for contact-free tracking from your nightstand. Sleep Cycle can also use microphone-based tracking from beside the bed. Apps that rely more on motion usually need the phone on the mattress.

### Can Sleep Apps Detect Sleep Apnea?

No. An app may record snoring or show breathing patterns, but that doesn't establish a diagnosis. If you have witnessed breathing pauses, heavy daytime sleepiness or loud, persistent snoring, see a clinician for proper assessment.

### Are Sleep Tracking Apps Accurate?

They're useful for broad patterns, but accuracy depends on the signal and your setup. A phone can't reproduce the measurements used in a sleep lab, so treat stage charts as estimates.

### Is a Free Sleep Tracker Enough?

Often, yes, if you mainly want rough sleep times or want to try a smart alarm. Paid tiers make more sense when you want longer history, exports, coaching, sound libraries or deeper trends.
