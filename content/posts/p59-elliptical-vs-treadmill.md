# Elliptical vs Treadmill: Which Cardio Machine Fits Your Goal?

The elliptical vs treadmill choice comes down to the movement you want to train. An elliptical guides your feet along a fixed path with no running-style flight phase. A treadmill lets you walk, climb an incline, jog or run on a moving belt. That makes the treadmill better for running practice, but not automatically better for every person or every workout.

**Quick answer:** Choose an elliptical for continuous, low-impact cardio if you like its movement and the pedal path suits your body. Choose a treadmill for walking, running, pacing and incline training. Calories depend on how hard you work, not on the machine's name.

## Elliptical vs Treadmill at a Glance

| Question | Elliptical | Treadmill |
|---|---|---|
| Can I walk naturally? | Not exactly; you follow a guided oval | Yes |
| Does it train running form and pace? | Limited carryover | Yes |
| Is there a flight phase? | No | Yes when running, no when walking |
| How do I make it harder? | Resistance and incline, on many models | Speed and incline |
| Upper-body work? | Moving handles on many models | Handrails are for balance |
| Home-buying check | Stride length and footprint | Belt length, folding and ceiling clearance |
| Which burns more calories? | Depends on effort | Depends on speed and grade |

## Which Burns More Calories?

A 2024 lab study in *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* compared seven indoor cardio machines. Thirty recreationally active men, average age about 42, did 12-minute bouts on each at a hard self-selected effort and at maximum effort. Researchers measured oxygen use and heart rate. The treadmill produced the highest energy expenditure, followed by the stair climber and then the elliptical. The bikes and rowing machine came lower.

That's useful evidence for those conditions, but it isn't a rule. The study tested one group of middle-aged men on specific machines. A hard elliptical session can still burn more than an easy treadmill walk. Console calorie numbers are estimates, and two machines may not use the same formula.

### A Simple Way to Compare at Home

On two different days, try each machine for 20 minutes: warm up, spend 15 minutes at a pace where you can talk in short sentences, then cool down. Note your effort on a 0 to 10 scale, your average heart rate if you track it and any joint discomfort. It's a practical comparison, not a controlled test.

## Is an Elliptical Easier on Your Knees?

An elliptical removes the landing impact of running. But less impact doesn't mean less stress on every part of the knee, and some people find the fixed knee bend uncomfortable.

A 2016 study of 16 healthy runners in the *Journal of Applied Biomechanics* compared treadmill running with a standard and a lateral elliptical. The lateral elliptical produced greater knee flexion and abduction, and the authors said that could increase symptoms in runners with anterior knee pain. Treadmill running also used more oxygen than the standard elliptical.

If your knees hurt, start with low resistance and a comfortable stride, or try flat treadmill walking. See a physical therapist or clinician if pain persists or your knee is swollen, unstable or locking.

## Which Is Better for Endurance?

Either machine can build aerobic fitness. The CDC recommends adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening on 2 days. It doesn't favor any machine.

For runners, the treadmill is more specific: it trains pace, running mechanics and, on many models, incline. An elliptical is handy on days when you want cardio without extra running impact, but it doesn't fully reproduce running's landing and push-off.

## Which Works More Muscles?

Both work your quads, glutes and calves. The elliptical's moving handles can add arm work, depending on how actively you push and pull. Incline walking on a treadmill increases the demand on your legs and glutes. Neither replaces strength training if building muscle is your goal.

## Buying One for Home

Measure the space you'll actually use, not just the footprint in the listing. For an elliptical, check the stride length and handle clearance. For a treadmill, check belt size, top speed, incline range and ceiling height when running on an incline. Try the exact model if you can. An elliptical with a stride that doesn't fit you isn't a bargain.

## The Bottom Line

- **Choose the elliptical** if running impact bothers you, you like smooth guided motion and the stride fits your body.
- **Choose the treadmill** if you want to walk, run intervals, train for a race or track pace and incline.

## FAQs

### Is 30 Minutes on an Elliptical the Same as 30 Minutes on a Treadmill?

Only in time. The work depends on speed, incline, resistance, cadence, body size and technique. Match your effort rather than assuming two 30-minute sessions burn the same calories.

### Is the Elliptical Better Than Running for Bad Knees?

It may feel better because there's no landing impact, but the fixed path bothers some knees. In one study, a lateral elliptical motion increased knee bending in ways that could aggravate front-of-knee pain. Flat treadmill walking is another low-impact option.

### Can I Replace Running With the Elliptical?

For general fitness, often yes. For race pace, running mechanics and getting your body used to landing, the elliptical is a supplement, not a substitute.

### Does the Treadmill Burn More Calories Than the Elliptical?

In a 2024 lab study of 30 men, the treadmill produced the highest energy expenditure of seven machines, with the elliptical third. At a matched effort the gap may be small, and a harder elliptical session can beat an easy treadmill walk.
