# Average Sleep Hours by Age: How Much Americans Sleep vs How Much They Need

Search for average sleep hours by age and you'll mostly find charts of recommended sleep. That's not the same as how much people actually get. Guidelines describe a healthy target. National surveys describe what people report.

The latest US data comes from the CDC's 2024 National Health Interview Survey, published in April 2026. It reports how many adults sleep less than 7 hours, by age. It doesn't publish an average number of hours for each age group, and you can't work one out from those percentages.

Here's how the recommendations compare with what Americans report.

## Recommended Sleep by Age

The CDC lists these recommended amounts, based on expert sleep societies. They're targets, not measured averages.

| Age | Recommended sleep per day |
|---|---|
| 6 to 12 years | 9 to 12 hours |
| 13 to 17 years | 8 to 10 hours |
| 18 to 60 years | 7 or more hours |
| 61 to 64 years | 7 to 9 hours |
| 65 and older | 7 to 8 hours |

Babies and younger children have separate, higher recommendations. Our [sleep duration FAQ](/faq/mind-sleep/sleep-duration/) covers every age group.

## How Many Adults Fall Short, by Age

In 2024, 30.5% of US adults said they slept less than 7 hours in a 24-hour period, on average.

| Age | Adults sleeping less than 7 hours |
|---|---|
| 18 to 34 | 27.2% |
| 35 to 49 | 33.4% |
| 50 to 64 | 34.5% |
| 65 and older | 27.2% |
| All adults | 30.5% |

Short sleep peaked in middle age, not among young adults. The survey doesn't explain why. Work, caregiving, health and other factors weren't separated out as causes.

## Why 30.5% Isn't an Average of Hours

The 30.5% figure shows how many people fall below a cutoff. It says nothing about how far below, or how much the other 69.5% sleep. Two groups could both have 30.5% under 7 hours and still have very different average sleep.

Working out a true average needs everyone's reported hours, which this summary doesn't publish. So be wary of any site that turns this figure into an exact "average American sleeps X hours" claim.

## Getting Enough Sleep vs Feeling Rested

Hours aren't the whole story. In the same survey, 54.8% of adults said they woke up feeling well-rested most days or every day.

| Age | Wake up well-rested most days |
|---|---|
| 18 to 34 | 52.2% |
| 35 to 49 | 50.1% |
| 50 to 64 | 54.5% |
| 65 and older | 63.5% |

Adults 65 and older were the most likely to feel rested, even though their recommended range is lower. You can sleep enough hours and still wake up tired. For a look at trouble falling and staying asleep, see our [sleep deprivation statistics](/mind-sleep/sleep-deprivation-statistics/).

## Can a Sleep Tracker Tell You Your Average?

Trackers estimate sleep from movement and heart signals. The national survey asks people to report their sleep. Both have limits. A tracker is handy for spotting your own trends, but it doesn't give population averages. ## FAQs

### How Many Hours Does the Average American Adult Sleep?

The CDC's latest report doesn't publish an average number of hours. It found that 30.5% of US adults slept less than 7 hours in 2024. Don't turn that percentage into an average, because it only shows how many people fall below 7 hours.

### Do Older Adults Need Less Sleep?

The CDC lists 7 to 8 hours for adults 65 and older, compared with 7 or more hours for adults 18 to 60. Individual needs vary. In 2024, older adults were also the most likely to wake up feeling well-rested, at 63.5%.

### Which Age Group Gets the Least Sleep?

In the 2024 survey, adults aged 50 to 64 were the most likely to sleep less than 7 hours, at 34.5%. Adults 35 to 49 were close behind at 33.4%. That measures how many fall short, not which group sleeps the fewest hours on average.

### Does 7 Hours Guarantee Good Sleep?

No. How long you sleep and how well you sleep are different things. The CDC survey measured feeling well-rested and trouble falling or staying asleep separately. If you often wake up unrefreshed, it may be worth checking even if your hours look fine.
