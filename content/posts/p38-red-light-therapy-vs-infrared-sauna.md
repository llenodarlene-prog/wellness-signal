# Red Light Therapy vs Infrared Sauna: 7 Differences in Light, Heat and Evidence

Red light therapy and infrared saunas are often sold side by side, so they're easy to mix up. Both involve light energy, but they don't do the same thing to your body.

Red light therapy, also called photobiomodulation (PBM), uses specific red and near-infrared wavelengths at set doses. The goal is a response inside your cells. An infrared sauna is built mainly to heat you up.

That difference matters more than any list of claimed benefits. Here's how the red light therapy vs infrared sauna comparison breaks down.

## Red Light Therapy vs Infrared Sauna at a Glance

| Factor | Red light therapy (PBM) | Infrared sauna |
|---|---|---|
| Main exposure | Red and near-infrared light | Infrared heat |
| Main goal | A light-driven cell response | Whole-body heating |
| Sweating | Not needed | Common |
| Core temperature rise | Usually small | Expected |
| Area treated | One area or the whole body | Usually the whole body |
| Key specs | Wavelength, irradiance, dose, distance | Heater type, temperature, time |
| Evidence | Depends on the condition and device | Heat-therapy studies, many small |
| FDA route | Some devices cleared for specific uses | A different product category |

## 1. Shared Wavelengths Don't Make Them the Same

Near-infrared light can be part of PBM, but an infrared sauna is designed to warm your body.

A PBM panel can work without the heat stress of a sauna. A sauna may give off infrared energy without the wavelength, dose or intensity used in PBM research. So a sauna with red-colored lights isn't automatically a red light therapy device.

## 2. Red Light Therapy Is About Dose

PBM uses non-ionizing light, usually in the red and near-infrared range, at defined doses. The FDA has published guidance for PBM devices that go through its 510(k) clearance process for specific uses. Clearance applies to a particular device and claim, not to red light in general.

If you're shopping, the specs that matter are wavelength, irradiance (light intensity), treatment distance and session length.

## 3. An Infrared Sauna Is About Heat

An infrared sauna uses infrared heaters to warm you at lower air temperatures than many traditional saunas. Your body responds mainly to the heat. Your skin warms, you sweat, your blood vessels widen and your heart works harder.

Mayo Clinic notes that some research suggests infrared saunas may help with certain chronic conditions. It also says larger and better studies are needed. Our [infrared vs traditional sauna](/longevity-recovery/infrared-sauna-vs-traditional-sauna/) comparison covers the sauna research in more detail.

## 4. Red Light Has More Skin Research

Red light and LED therapy have a research base focused on skin. A 2026 review of at-home LED devices for facial aging found 8 studies with 365 participants. Across them, users saw significant improvements in wrinkles, texture, elasticity and fine lines around the eyes. Side effects were rare, mild and short-lived.

That's encouraging, but it's specific to the devices tested, and the evidence is still small. Infrared sauna research isn't really about facial skin. Claims that sweating cleans toxins out of your skin aren't a substitute for evidence.

## 5. Recovery Evidence Depends on the Type of Red Light

Results differ sharply by how the light is applied.

- **Local red light:** a 2015 meta-analysis of 13 good-quality trials found that applying light to muscles before exercise improved some performance measures.
- **Whole-body red light:** a 2025 review found just 5 studies with 105 active people. It found no benefit for exercise recovery or performance, though 2 studies reported better sleep quality.

So the broad claim that red light speeds recovery is too simple. Infrared sauna research belongs to the wider heat-therapy literature, which tests a different mechanism. Our [sauna vs hot tub](/longevity-recovery/sauna-vs-hot-tub/) comparison covers what that heat research shows.

## 6. Heat Has the Stronger Link to Heart Health

For the heart and blood vessels, sauna and heat research is the more relevant evidence. Heat widens blood vessels and works the heart, which isn't the main target of red light therapy. Some sauna studies report favorable links or results, but many are small or observational.

Those sauna findings shouldn't be applied to red light therapy just because both involve infrared somewhere on the spectrum.

## 7. Combining Them Isn't Proven

Some spas and home devices combine both. There isn't strong evidence for any specific combination, or for doing one before the other. If you use both, treat them as two separate exposures with their own settings, and watch your heat tolerance, skin response and total session time.

### How to Compare Home Devices

For a red light device, check:

- wavelength and irradiance at a stated distance
- treatment area and recommended dose
- eye-safety instructions
- FDA clearance, if the seller makes a medical claim

For an infrared sauna, check:

- heater type and temperature range
- electrical and installation requirements
- cabin size and ventilation
- warranty and independent safety certification

A small red light panel and a full sauna cabin aren't comparable purchases, and prices span from cheap gadgets to thousands of dollars. Compare products that solve the same problem. Then count the full cost, including electricity, replacement parts and how often you'll use it. The [sauna FAQ](/faq/longevity-recovery/sauna/) covers safe session length and temperature.

## Which Should You Choose?

| If your goal is... | Consider |
|---|---|
| A specific skin use backed by a device's evidence | Red light therapy |
| Targeted treatment of one area | Red light therapy |
| A whole-body heat session | Infrared sauna |
| Relaxation through warmth and sweating | Infrared sauna |

## FAQs

### Is Red Light Therapy the Same as an Infrared Sauna?

No. Red light therapy delivers selected wavelengths at set doses, while an infrared sauna is mainly a heat exposure. Near-infrared light can show up in both, but sharing a wavelength doesn't make the dose or the goal the same.

### Does an Infrared Sauna Include Red Light Therapy?

Some saunas add red or near-infrared LEDs, but that doesn't make the sauna a red light therapy device. To judge a red light claim, look for the wavelength, irradiance, dose and evidence for that specific light system, not the color of the cabin lights.

### Can Red Light Therapy Make You Sweat?

A properly designed red light session doesn't need sweating to work. Some large panels feel warm, but heat isn't how the therapy is meant to act. If a device mainly heats your whole body, it shouldn't be described as standard red light therapy.

### Which Is Safer for Daily Use?

There's no universal daily rule. Safety depends on the dose, the device instructions, your heat tolerance, medicines, health conditions and the area treated. More exposure isn't automatically better for either light or heat therapy.
