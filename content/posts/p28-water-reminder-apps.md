# 7 Water Reminder Apps Compared by Tracking, Alerts and Apple Watch Support

Water reminder apps solve a small problem. They help you remember to drink, and they keep a simple log if that helps you stay consistent. So the features that matter are practical ones: reminder timing, quick logging, bottle presets, watch access, widgets and whether the free version gets annoying after a few days.

These apps are less reliable as judges of exactly how much you need. Fluid needs vary with diet, activity, climate, pregnancy, illness, medication and health conditions. Total water also includes what you get from food, not just drinks.

We compared seven apps by reminder design, logging, Apple Watch support, free limits and price. We checked US App Store listings in early November 2026. We don't treat an app's daily target as medical advice.

## A Note About Hydration Targets

Several apps calculate a goal from your weight, sex, activity or the weather. Those numbers can work as prompts, but real fluid needs vary far more.

For reference, the National Academies set a daily Adequate Intake of 3.7 liters of total water for men and 2.7 liters for women, including water from food. Our [Water Intake Calculator](/water-intake-calculator/) explains how that splits between drinks and food.

For most healthy adults, an app works best as a reminder and logging tool. If you have a heart, kidney or other condition that affects fluids, follow your clinician's advice rather than an app's generic target.

## 1. WaterMinder: Best for Detailed Tracking and Apple Watch

![WaterMinder App Store artwork with hydration reminders on iPhone and Apple Watch](shot-waterminder-app-store "Screenshot: WaterMinder listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

WaterMinder tracks water and other drinks, with custom reminders, Apple Watch support, widgets and Apple Health sync. You can set your own target instead of relying on a calculated one.

Premium options in the US App Store include **$2.99 a month** and yearly plans listed between **$14.99 and $29.99**, depending on the offer.

### Where It Fits

It's one of the more polished choices if you'd rather log from your wrist than open your phone after every drink.

**Best fit:** Apple Watch users and people who log several drink types.

## 2. Waterllama: Best for Gamified Reminders

![Waterllama App Store artwork with ratings, press logos and an Apple Design Award finalist badge](shot-waterllama-app-store "Screenshot: Waterllama listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Waterllama turns hydration into a character-based habit game. The free version gives you tracking, reminders and some drink types. Premium adds a much bigger character library, more drinks, custom beverages and extra challenges.

It offers monthly and annual subscriptions plus a one-time purchase. Prices shown in the App Store vary by offer, so check the live checkout.

### Where It Fits

The visual rewards help if a plain number is easy for you to ignore. If you'd prefer a quiet, practical log, Waterlogged or WaterMinder may suit you better.

**Best fit:** People motivated by playful progress.

## 3. Waterlogged: Best for Simple Reminders and a One-Time Upgrade

![Waterlogged app showing a water bottle filling toward a 64 ounce daily goal](shot-waterlogged-app-store "Screenshot: Waterlogged listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Waterlogged focuses on fast logging, with saved bottles, cups and custom sizes. You set your own goal and see basic progress without any game elements.

Premium adds smarter and interval reminders, plus Apple Health and Fitbit integrations. The listing describes Premium as a **one-time purchase, not a subscription**.

**Best fit:** People who want a simple tracker and dislike recurring fees.

## 4. Plant Nanny: Best If You Want a Habit Game

![Plant Nanny App Store artwork headlined Drink Water, Grow Cute Plants](shot-plant-nanny-app-store "Screenshot: Plant Nanny listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Plant Nanny links every drink you log to the growth of a virtual plant. It includes reminders, history and progress views. The paid SPARKFUL membership adds more plants and features.

US App Store options include **$7.99 a month and $69.99 a year**, with other promotional prices at times.

### Where It Fits

This is about motivation more than precision. If keeping a virtual plant alive makes the reminder harder to ignore, the design is doing its job.

**Best fit:** People who respond to visual rewards and streaks.

## 5. My Water: Best for Basic Reminders and Graphs

![My Water App Store artwork calling it the number one water tracker](shot-my-water-app-store "Screenshot: My Water listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

My Water offers reminders, metric and ounce units, graphs, Apple Watch support and Apple Health sync. It suggests a daily intake but notes that activity, climate and health can change what you need.

Premium adds more drink types, stats and customization. US App Store options include **$7.99 a month and $39.99 a year**.

**Best fit:** People who want a familiar reminder-and-chart layout.

## 6. Aqualert: Best for Simple Scheduled Reminders

![Aqualert app daily goal screen with a progress ring and illustrated character](shot-aqualert-app-store "Screenshot: Aqualert listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Aqualert centers on scheduled drink alerts, daily goals and history. You choose the reminder interval and the amount each drink counts for.

The free version shows ads. Removing them costs **$0.99 for a month or $7.99 for a year**.

### Where It Fits

Aqualert is right if you mainly want your phone to say "drink something now" at set times. It's lighter than apps built around characters, big drink libraries or dashboards.

**Best fit:** Basic scheduled nudges.

## 7. Water Reminder: Best for Drink Nutrition in Apple Health

![Water Reminder app showing 52 of 96 ounces logged with drink type tiles](shot-water-reminder-app-store "Screenshot: Water Reminder listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Water Reminder by VGFIT logs your drinks and can send water, caffeine, calories, protein, fat, carbs and sugar to Apple Health for supported beverages.

The free version covers basic water tracking. Premium adds more drink types, sounds, widgets and Watch features, with monthly and annual plans.

### Where It Fits

It's most useful if you want drink logging to feed a fuller Apple Health record. If you only care about plain water, you may not need the extra nutrition fields.

**Best fit:** Apple Health users tracking more than water.

## FAQs

### What Is the Best Free Water Reminder App?

Waterllama, Waterlogged, Plant Nanny, My Water and Aqualert all offer free tracking or reminders, with different paid limits. Waterlogged stands out if you'd eventually prefer a one-time upgrade to a subscription.

### Can a Water App Tell Me Exactly How Much to Drink?

No. No app can set an exact need from a few profile details, because fluid needs vary with activity, climate, food, pregnancy, illness, medication and health conditions. Treat app targets as rough estimates unless a clinician has given you a specific plan.

### Which Water App Works With Apple Watch?

WaterMinder and Waterllama both advertise Apple Watch support, and My Water supports it too. Water Reminder includes Watch features in its paid plan. Check compatibility with your watchOS version before subscribing.

### Is Drinking More Water Always Better?

No. Drinking far too much, especially in a short time, can be harmful, and some health conditions affect fluid balance. The goal is enough fluid for you, not as much as possible.

### Do Coffee and Tea Count Toward Hydration?

Yes. Drinks such as coffee and tea contribute to your fluid intake. Some apps apply their own "hydration percentages" to different drinks, but those are company formulas, not medical standards.
