# 7 Sleep Apps for Apple Watch Compared by Tracking, Smart Alarms and Cost

Apple Watch already tracks sleep, so third-party sleep apps for Apple Watch have to earn their place. The good reasons to add one are specific: deeper trends, automatic detection, smart alarms, nap tracking, sound recording, more heart rate context or a different view of your Apple Health data.

That makes this comparison more focused than "which app shows the most charts?" Some apps run their own analysis on Watch data. Others add an alarm or audio recording from your iPhone. A few are best thought of as a new dashboard on top of Apple's own sleep stages.

We checked US App Store listings in early November 2026. This is a feature comparison, not a validation study. Consumer sleep apps help you spot patterns, but their stage labels and scores aren't clinical sleep testing. If you mainly want calming audio at bedtime, our [meditation apps for sleep comparison](/mind-sleep/meditation-apps-for-sleep-compared/) is the better place to start.

## Check Apple's Built-In Sleep Data First

Apple's Health and Sleep features already show sleep time and stage estimates on supported watches. Apple labels the stages Awake, REM, Core and Deep. If you only want a nightly total and a simple stage chart, another subscription may add clutter without adding much value.

A third-party app earns its keep when you want something Apple doesn't stress. That might be a smart alarm, richer trends, automatic nap handling or a long-term dashboard you prefer. For what those stage labels mean, see our [sleep stages FAQ](/faq/mind-sleep/sleep-stages/).

## 1. AutoSleep: Best One-Time Purchase for Detailed Data

![AutoSleep App Store artwork with an illustrated sleeper and a five-star rating](shot-autosleep-app-store "Screenshot: AutoSleep listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

AutoSleep is built specifically for Apple Watch and costs **$9.99 as a one-time purchase**. The developer says there are no subscriptions, no ad plugins, no third-party analytics code and no data upload from the app.

It can use Apple's sleep stages and show sleep time, heart rate, consistency, breathing rate, wrist temperature and other metrics your watch supports.

### Why It Stands Out

The one-time price is the main difference. If you plan to use a sleep app for years, paying once may beat another monthly bill.

The screens are also packed with data. That suits people who enjoy digging into numbers. It can feel like too much if your only question is "how long did I sleep?"

**Best fit:** Data-minded Watch users who prefer to pay once.

## 2. Pillow: Best for Watch Tracking Plus a Smart Alarm

![Pillow app sleep chart under the headline Sleeping better, made simple](shot-pillow-app-store "Screenshot: Pillow listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Pillow analyzes sleep automatically when you wear your Apple Watch. It also has a smart alarm that aims to wake you during lighter sleep. You'll see sleep stages, a sleep score and heart rate analysis.

Audio features can record snoring and sleep talking, which the developer says is processed on your device. The app is free to download. In the App Store, Premium options include **$19.99 a month and $39.99 a year**.

**Best fit:** Watch users who specifically want a smart alarm with their sleep analysis.

## 3. SleepWatch: Best for Coaching and Trend Reports

![SleepWatch App Store artwork listing sleep tracking, sound recording and smart alarm features](shot-sleepwatch-app-store "Screenshot: SleepWatch listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

SleepWatch pairs automatic Watch tracking with reports, trends and optional coaching. Depending on your hardware and permissions, it can show sleep time, heart rate, disruptions, rhythm and other data from Apple Health.

Premium adds personal coaching, fuller reports and a smart alarm. The US App Store lists **$4.99 a month or $39.99 a year**.

### Why It Stands Out

SleepWatch tries to turn data into suggestions, not just charts. That helps if you want nudges to change habits. If your routine is already simple, the lighter Sleep++ may suit you better.

**Best fit:** People who want trends and coaching on top of Watch data.

## 4. Sleep++: Best Simple Free Tracker

![Sleep++ app showing nightly sleep stage bars for three nights](shot-sleep-plus-plus-app-store "Screenshot: Sleep++ listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Sleep++ focuses on automatic Apple Watch tracking and a morning report. It works with Apple Health and can write sleep data back into it.

The app is free. The App Store shows an optional **$1.99 support purchase** instead of a subscription.

### Why It Stands Out

It's ideal if you just want to wear the watch, sleep and check a report in the morning. It doesn't try to become a full wellness platform.

**Best fit:** People who want a lightweight tracker with no subscription.

## 5. Sleep Cycle: Best for Smart Alarms and Sound Analysis

![Sleep Cycle App Store artwork headlined Sleep Better Tonight with a smart alarm feature list](shot-sleep-cycle-app-store "Screenshot: Sleep Cycle listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Sleep Cycle is better known for its smart alarm and sound-based analysis than for Watch tracking. It analyzes sleep from your phone, records sounds such as snoring or coughing and connects with Apple Health.

Premium adds deeper analysis, sleep content and data export. The App Store shows several Premium prices and offers rather than one standard rate.

### Why It Belongs Here

Watch owners may already have stage data. Sleep Cycle adds a different routine built around alarms, sounds and bedtime habits. That makes it a companion to AutoSleep rather than a replacement.

**Best fit:** People who care more about a gentle wake-up and sound tracking than dense dashboards.

## 6. NapBot: Best for Automatic Tracking and Clean Trends

![NapBot app on Apple Watch showing a night of deep, light and awake sleep](shot-napbot-app-store "Screenshot: NapBot listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

NapBot analyzes sleep automatically on iPhone and Apple Watch, using on-device machine learning. It shows sleep phases, sleeping heart rate and how much noise you were exposed to.

The free version covers the core tracking. Pro adds full history and trend analysis for **$4.99 a month or $29.99 a year**.

**Best fit:** Watch users who want automatic tracking with a focused trend view.

## 7. REM: Best for Nap Detection and Snoring Features

![REM app dashboard with heart rate, time asleep and deep sleep tiles](shot-rem-app-store "Screenshot: REM listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

REM offers automatic Watch tracking, stage estimates, a sleep score, heart rate analysis, nap detection, trends, snore detection and a smart alarm.

It runs on a Premium model with a free trial. App Store options include **$4.99 a month and $29.99 a year**, plus longer plans.

### Why It Stands Out

Nap detection sets it apart for people whose sleep doesn't happen in one overnight block. It also pairs Watch data with optional sound detection on your iPhone.

**Best fit:** People who want nap tracking and more depth than a minimal tracker.

## FAQs

### Does Apple Watch Track Sleep Without an App?

Yes. Supported Apple Watch models record sleep and save it to Apple Health. Third-party apps help when you want different analysis, long-term trends, smart alarms, sound recording or a dashboard you prefer.

### Which Apple Watch Sleep App Has No Subscription?

AutoSleep is a one-time purchase, currently $9.99. Sleep++ is free with an optional support purchase. Those are the clearest picks here if you want to avoid another recurring bill.

### Which Sleep App Has the Best Smart Alarm?

Pillow, SleepWatch, Sleep Cycle and REM all advertise a smart alarm. We haven't tested how accurately they time your wake-up, so choose based on the wider tracking features and price instead.

### Can Apple Watch Detect Sleep Apnea With These Apps?

Some apps flag patterns using breathing, blood oxygen or sound data, but that isn't a diagnosis. Apple offers a separate regulated sleep apnea notification feature on eligible hardware in supported regions. Loud snoring, witnessed breathing pauses or heavy daytime sleepiness need a clinical assessment.

### Are Apple Watch Sleep Stages Accurate?

They're estimates. Wearables use signals such as movement and heart rate instead of the brain wave measurements used in a sleep lab. They're useful for broad patterns, but don't read individual stage numbers as lab-grade results.
