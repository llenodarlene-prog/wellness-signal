# Best Body Composition Scales: What They Measure and How Accurate They Are

The best body composition scales measure your weight very accurately and estimate everything else. That's the most important thing to know before you buy one. Smart scales use bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA): they send a small, safe electrical current through your body and use the result to predict body fat and muscle. The weight is measured. The body fat percentage is a prediction.

We compared three scales using the makers' official product and support pages, checked in November 2026, and looked at two peer-reviewed studies on accuracy. This is a research-based comparison, not a hands-on test.

## What the Research Says About Accuracy

Two studies explain why you should treat body fat readings as trends, not exact facts.

A 2026 systematic review of 12 studies compared BIA devices with a four-compartment model, a reference method. Average body fat errors ranged from 3.5 percentage points too low to 4.4 points too high. But for any one person, the limits of agreement typically spanned 15 to 20 percentage points. In plain terms, your reading could be well off even when the average across many people looks close. The authors concluded BIA was not equivalent at the individual level.

A 2021 study in France tested three smart scales against DEXA scans in hospital patients. Weight was very close, with median errors of 0 to 0.3 kg. Fat mass errors were much larger, from 2.2 to 4.4 kg. The authors concluded smart scales are not accurate for body composition and shouldn't replace DEXA in patient care.

So buy a body composition scale to spot changes over time, not to get a lab-grade number.

## When a Basic Scale Is Enough

If you mainly want to track your weight, a basic digital scale may be all you need. The 2021 study found weight readings were far more reliable than fat or muscle estimates. A plain tape measure for your waist adds useful context at no extra cost, and the [Waist-to-Height Ratio Calculator](/waist-to-height-ratio-calculator/) turns that into a simple screening number.

## How to Get More Consistent Readings

- **Same time, same conditions.** Weigh yourself at the same time of day, such as after waking and using the bathroom.
- **Same scale, same spot.** Use a hard, level floor, not carpet.
- **Bare feet, dry skin.** Electrodes need good contact.
- **Don't mix brands.** Body fat from one brand's scale isn't comparable with another's.
- **Look at weeks, not days.** Hydration, meals and exercise shift daily readings.

If you have a pacemaker or another implanted medical device, or you're pregnant, check your scale's manual before using body composition mode.

## FAQs

### Are Body Composition Scales Accurate?

For weight, yes; for body fat, not precisely. A 2026 systematic review found individual BIA body fat readings could differ from a reference method across a range of 15 to 20 percentage points. They're most useful for tracking trends on the same scale over time.

### What Is the Best Body Composition Scale?

It depends on your budget and needs. Withings Body Scan gives the most detail, with segmental estimates and an ECG, at $499.95. Withings Body Smart at $129.95 suits steady tracking, and the RENPHO Elis 1 is a low-cost starting point.

### Is a Smart Scale as Good as a DEXA Scan?

No. A 2021 study comparing three smart scales with DEXA found fat mass errors of 2.2 to 4.4 kg. The authors concluded smart scales shouldn't replace DEXA in patient care.

### Who Shouldn't Use a Body Composition Scale?

Check the manual first if you have a pacemaker or another implanted electronic device, or if you're pregnant. When in doubt, use weight-only mode and ask your doctor.
