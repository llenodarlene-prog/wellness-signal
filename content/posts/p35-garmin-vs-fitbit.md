# Garmin vs Fitbit: 8 Differences in Training, Sleep and Subscriptions

Garmin and Fitbit now overlap on steps, heart rate, sleep and readiness scores. They still come from different places. Garmin builds a broad sports and GPS lineup. Fitbit has moved into Google's health platform, with a simpler, everyday-wellness feel.

The Garmin vs Fitbit picture also changed in 2026. Google's store now says "the Fitbit app is now the Google Health app," with a paid Google Health Premium tier on top. Many older comparisons haven't caught up.

Here's how the two compare today, based on official pages and independent accuracy studies.

## Garmin vs Fitbit at a Glance

| Factor | Garmin | Fitbit (Google Health) |
|---|---|---|
| Core focus | Sports, GPS and outdoor training | Everyday health and fitness |
| Device range | Wide, from trackers to multisport watches | Smaller tracker range plus Pixel Watch |
| Training detail on the device | Deep | Simpler |
| Readiness score | Training Readiness on compatible devices | Readiness in Google Health |
| App | Garmin Connect | Google Health |
| Paid tier | Optional Garmin Connect+ | Google Health Premium, from $9.99/month |
| Best fit | Structured, sport-specific training | Simpler wellness tracking |

## 1. Garmin Goes Deeper on Training

Garmin's strength is sport-specific structure. Many of its watches support workout targets, pace data, training load, recovery time, heart rate zones and detailed activity profiles. Higher-end models add maps and navigation.

Fitbit has always focused on clear daily health summaries. Under Google Health, that now means activity, readiness, sleep, health metrics and coaching in one consumer app.

## 2. Garmin's Readiness Score Is Built Around Training

Garmin says the main drivers of Training Readiness are how well you slept last night and how much recovery your recent activities still need. Training load, HRV status, recent stress and your sleep before last night also feed in.

Garmin also offers Body Battery, an energy estimate that rises and falls through the day. Both are Garmin's own summaries, though. They're estimates, not direct measures of whether a workout is safe or ideal.

## 3. Fitbit Now Lives Inside Google Health

With a paired Fitbit or Pixel Watch, Google Health tracks steps, calories, distance, cardio load, readiness and exercise. It shows a sleep score and sleep stages. It also tracks heart rate, HRV, breathing rate, skin temperature and SpO2.

Google Health Premium starts at $9.99 a month and adds personalized coaching built with Google's Gemini AI. New users get a 3-month free trial.

![Fitbit app Today screen showing 8,560 steps and scores for stress management, sleep and zone minutes](shot-google-health-app-store "Image: Google Health (Fitbit) on the App Store, November 2026.")

## 4. Heart Rate Accuracy Depends on the Device

There's no brand-wide guarantee. A 2026 study tested 10 wearables on 45 adults during rest, walking and other activities, in hot, cold and neutral rooms. The Fitbit Charge 6 and Google Pixel Watch 2 were the most accurate. The Fitbit Inspire 3 was among the least. Irregular movement cut accuracy for every device.

A 2025 study looked at fast heart rate changes. The Fitbit Charge 5 and Sense 2 were the most accurate overall, while the Garmin Vivosmart 4 was steadier during transitions. Every wrist device got worse when heart rate changed quickly.

Accuracy depends on the model and the activity, not the logo. A chest strap is still the better choice when interval heart rate needs to be precise.

## 5. Treat Calorie Counts as Estimates

Neither brand measures calories directly. Wearables estimate energy use with their own formulas, based on sensor data and your profile. Use the numbers to spot trends, not as an exact tally of how much food a workout earned.

## 6. Sleep Stages Are Rough for Both

A lab study tested six wearables on 53 adults against polysomnography, the clinical sleep test. Telling sleep from wake was fairly accurate, at 89% agreement for the Garmin Forerunner 245. Getting specific sleep stages right was much weaker, at 50% for the same Garmin. That's a limit of wrist wearables in general.

Google Health emphasizes sleep score, duration, schedule, consistency and stages. Garmin links sleep to its training and recovery features. For phone-based options, see our [sleep tracking apps for iPhone](/mind-sleep/sleep-tracking-apps-for-iphone/) comparison.

## 7. Garmin Usually Suits Runners Better

It isn't just GPS. Garmin's lineup is built around structured training, pace and lap data, chest strap support, race metrics and, on many models, routes and navigation. Fitbit records runs and cardio load, but it's less focused on deep sport analysis.

If you want something simpler, Google Health may feel easier. Garmin handles everyday tracking too, but its app and watches can feel busy if you don't care about training metrics.

## 8. Both Now Have Optional Paid Tiers

This is where the two have moved closer. Garmin Connect still gives you plenty of core features with no subscription. Garmin now also sells Connect+, an optional add-on with AI insights, live activity stats, extra training guidance and nutrition logging.

Google Health Premium starts at $9.99 a month. Many Google Health features work with a Fitbit or Pixel Watch without it. So compare what you'd actually use, rather than assuming Garmin has no subscription or Fitbit needs one.

## FAQs

### Is Garmin More Accurate Than Fitbit?

Not across every measure or device. Independent studies show accuracy changes by model, activity and signal. In a 2026 heart rate study, the Fitbit Charge 6 was among the most accurate of 10 devices. Garmin offers more sports features, but that's different from being more accurate.

### Does Garmin Require a Subscription Now?

No. Many core Garmin Connect features work without one. Garmin now offers Connect+ as an optional paid add-on with AI insights and extra training tools. Older comparisons often say Garmin has no premium tier at all, which is out of date.

### Is Fitbit Premium Still Called Fitbit Premium?

Google's US store now says the Fitbit app is the Google Health app, and the paid tier is called Google Health Premium. It starts at $9.99 a month. Older references to Fitbit Premium may now be out of date.

### Which Is Better for Sleep, Garmin or Fitbit?

Both are useful for trends, but neither replaces a clinical sleep study. In lab testing, wearables were far better at spotting sleep versus wake than at naming stages. Garmin ties sleep to training and recovery, while Google Health focuses on sleep summaries and coaching.
