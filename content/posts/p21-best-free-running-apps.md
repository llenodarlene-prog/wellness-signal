# 7 Best Free Running Apps Compared by GPS, Training Plans and Coaching

The best free running apps don't all solve the same problem. One runner just wants a clean record of distance and pace. Another needs audio cues for intervals, a beginner plan, Apple Watch tracking or a community that keeps them showing up.

Many roundups treat every running app as the same product with a different logo. This one focuses on what you can use without paying, what sits behind a subscription and which kind of runner each app suits.

We checked US App Store listings and official product pages in early November 2026. This is a desk-based feature comparison. We didn't test GPS accuracy, coaching quality or battery use on specific phones and watches. For a wider look at no-cost workout platforms, see our [free fitness apps comparison](/fitness/best-free-fitness-apps/).

## Free Running Apps at a Glance

Here's how the seven apps compare on the features most runners ask about first.

| App | Best for | Useful free access | Training plans | Apple Watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nike Run Club | Guided runs and race plans | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Strava | Social tracking and a multi-sport history | Yes | Advanced tools are paid | Yes |
| ASICS Runkeeper | GPS tracking with audio updates | Yes | Some plans are premium | Yes |
| adidas Running | Multi-sport tracking and challenges | Yes | Varies by current plan | Yes |

"Free" here means an ongoing free experience you can keep using, not just a trial. Features and access can vary by region.

## How We Compared These Apps

Five practical questions shaped the comparison:

1. **Can you record a normal run without paying?**
2. **Does the free version give feedback beyond a map?**
3. **Are training plans, intervals or guided runs included?**
4. **Does it work with Apple Watch or other common devices?**
5. **What becomes paid once you want deeper analysis, routes or coaching?**

Search results often mix free trackers, paid coaching services and device companion apps. The useful question isn't which app has the longest feature list. It's what's left once the sign-up screen disappears.

## 1. Nike Run Club: Guided Runs Without a Subscription

![Nike Run Club App Store artwork of two runners with the words Run, Leave Yourself in the Dust](shot-nike-run-club-app-store "Screenshot: Nike Run Club listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Nike Run Club stands out because its core experience isn't built around a paid tier. It combines GPS tracking with Guided Runs, coaching prompts and plans for goals from getting started to 5K, 10K, half marathon and marathon.

It tracks pace, distance, route, elevation, heart rate and cadence where supported. Apple Watch can record runs on its own, and Apple Health can store your workouts.

### Where the Free Access Is Strongest

Nike Run Club is one of the clearest choices if you want coaching without paying for a training plan. Guided Runs also make it friendlier for beginners than a bare GPS tracker.

The trade-off is focus. It's built for running, so it's less useful if you want one feed for cycling, hiking and lifting too.

**Best fit:** Beginners, fans of audio guidance and anyone training for common race distances without another subscription.

**Cost:** Free.

## 2. Strava: Social Motivation and a Multi-Sport History

![Strava app on iPhone and Apple Watch under an Apple Watch App of the Year badge](shot-strava-app-store "Screenshot: Strava listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Strava is broader than a running app. It records runs alongside rides, hikes, strength sessions and dozens of other activities. Then it puts that history inside a social network where you follow athletes, share workouts and join challenges.

The free tier covers activity recording, the feed, basic stats and community features. A subscription adds route planning, deeper analysis and training tools. The US App Store lists **$11.99 a month and $79.99 a year**, plus bundles and family plans.

### Where It Stands Out

Strava is strongest when the social side keeps you going. It also suits runners who already use Garmin, Apple Watch or Fitbit and want one home for every activity.

If you want a detailed free race plan, Nike Run Club or Decathlon Coach will give you more right away. Strava's free version shines as a record and a community, not as a coach.

**Best fit:** Runners who train socially or want one history across sports.

## 3. ASICS Runkeeper: Audio Stats and Straightforward GPS Tracking

![ASICS Runkeeper app dashboard with weekly running distance chart](shot-asics-runkeeper-app-store "Screenshot: ASICS Runkeeper listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Runkeeper follows a familiar pattern. You start a run, record it with GPS, hear your stats along the way and review progress afterward. It also records walks, rides and hikes.

The free version handles regular tracking. Runkeeper Go adds premium training and analysis. App Store options include **$9.99 a month and $39.99 a year**.

### Where It Stands Out

Audio updates help if you want pace, time or distance without looking at your phone. Apple Watch support allows phone-free tracking with supported setups.

The free tier is less compelling if you mainly want a detailed race plan, because Runkeeper keeps more of its tailored features inside Go.

**Best fit:** Runners who want familiar GPS tracking with spoken feedback.

## 4. adidas Running: For Runners Who Track Other Sports Too

![adidas Running app showing a 01:25:12 run with a route map](shot-adidas-running-app-store "Screenshot: adidas Running listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

adidas Running tracks running, walking, cycling and many other sports. You get GPS, live stats, activity summaries and community challenges. The current listing presents it as a broad fitness tracker rather than a running-only tool.

The app stays usable without paying. Some advanced or personalized features depend on the current plan and region. adidas has changed its membership setup over the years, so check the upgrade screen instead of trusting older Runtastic prices you see quoted online.

### Where It Stands Out

It's a practical pick if running sits alongside cycling, walking and other activities. Challenges and community features sit right next to tracking.

If you want an explicit free marathon plan, Nike Run Club is easier to judge, because its plan access is clearer.

**Best fit:** Multi-sport users who still run most often.

## 5. Map My Run: Route and Workout Tracking

![Map My Run app showing a 3.02 mile route on a map with a pause button](shot-map-my-run-app-store "Screenshot: Map My Run listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Map My Run records distance, pace, elevation, calories and routes. It also logs a wide range of other workouts. You get audio feedback during runs, and it connects with supported wearables and heart rate devices.

The core tracker is free. Coaching and premium features sit in its MVP plan, which the App Store lists at **$9.99 a month or $39.99 a year**, with other offers at times.

### Where It Stands Out

Map My Run suits runners who like reviewing routes and want one log for outdoor runs, treadmill sessions and other workouts.

Its long activity list matters less now that most rivals cover many sports. Choose it for the workflow and integrations, not the list itself.

**Best fit:** Runners who want a conventional GPS log with route features.

## 6. Decathlon Coach: Free Structured Training Across Sports

![Decathlon Coach app listing running, walking and Pilates and yoga programs](shot-decathlon-coach-app-store "Screenshot: Decathlon Coach listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Decathlon Coach says outright that its training programs are free. It covers running, trail running, walking, strength training, Pilates, yoga and more. Programs come with voice guidance and can be matched to your level and goal.

The listing advertises more than 250 coaching programs and sessions, plus Apple Watch support for tracking.

### Where It Stands Out

It's a strong option if you want structured sessions without paying for a running coach. The wider sports catalog can also replace a separate cross-training app.

It may feel less running-specific than Nike Run Club. The amount of free structured content is why it's here.

**Best fit:** Runners who want free programs and also cross-train.

**Cost:** Free core programs.

## 7. Sports Tracker: Outdoor Routes and Heatmaps

![Sports Tracker App Store artwork of a night runner headlined The Tracker for All Your Sports](shot-sports-tracker-app-store "Screenshot: Sports Tracker listing on the Apple App Store, retrieved November 2026.")

Sports Tracker combines GPS recording with route discovery, heatmaps and GPX route files. It tracks running, hiking, cycling and many other activities, with weekly, monthly and yearly summaries.

Its Apple Watch app records activities and shows maps, route navigation and workout stats on your wrist. Premium is optional, listed in the App Store at **$4.49 a month or $29.99 a year**.

### Where It Stands Out

This appeals more to outdoor runners who care about routes than to someone who wants a coach talking them through intervals. Heatmaps and saved routes help when you're exploring somewhere new.

**Best fit:** Outdoor runners who want route discovery and broader adventure tracking.

## Which Free Running App Should You Choose?

Start with the job you need the app to do. Then try the matching app for a few runs.

| If you mainly want... | Start with... |
|---|---|
| Guided runs and free race plans | Nike Run Club |
| Social motivation and cross-sport history | Strava |
| Spoken pace and distance updates | ASICS Runkeeper |
| Multi-sport tracking and challenges | adidas Running |
| Familiar GPS and route logging | Map My Run |
| Free structured programs across sports | Decathlon Coach |
| Heatmaps, GPX routes and exploring | Sports Tracker |

A paid tier can add useful analysis, but it won't make your GPS record better on its own. Start with the free workflow you'll use every week. Pay only when a missing feature is specific enough to justify it.

## FAQs

### What Is the Best Completely Free Running App?

Nike Run Club and Decathlon Coach are the clearest picks for real coaching without a standard subscription. Choose Nike for running-specific Guided Runs, or Decathlon for a wider range of sport programs.

### Is Strava Free for Running?

Yes. Strava's free tier records runs and lets you take part in its social network. A subscription adds deeper analysis, route tools and training features, but the free version works well if you mainly want recording and community.

### Which Free Running App Is Best for Beginners?

Nike Run Club is especially beginner-friendly, because its Guided Runs explain pace and effort as you go. Decathlon Coach offers structured programs by level, and Runkeeper is a simple tracker with audio feedback.

### Can I Run Without Carrying My Phone?

Often, yes. Nike Run Club, Strava, Runkeeper, Decathlon Coach and Sports Tracker all document Apple Watch support. Phone-free features vary by app and watch model, so test the setup before race day.

### Are Free Running Apps Accurate?

They give useful distance and pace estimates, but accuracy depends on your device, signal, surroundings and the app itself. We didn't run controlled GPS tests, so we don't claim any one app is more accurate than another.
