# 7 Mindbody Alternatives Compared for Fitness and Wellness Businesses

Mindbody is one of the best-known software platforms in fitness and wellness, but it is not the only way to manage bookings, memberships, payments, marketing, and client records. Studio owners often compare alternatives because pricing models, payment fees, marketplace exposure, and multi-location needs can differ substantially.

Choosing between Mindbody alternatives depends less on which platform has the longest feature list and more on how a business actually operates. A solo appointment-based provider, a Pilates studio with reformers, and a multi-location fitness brand may need very different systems.

## Key Takeaways

- Mindbody starts at **$79 per month per location** in North America and Asia, according to its current pricing page.
- Vagaro publishes a lower US entry price, while WellnessLiving publishes tiered plans and PushPress offers a free core plan with higher payment-processing rates.
- Mariana Tek and Zenoti use quote-based pricing and target businesses with more complex operational needs.
- Square Appointments can be attractive for appointment-heavy businesses already using Square, but its structure is not a direct replacement for every class-based studio workflow.
- Mindbody's consumer app remains a meaningful differentiator because the company says participating businesses can reach more than **3 million monthly shoppers**.
- The US fitness market is large and still growing. The Health & Fitness Association reported **81 million fitness-facility members in 2025**, while boutique studios led year-over-year traffic growth in the second quarter of 2026.
- The best comparison should include software fees, payment processing, add-ons, migration, and marketplace commissions rather than monthly subscription price alone.

## How We Compared Mindbody Alternatives

This comparison uses current official pricing and product pages where available. We reviewed published subscription costs, payment processing, booking capabilities, marketing features, consumer discovery, branded apps, and support for single or multiple locations.

We did not rank platforms by affiliate availability. A platform is included because it serves a relevant fitness, wellness, beauty, or appointment-based business model, not because it offers a referral programme.

## Mindbody as the Baseline

Mindbody currently advertises pricing from **$79 per month per location**, the entry point of its [subscription and payments business model](/fitness/mindbody-business-model/). Its Starter plan includes business management tools, integrated payments, booking widgets, a business website, a Mindbody app listing, and basic reporting.

Higher tiers add analytics, resource management, marketing, lead-management tools, and other features. Mindbody also states that its consumer app reaches more than **3 million monthly shoppers**, which is an important difference from software products focused mainly on back-office operations.

Mindbody says listing in its marketplace is free, while a **20% fee capped at $30** applies when the marketplace delivers a qualifying new customer and that person makes a first purchase. Existing clients and later purchases are described as commission-free under the published policy.

## 1. Vagaro

Vagaro publishes one of the lowest entry prices among the larger wellness-management platforms. Its current US pricing page lists a promotional rate of **$23.99 per month for the first six months** for one location, with a regular price shown as $30.

The base subscription includes calendars, memberships, packages, reporting, customer records, inventory, invoices, a booking widget, and a marketplace listing. Paid add-ons include a branded app, website builder, payroll, check-in tools, livestreaming, integrations, and data services.

### Where Vagaro Differs From Mindbody

Vagaro is particularly relevant for businesses combining appointments with classes, retail, beauty, or wellness services. Its pricing page is also more explicit about optional modules than many quote-led competitors.

Mindbody's strongest advantage remains its large fitness-and-wellness consumer marketplace. A business that depends heavily on marketplace discovery should compare actual new-client volume rather than assuming the lower software fee automatically produces a lower customer-acquisition cost.

## 2. WellnessLiving

WellnessLiving currently publishes Starter pricing at **$69 per month**, with higher plans adding broader marketing and management features. Its product set includes scheduling, staff tools, client apps, lead management, loyalty features, widgets, check-in tools, and reporting.

The platform also offers paid or higher-tier options for white-label apps and other business services. That makes it closer to Mindbody than a basic appointment scheduler, especially for studios that want booking, memberships, marketing, and member engagement in one environment.

### What Buyers Should Check

The relevant comparison is the full monthly invoice at the feature level the business actually needs. A $69 entry plan may not include the same combination of marketing, app, analytics, and multi-location capabilities required by a growing studio.

Businesses should also compare payment-processing fees and implementation terms. These charges can matter more over time than a modest difference in software subscription pricing.

## 3. PushPress

PushPress targets gyms and training facilities rather than the broader beauty-and-wellness market. Its current pricing includes a **Free** plan, **Pro at $159 per month**, and **Max at $229 per month**.

The Free plan removes monthly software fees but carries higher published payment-processing rates. PushPress lists credit-card processing at **4.99% + $0.30** on Free, compared with **2.89% + $0.30** on Pro and **2.75% + $0.30** on Max.

### Why Processing Rates Change the Comparison

A free software plan is not necessarily the cheapest option for a business with meaningful transaction volume. The difference in payment-processing rates can exceed the monthly subscription savings as sales increase.

PushPress is a stronger fit for gyms, CrossFit-style facilities, strength studios, and coaching businesses than for spas or appointment-heavy aesthetics businesses. Its plan design reflects that gym-focused operating model.

## 4. Square Appointments

Square Appointments provides scheduling and payment tools within the broader Square ecosystem. Its current US structure includes a **free plan**, a **$49 per location monthly Plus plan**, and a **$149 per location Premium plan**.

The product includes online booking, reminders, deposits, no-show policies, group classes, packages, and multi-location capabilities at higher tiers. Businesses can also connect the scheduling product with Square's payments, marketing, banking, and point-of-sale tools.

### Best for Appointment-Heavy Businesses

Square can be a practical option for smaller wellness providers that primarily sell appointments rather than complex class memberships. It is less directly comparable with Mindbody when a business needs a fitness marketplace, deeply specialised studio reporting, or complex membership workflows.

Its published pricing makes cost modelling easier. Businesses still need to include card-processing fees, paid marketing tools, and location-based subscription charges when calculating total cost.

## 5. Mariana Tek

Mariana Tek focuses specifically on boutique fitness and positions its platform for both single-location studios and larger brands. Its public pricing page does not publish dollar amounts and instead uses demo-led packages.

Every plan includes core functions such as multi-location management, unlimited class schedules and instructors, membership pricing, CRM, reporting, kiosk check-in, waivers, and transactional email. Higher packages add marketing automation, SMS, gamification, feedback tools, and additional growth services.

### Where Mariana Tek Stands Out

Mariana Tek's positioning is narrower than Mindbody's. It is designed around class-based boutique fitness such as Pilates, yoga, barre, cycling, HIIT, and boxing.

The company says its typical onboarding process lasts **five to eight weeks**. That is useful information for studios planning a migration, because switching software involves more than signing up for a new monthly plan.

## 6. Zenoti

Zenoti is designed for beauty, wellness, medspa, fitness, and multi-location businesses. Its current pricing is quote-based, with functionality spanning appointments, payroll, inventory, memberships, payments, marketing, reputation management, and AI-assisted workflows.

The system is more enterprise-oriented than simple studio scheduling software. Zenoti also promotes AI tools for marketing, lead management, customer service, retention, employee scheduling, inventory, and business analysis.

### Best for Operational Complexity

Zenoti may suit businesses that would otherwise need several separate systems. The company states that many customers consolidate multiple subscriptions, although this is a vendor claim rather than independent proof of savings for every buyer.

A small independent studio should compare implementation demands and total contract cost carefully. Enterprise breadth can be valuable, but only when the organisation uses enough of the platform to justify it.

## 7. ABC Glofox and Enterprise Gym Platforms

ABC Glofox is aimed at gyms, studios, and fitness brands that need branded member experiences, payments, scheduling, and multi-location support. Public pricing is generally quote-led, so it is more difficult to compare directly with published entry prices from Vagaro or WellnessLiving.

For larger operators, quote-based pricing is not automatically a disadvantage. Enterprise contracts often depend on location count, member volume, implementation, integrations, and support requirements.

### When Enterprise Software Makes Sense

A growing multi-site gym brand should compare migration, API access, reporting, data ownership, and location-level management before focusing on subscription price. These operational factors can become more important than a small difference in monthly software cost.

Independent studios with simpler requirements may be better served by a platform with transparent pricing and fewer implementation demands.

## Market Data: Why Studio Software Is Becoming More Important

The Health & Fitness Association reported that **81 million Americans held a [fitness-facility membership](/fitness/fitness-statistics-by-activity-and-age/) in 2025**, up 5.2% from 2024. More than 100 million people used a facility when non-members were included.

The same research found that members made an estimated **7 billion visits** to fitness facilities in 2025. In the second quarter of 2026, boutique studios led the major facility segments with **2.5% year-over-year growth in visits per location**.

These figures do not prove that a particular software platform creates growth. They do show that operators are managing more active consumers across memberships, classes, payments, and different access models.

## What Studios Should Compare Before Switching

A platform comparison should include more than the headline subscription fee. The following costs and capabilities can materially change total value:

1. Monthly or annual software subscription.
2. Payment-processing rates and fixed transaction fees.
3. Branded app charges.
4. Marketing email or SMS fees.
5. Marketplace or customer-acquisition commissions.
6. Website and booking-widget costs.
7. Data migration and onboarding.
8. Multi-location pricing.
9. Reporting and API access.
10. Cancellation terms and contract length.

A business should model these costs using its own sales volume, staff count, and locations. A lower starting price can become more expensive once add-ons or transaction costs are included.

## Which Mindbody Alternative Is Best?

There is no universal best option. Vagaro is a strong candidate for small businesses seeking low published entry pricing, while WellnessLiving offers a broader studio-management bundle with published tiers.

PushPress is more appropriate for gym-centric operators, and Square can work well for appointment-heavy businesses already using its payment ecosystem. Mariana Tek, Zenoti, and Glofox are more relevant when multi-location management and operational complexity justify quote-based enterprise systems.

## FAQs

### What Is the Cheapest Mindbody Alternative?

Among the platforms reviewed here, PushPress has a free software tier and Vagaro publishes a low entry subscription price. The cheapest total option depends on payment volume, processing rates, required add-ons, and whether the business needs advanced marketing or branded apps.

### Is Vagaro Cheaper Than Mindbody?

Vagaro currently publishes a lower entry subscription price than Mindbody. However, Vagaro charges separately for several optional services, while Mindbody includes different features in its plans. Total cost depends on staff, locations, payments, and required functionality.

### Why Do Studios Leave Mindbody?

Public reviews and competitor comparisons frequently mention pricing, complexity, customer support, and the need for different integrations. Those experiences vary by business, so a studio should identify its own operational problem before assuming another platform will solve it.

### Does Any Alternative Match Mindbody's Consumer Marketplace?

Several platforms provide directories or booking discovery, but Mindbody's consumer marketplace remains a major differentiator. The company says its app reaches more than 3 million monthly shoppers, so studios relying on discovery should compare actual acquisition performance carefully.

### Should a Studio Choose Software Based on Monthly Price?

No. Subscription price is only one cost. Payment processing, marketing messages, branded apps, marketplace commissions, migration, multi-location charges, and contract terms can materially affect the amount a studio pays over a year.
