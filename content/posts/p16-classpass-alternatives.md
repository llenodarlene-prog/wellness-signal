# 8 ClassPass Alternatives and How Their Membership Models Differ

ClassPass combines studio bookings, gym access, wellness services, and a credit-based membership. That makes ClassPass alternatives difficult to compare, because many competing programmes solve only part of the same problem.

Some alternatives provide access to large gym networks through employers or health plans. Others offer public multi-gym memberships, while digital services replace in-person classes with on-demand workouts at a much lower cost.

The best ClassPass alternative therefore depends on why someone uses ClassPass in the first place. A person who wants boutique Pilates classes has different needs from someone who mainly wants access to several gyms or home workouts.

## Key Takeaways

- ClassPass uses variable credits rather than one fixed cash price for each booking.
- Wellhub and One Pass Select can offer large multi-provider networks, but access generally depends on employer or health-plan eligibility.
- Active&Fit Direct is also eligibility-based, while Active&Fit Now is available to US adults without an employer sponsor.
- Active&Fit Now currently advertises a **$32 monthly standard gym option plus a $29 enrolment fee**, with premium studios priced separately.
- Apple Fitness+ costs **$9.99 per month or $79.99 annually in the US**, but it is digital-only rather than a studio-booking marketplace.
- Nike Training Club provides free guided workouts, while FitOn offers free workouts with paid PRO features.
- The US fitness market increasingly includes non-traditional access. The Health & Fitness Association reported that more than **100 million Americans** used a fitness facility in 2025 when non-members were included.
- No alternative reviewed here exactly duplicates ClassPass's combination of boutique studios, credits, and consumer marketplace access.

## How We Chose ClassPass Alternatives

We selected programmes that replace at least one major ClassPass use case: multi-gym access, boutique studio access, employer-funded fitness networks, or digital workout variety. We used current official programme information wherever possible.

The list intentionally includes both in-person and digital alternatives. They are not direct equivalents, so the article identifies the limitations of each option instead of ranking them as if their access models were identical.

## How ClassPass Works as the Baseline

ClassPass members buy credits and use those credits for eligible classes, gym visits, and wellness services. The credits required for a booking can change based on location, time, popularity, equipment, promotions, and other factors.

That variable pricing is a defining feature of the [ClassPass business model](/fitness/classpass-business-model-studio-partners/). It gives users flexibility across providers, but it can make the effective cost of a particular class harder to predict than a fixed gym membership. Before switching, estimate your current [ClassPass cost](/classpass-cost-calculator/) per class so each alternative has a fair baseline.

## 1. Wellhub

Wellhub is a corporate wellness platform formerly known as Gympass. It connects eligible employees with gyms, studios, and digital wellness providers through employer-sponsored access.

Wellhub reported in 2026 that its network had surpassed **100,000 fitness and wellness partners globally**. The company also said it connected nearly 40,000 corporate clients and more than 5 million employee subscribers at the beginning of the year.

### How Wellhub Differs From ClassPass

Wellhub is primarily a B2B employee benefit rather than a direct consumer membership. That means a person generally needs an employer that offers the service before they can choose a plan.

The platform includes gyms, studios, and wellness apps, which makes it one of the closer conceptual alternatives to ClassPass. However, the employer-funded structure changes both eligibility and pricing.

Wellhub's corporate pricing is set with employers, and employees then choose from the membership plans their employer makes available. Those corporate prices are not equivalent to an individual consumer subscription.

## 2. One Pass Select

One Pass Select is a subscription-based fitness and wellbeing programme offered through UnitedHealthcare-related employer arrangements. It provides access to gym networks, digital fitness, and additional benefits through different membership tiers.

UnitedHealthcare states that the programme has no long-term contracts or annual gym registration fees and allows members to change tiers monthly. The company also reports that **66% of employees who enrolled were actively engaged** in its 2024 utilisation data.

### Pricing and Network Access

A published member flyer shows tiers ranging from a digital-only option through Classic, Standard, Premium, and Elite. Pricing depends on the tier and the sponsoring plan, so members should check current rates through their eligible plan.

One Pass Select works best as a ClassPass alternative for people who receive access through an employer or eligible UnitedHealthcare arrangement. It is not a generally available consumer subscription for everyone.

## 3. Active&Fit Direct

Active&Fit Direct is offered through participating employers, health plans, and membership organisations. The programme is operated by American Specialty Health Fitness.

Its current public information advertises standard fitness memberships from **$28 per month**, plus a programme enrolment fee and applicable taxes. The programme includes access to thousands of gyms, discounted premium studios, digital workouts, and selected wellbeing services.

### When It Makes Sense

Active&Fit Direct is attractive to eligible users who value gym access more than boutique-class variety. Members can also purchase access to additional gyms, although premium studios may carry separate pricing.

The main limitation is eligibility. Users must access the programme through a sponsoring organisation rather than subscribing directly as ordinary consumers.

## 4. Active&Fit Now

Active&Fit Now is the public version of the Active&Fit model. It is available to US residents aged 18 and older without requiring an employer or health-plan sponsor.

The programme currently advertises access to **8,800+ gyms** for **$32 per month**, plus a $29 enrolment fee (waived with code JUSTMOVE through December 31, 2026) and applicable taxes. It also lists more than 3,500 premium exercise studios with discounts at many locations.

### A Stronger Public Alternative for Gym Users

Active&Fit Now is one of the more practical alternatives for someone whose main ClassPass use is multi-gym access. It has fixed monthly gym pricing rather than a credit model.

However, premium boutique studios are not necessarily included in the base $32 membership. Their pricing can exceed the standard fee, so users looking for Pilates, barre, cycling, or specialist studios should check local availability first.

## 5. Apple Fitness+

Apple Fitness+ replaces in-person studio discovery with a digital fitness subscription. It currently costs **$9.99 per month or $79.99 per year in the United States** after the standard introductory trial.

The service includes 12 workout types, meditation, personalised recommendations, and sessions from five to 45 minutes. It can also display real-time metrics when used with supported devices.

### Best for Home and Travel Workouts

Apple Fitness+ is much cheaper than maintaining access to multiple physical studios, but it does not provide in-person gym or class bookings. It is therefore an alternative for users who value workout variety more than venue access.

The subscription can be shared with up to five family members. That can make the effective household cost lower for families already using Apple devices.

## 6. Nike Training Club

Nike Training Club offers guided workouts without a paid fitness subscription. Nike describes the app as providing free training guidance across strength, HIIT, yoga, bodyweight, and other formats.

Nike also states that its training apps provide access to more than **185 free workouts** for members. The app focuses on digital training rather than local studio reservations.

### Best for Users Who Mainly Need Programming

Someone using ClassPass for workout variety rather than social or in-person experiences may find Nike Training Club sufficient. There are no credits to manage and no monthly studio subscription.

The trade-off is obvious: users supply their own training space and equipment. The app cannot replace access to reformer Pilates machines, specialised studios, pools, or full gym facilities.

## 7. FitOn

FitOn offers a large library of free digital workouts, including cardio, strength, Pilates, yoga, dance, barre, and meditation. The company says workouts remain free, while its paid PRO subscription adds features such as structured programmes, meal plans, offline downloads, premium music, and certain device integrations.

FitOn also reports a community of more than **15 million members**. That is a company-provided figure rather than an independently audited active-user count.

### Where FitOn Fits

FitOn is useful for people seeking guided classes at home without paying for each workout. It is not a substitute for in-person studio access.

The free model makes it particularly relevant for users who find ClassPass expensive but do not need physical facilities. Paid PRO features are optional rather than necessary for access to the core workout library.

## 8. A Direct Gym or Studio Membership

Sometimes the best ClassPass alternative is not another aggregator. A direct membership can offer predictable pricing, priority booking, unlimited classes, or member-only benefits at a venue someone uses frequently.

The Health & Fitness Association reported **81 million [US fitness-facility members](/fitness/fitness-statistics-by-activity-and-age/) in 2025**, with more than 100 million total facility users when flexible and non-member access was included. That indicates consumers are using both conventional memberships and alternative access models.

### When Direct Membership Can Cost Less

Someone who attends the same Pilates studio three times each week may receive better value from an unlimited studio membership than from a variable-credit marketplace. Conversely, a traveller or someone who wants several workout types may still benefit from an aggregator.

The comparison requires actual usage. Users should calculate what they paid per visit over several months rather than comparing headline membership prices alone.

## Market Data: Flexible Access Is Becoming More Important

The US fitness market is not limited to traditional monthly gym memberships. HFA reported that more than **100 million Americans used a fitness facility in 2025** when members and non-members were combined.

Earlier HFA data also found that total customer penetration exceeded conventional membership penetration because people increasingly used day passes, aggregators, employer programmes, and other flexible arrangements.

Employer-funded platforms are also expanding. Wellhub reported reaching **1 billion cumulative check-ins in 2025**, up from 100 million in 2022, although this is company-reported platform data rather than an industry-wide measure.

## Which ClassPass Alternative Is Best?

For employer-sponsored access, Wellhub and One Pass Select provide broad networks but require eligibility. Active&Fit Direct serves a similar sponsored market, while Active&Fit Now provides a public option for US adults.

For digital fitness, Apple Fitness+ offers a paid premium library, while Nike Training Club and FitOn provide substantial free workout access. A direct gym or studio membership remains the better comparison for anyone who repeatedly visits the same venue.

## How to Compare the Real Cost

Before cancelling ClassPass, review three months of actual bookings and calculate the following:

1. Total subscription and additional-credit spending.
2. Average number of in-person visits each month.
3. Most frequently used studios or gyms.
4. Effective cost per visit.
5. Late-cancellation or missed-booking charges.
6. How often credits expire or roll over.
7. Whether digital workouts could replace some paid visits.
8. Whether an employer already provides Wellhub, One Pass, or Active&Fit access.

This approach avoids switching to a cheaper-looking programme that does not cover the activities someone actually uses.

## FAQs

### What Is the Closest Alternative to ClassPass?

Wellhub is one of the closest models because it connects users with a large network of gyms, studios, and wellness apps. However, it is generally offered through employers, so it is not a direct consumer replacement available to everyone.

### Is There a Cheaper ClassPass Alternative?

Yes, depending on the goal. Active&Fit Now offers standard gym access from $32 monthly plus an enrolment fee, while Nike Training Club and FitOn provide free digital workouts. These options do not replicate ClassPass's boutique-studio credit system.

### Can I Use Multiple Gyms Without ClassPass?

Yes. Programmes such as Active&Fit Now, Active&Fit Direct, One Pass Select, and Wellhub can provide access to multiple fitness locations, depending on eligibility and plan. Network coverage and premium studio charges vary by programme.

### Is Wellhub Available Without an Employer?

Wellhub is primarily an employer-sponsored wellbeing benefit. Individual availability generally depends on whether a person's employer offers access. Consumers seeking a public multi-gym programme may find Active&Fit Now more straightforward.

### Is Apple Fitness+ a Good ClassPass Replacement?

It can replace the digital-workout side of ClassPass for people comfortable exercising at home or while travelling. It does not provide access to physical gyms or studios, so it is not a direct replacement for in-person bookings.

### Should I Choose ClassPass or a Direct Studio Membership?

A direct membership is often more predictable for people who repeatedly attend one venue. ClassPass may provide more value for users who regularly visit several studios, travel often, or want different fitness formats without several separate memberships.
