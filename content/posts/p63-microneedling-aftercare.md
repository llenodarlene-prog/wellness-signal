# Microneedling Aftercare: What Healing Looks Like and When to Call Your Provider

Good microneedling aftercare starts with your provider's written instructions, because recovery depends on the device, needle depth, treatment area and your skin. Redness, tightness and some peeling are common after the procedure. This guide explains what's typical according to the FDA, how radiofrequency (RF) microneedling differs, and which symptoms mean you should call your provider.

**Quick answer:** Follow the aftercare plan from the clinician who treated you, keep your routine gentle and protect your skin from the sun. Contact your provider promptly if symptoms get worse instead of better.

## First, Know What Treatment You Had

| Procedure | Why it matters for aftercare |
|---|---|
| Standard professional microneedling | Needle depth and passes are chosen by your provider |
| RF microneedling | Adds heat, with additional reported risks |
| At-home roller | Different design, hygiene concerns and often unclear depth |
| Microneedling with serums or other treatments | Not covered by microneedling-only safety information |

The FDA says microneedling devices are not approved for delivering cosmetics, topical medicines, vitamin solutions, drugs or blood products such as PRP into the skin. It also warns that reusing a needle cartridge is unsafe, even if it's cleaned. Ask your provider whether a new cartridge is used for each patient and each session.

## What's Normal After Microneedling

The FDA lists bleeding, bruising, redness, tightness, itching and peeling as possible effects, and says these typically go away without treatment after a few days or weeks. Its device page also lists less common risks: infection, burning and pigment changes, meaning skin that gets darker (hyperpigmentation) or lighter (hypopigmentation).

The FDA recommends having microneedling done by a health care provider with special training, because longer needles can reach nerves and blood vessels. It also lists a current cold sore outbreak as a reason not to have treatment.

### Keep a Simple Recovery Log

| Time | What to note |
|---|---|
| Treatment day | Redness, swelling, stinging and your provider's instructions |
| Next few days | Whether redness and discomfort are improving |
| Later | Any lasting color changes or symptoms that haven't settled |

Photos in similar lighting can help your provider judge a problem.

## Skincare After Microneedling

Keep your routine simple. Use only the cleanser and moisturizer your provider approves, and don't add new exfoliants, acids, scrubs or active serums until they say it's okay. Treated skin can react differently to products you normally use.

Sun protection matters, but ask your provider which sunscreen to use and when to start, since freshly treated skin can be sensitive.

## When to Call Your Provider

Contact your provider promptly if you notice:

- Pain, redness or swelling that's getting worse instead of better
- Spreading warmth, pus or fever
- Blisters or burns
- Skin color changes that don't fade
- Anything else that worries you

Seek urgent care for severe symptoms.

## RF Microneedling Is Different

RF microneedling uses microneedles to deliver radiofrequency energy that heats the skin. On October 15, 2025, the FDA issued a safety communication saying it was aware of reports of serious complications with some uses of RF microneedling, including burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement and nerve damage, sometimes needing surgical repair or medical treatment. Don't assume standard and RF aftercare are the same.

## Questions to Ask Before You Leave

- What device did you use, and was it RF?
- What reaction should I expect at these settings?
- Which cleanser, moisturizer and sunscreen should I use, and when?
- When can I restart my usual skincare and makeup?
- Who do I contact after hours if something goes wrong?
- Do I need a follow-up visit?

## FAQs

### What Should I Put on My Skin After Microneedling?

Only what your provider approves, usually a gentle cleanser and moisturizer. The FDA notes microneedling devices aren't approved for pushing cosmetics, medicines or other products into the skin.

### How Long Does Redness Last After Microneedling?

The FDA says effects like redness, tightness, itching and peeling typically go away without treatment after a few days or weeks. Redness that gets worse, especially with pain, pus or fever, needs prompt attention.

### Can I Use Retinol After Microneedling?

Not until your provider says so. Ask when to restart retinoids, acids and other active products.

### When Can I Exercise After Microneedling?

Ask the provider who treated you. Heat, sweat and friction can irritate treated skin, and timing may depend on how deep the treatment was.
