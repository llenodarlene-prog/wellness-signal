# Average Steps per Day: What US Walking Data Actually Shows

"How many steps does the average person take?" sounds like a simple question. Two studies can give quite different answers, though. A phone left on a desk misses steps. A wristband may count arm swings. Research devices use their own rules for how long they must be worn and what counts as a step.

So there's no single, current figure for average steps per day in the US. What we do have are carefully measured studies of specific groups. Here's what they show and how to read them.

## What US Research Measured

One of the most detailed US sources is the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) for 2005 to 2006. Participants wore research accelerometers for up to a week. A study of that data analyzed 3,388 adults aged 20 and over.

| Group | Median steps per day |
|---|---|
| Men, least active fifth | 2,247 |
| Men, most active fifth | 12,334 |
| Women, least active fifth | 1,755 |
| Women, most active fifth | 9,824 |

These are medians for the lowest and highest fifths of each group, not the overall average. The gap between the least and most active people is huge, which is why one "average" figure can mislead. The data is also 20 years old, so it describes that period, not 2026.

## Why Step Averages Differ So Much

Studies vary by country, year, age mix, device and how they handle time when the device isn't worn. Who takes part matters too.

- **Research surveys** like NHANES try to represent the population, using weights and set wear rules.
- **App and wearable data** describe the company's own customers. People who track steps may already walk more than average.
- **Phones** only count steps when they're on you, so they miss time spent at home without them.

Both kinds of data can be useful, but they answer different questions. Before quoting any average, check the year, the ages, the device and whether it's a mean or a median.

## Average Steps vs Healthy Steps

"Americans average X steps" describes what people do. "More steps are linked to lower risk" is a health claim. They shouldn't sit in the same table.

A 2025 review in *Lancet Public Health* pooled 57 studies from 35 groups of people. For death from any cause, heart disease, dementia and falls, the benefits of extra steps started to level off at around 5,000 to 7,000 steps a day. That's a finding about health links, not the average American's step count. Our [how many steps per day](/fitness/how-many-steps-per-day/) guide covers that research in detail.

## Comparing Ages and Abilities

Step counts often differ by age because of retirement, mobility, jobs and routines. When you see figures for different ages, check that both groups wore the same devices for similar periods.

Step counters also miss a lot. Cycling, swimming, strength training and seated exercise barely register. For people who use a wheelchair or have limited mobility, a step count isn't a fair measure of activity. A step total is one activity measure, not a score of how healthy you are.

## How to Find Your Own Baseline

Instead of comparing yourself with an internet average, track a week or two with the same device. Look at your weekday and weekend patterns. Then, if it suits you, add a manageable number of steps rather than chasing a fixed goal.

## FAQs

### How Many Steps Does the Average American Take a Day?

There's no single reliable current figure for all US adults. Published estimates come from specific groups, devices and years. In 2005 to 2006 NHANES data, men in the most active fifth had a median of 12,334 steps a day, and those in the least active fifth had 2,247.

### Are Phone Step Counts Accurate?

They're useful for your own trends, but they miss steps when you're not carrying your phone. Research devices follow set wear rules and still have limits. Don't assume counts from two different devices are directly comparable.

### Is 10,000 Steps the Average?

No. Ten thousand steps is a popular goal, not the typical US step count. Research on step levels looks at links with health outcomes, and a 2025 review found many benefits started to level off around 5,000 to 7,000 steps.

### Do Indoor Steps Count?

Most step counters record indoor and outdoor walking. Accuracy varies with where you wear the device, your speed and how you walk. Slow walking and walking aids can reduce the count, so treat your total as an estimate.
