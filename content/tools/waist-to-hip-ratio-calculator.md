Your waist-to-hip ratio compares the size of your waist with the size of your hips. It's a quick way to see where your body stores fat, since fat around the middle is linked to higher health risks than fat on the hips. This waist-to-hip ratio calculator divides one by the other and compares the result with the World Health Organization's cut-off points.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Choose your sex. WHO uses a different cut-off for men and women.
2. Measure your waist and hips in the same unit, then enter both.
3. Read your ratio and how it compares with the WHO cut-off.

## How to Measure

The WHO's 2008 expert consultation describes a standard method:

- **Waist:** Midway between the lower edge of your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone (iliac crest).
- **Hips:** Around the widest part of your buttocks, with the tape parallel to the floor.
- **Position:** Stand with feet close together and arms at your sides. Measure at the end of a normal breath out.
- **Repeat:** Take each measurement twice. If they're within 1 cm, use the average.

## How the Calculator Works

Waist-to-hip ratio = waist ÷ hip, using the same unit for both.

| Sex | WHO cut-off | What WHO links it to |
|---|---|---|
| Men | 0.90 or more | Substantially increased risk of metabolic complications |
| Women | 0.85 or more | Substantially increased risk of metabolic complications |

### A Worked Example

A woman with a 32-inch waist and 40-inch hips:

1. **Ratio:** 32 ÷ 40 = **0.80**
2. **Compare:** 0.80 is below the WHO cut-off of 0.85 for women.

A man with a 38-inch waist and 40-inch hips has a ratio of 38 ÷ 40 = **0.95**, which is above the 0.90 cut-off for men.

## Waist-to-Hip vs Waist-to-Height

Waist-to-hip ratio looks at fat distribution. Waist-to-height ratio compares your waist with your height and is used in UK NICE guidance. ## Limitations

- **It's a screening measure, not a diagnosis.**
- **Measurement errors matter.** A small change at either site moves the ratio, so measure carefully and the same way each time.
- **The cut-offs aren't universal.** WHO's report notes that appropriate cut-offs can vary between populations.
- **It doesn't suit everyone,** including during pregnancy.

## FAQs

### What Is a Healthy Waist-to-Hip Ratio?

WHO links a ratio of 0.90 or more in men and 0.85 or more in women to a substantially increased risk of metabolic complications. A ratio below those cut-offs is generally considered lower risk, though it's one measure among many.

### How Do I Calculate My Waist-to-Hip Ratio?

Divide your waist measurement by your hip measurement, using the same unit. A 32-inch waist and 40-inch hips give a ratio of 0.80.

### Where Should I Measure My Waist?

WHO's method places the tape midway between your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone, measured after a normal breath out. This isn't always at your belly button.
