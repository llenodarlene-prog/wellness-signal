VO2 max is the most oxygen your body can use during hard exercise, measured in milliliters per kilogram of body weight per minute. Labs measure it with a mask on a treadmill. This VO2 max calculator estimates it from a simple field test instead: how far you can run in 12 minutes, using the formula from Kenneth Cooper's 1968 study.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Warm up, then run as far as you can in exactly 12 minutes on a flat, measured course such as a track.
2. Enter the distance and choose miles, kilometers or meters.
3. Read your estimated VO2 max in mL/kg/min.

## How the Calculator Works

VO2 max (mL/kg/min) = (distance in meters − 504.9) ÷ 44.73

Distances in miles are converted at 1,609.344 meters per mile.

### A Worked Example

Say you cover 1.5 miles in 12 minutes.

1. **Convert:** 1.5 × 1,609.344 = 2,414 meters
2. **Estimate:** (2,414 − 504.9) ÷ 44.73 = **42.7 mL/kg/min**

## How to Read Your Result

A field estimate and a lab measurement aren't the same thing, so compare with care. For context, the FRIEND registry's measured treadmill averages for adults aged 40 to 49 are 38.8 mL/kg/min for men and 27.9 for women. ## Limitations

- **Pacing matters.** Starting too fast or too slow lowers your distance and your estimate.
- **Conditions change the result.** Wind, heat, hills and surface all affect distance.
- **It's an all-out effort.** Check with a clinician first if you have a heart condition, symptoms such as chest pain, or are new to exercise.

## FAQs

### How Do I Calculate My VO2 Max?

Run as far as you can in 12 minutes, then use Cooper's formula: subtract 504.9 from your distance in meters and divide by 44.73. Covering 1.5 miles (2,414 meters) gives about 42.7 mL/kg/min.

### Is the Cooper Test Accurate?

It's a reasonable estimate for people who can pace a hard 12-minute run. A lab test that measures oxygen directly is more accurate. Treat the result as a benchmark to retest over time.
