Your waist-to-height ratio compares your waist size with your height. It's a simple way to check for fat carried around the middle, which NICE links to higher risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease. The UK's National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) includes it in its 2025 obesity guideline.

The rule of thumb is easy to remember. Keep your waist to less than half your height.

## How the Calculator Works

Divide your waist by your height, using the same unit for both. NICE uses these bands for adults.

| Waist-to-height ratio | NICE band |
|---|---|
| 0.4 to 0.49 | Healthy, no increased health risk |
| 0.5 to 0.59 | Increased health risk |
| 0.6 or more | High health risk |

The calculator also shows the waist size that would keep you under 0.5, which is simply half your height.

### A Worked Example

Someone 66 inches tall with a 32-inch waist has a ratio of 32 ÷ 66 = 0.48. That's in the healthy band. A waist under 33 inches keeps them below 0.5.

## How to Measure Your Waist

Where you measure changes the result, so be consistent.

1. Stand up straight.
2. Find the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hips.
3. Wrap the tape around your waist midway between the two.
4. Breathe out naturally before you read the tape.

NICE notes this spot is usually just above the belly button.

## Why Use It Alongside BMI?

BMI uses only height and weight, so it can't show where fat sits. Waist-to-height ratio adds that missing piece. NICE recommends using it for adults with a BMI under 35. Our [BMI Calculator](/bmi-calculator/) works out the first number.

## Limits to Keep in Mind

- **It's a screening tool.** A ratio above 0.5 is a reason to talk with a clinician, not a diagnosis.
- **It doesn't apply in pregnancy.** NICE's guideline doesn't cover pregnancy.
- **It works across groups.** NICE says the bands apply to adults of both sexes and all ethnicities with a BMI under 35, including people with high muscle mass.
- **Lower isn't always better.** NICE's healthy band starts at 0.4.

## FAQs

### What Is a Healthy Waist-to-Height Ratio?

NICE classes a ratio of 0.4 to 0.49 as healthy for adults. From 0.5 to 0.59 means increased health risk, and 0.6 or more means high health risk. Put simply, keep your waist to less than half your height.

### Is Waist-to-Height Ratio Better Than BMI?

It answers a different question. BMI compares weight with height, while waist-to-height ratio shows fat around the middle. NICE recommends using it with BMI for adults with a BMI under 35, since central fat is linked to extra health risk.

### Where Should I Measure My Waist?

NICE says to measure midway between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hips, usually just above the belly button, after breathing out naturally. Keep the tape level. Use the same spot each time so you can compare results over time.
