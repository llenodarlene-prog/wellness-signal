How much protein you need depends mostly on your body weight and how active you are. This protein calculator shows two sourced reference amounts: the adult Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) of 0.8 grams per kilogram, and the 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg range the International Society of Sports Nutrition suggests for most people who exercise. These are reference amounts, not a personal prescription.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter your body weight in pounds or kilograms.
2. Choose a scenario: general adult, or regular exercise.
3. Read your daily protein in grams, with the RDA shown for comparison.

## How the Calculator Works

Protein in grams per day = body weight in kilograms × grams per kilogram.

| Scenario | Grams per kg per day | Source |
|---|---|---|
| General adult | 0.8 | Adult RDA |
| Most exercising adults | 1.4 to 2.0 | ISSN position stand, 2017 |

### A Worked Example

Take someone who weighs 160 pounds, which is 72.6 kg.

1. **RDA:** 72.6 × 0.8 = about **58 g a day**
2. **Exercising:** 72.6 × 1.4 to 72.6 × 2.0 = about **102 to 145 g a day**

## What the Two Numbers Mean

The RDA for adults, 0.8 g/kg, is the amount that meets the needs of nearly all healthy adults. A 2017 review in *Advances in Nutrition* notes it has stayed essentially unchanged for more than 70 years. It's a minimum to avoid deficiency, not necessarily the best intake for everyone.

The International Society of Sports Nutrition's 2017 position stand says 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg a day is enough for most exercising people to build and maintain muscle. It notes higher intakes may help resistance-trained people keep lean mass while eating fewer calories.

## Limitations

- **Medical conditions change needs.** People with kidney disease should ask a clinician before raising protein intake.
- **Pregnancy and breastfeeding** raise protein needs. Ask your care team.
- **These are general references.** A registered dietitian can tailor targets to your health, goals and body composition.

## FAQs

### How Much Protein Do I Need a Day?

For healthy adults, the RDA is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight, about 58 g a day for someone who weighs 160 pounds. If you exercise regularly, the ISSN suggests 1.4 to 2.0 g/kg, which is about 102 to 145 g at that weight.

### How Much Protein Do I Need to Build Muscle?

The International Society of Sports Nutrition says 1.4 to 2.0 grams per kilogram a day is enough for most exercising people to build and maintain muscle. Resistance training is what drives muscle growth. Protein supports it.

### Can You Eat Too Much Protein?

The adult range for protein set by the National Academies is 10 to 35% of calories. People with kidney disease or other health conditions may need to limit protein, so they should check with a clinician first.
