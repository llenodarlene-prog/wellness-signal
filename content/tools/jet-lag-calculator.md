Jet lag happens when your body clock is still set to home while the clock on the wall says something else. Crossing time zones throws off your sleep, energy and appetite until your internal rhythm catches up.

This jet lag calculator estimates how many days that catching up may take. It uses the average adjustment rates in the CDC Yellow Book, the CDC's guide for travel health. You can also see how much shifting your sleep before you fly might help.

## How the Calculator Works

Your body clock adjusts gradually after you arrive. The CDC Yellow Book gives these average rates.

| Direction | Average adjustment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Eastward (e.g. US to Europe) | About 1 hour per day | Your clock must move earlier, which is harder |
| Westward (e.g. Europe to US) | About 1.5 hours per day | Your clock must move later, which comes more naturally |

The calculator divides the hours your clock needs to shift by that daily rate, and rounds up. Any hours you shift before the trip come off the total first.

### A Worked Example

A flight from New York to Paris crosses 6 time zones heading east.

1. **No preparation:** 6 hours ÷ 1 hour a day = about 6 days to adjust.
2. **With 3 days of pre-trip shifting:** going to bed and waking 1 hour earlier each day for 3 days leaves 3 hours, or about 3 days.

The return trip west covers the same 6 hours at 1.5 hours a day, so it takes about 4 days.

## Why Eastward Is Harder

The CDC Yellow Book explains that for most people, the body's internal clock runs on a cycle slightly longer than 24 hours. People tend to drift a little later each night anyway. Flying west asks your clock to go later, which fits that tendency. Flying east asks it to go earlier, which works against it.

## How to Reduce Jet Lag

The CDC Yellow Book suggests a few approaches.

- **Shift your sleep before you go.** For 2 to 3 days before the trip, move your sleep 1 hour a day toward your destination: earlier for eastward trips, later for westward ones.
- **Protect your sleep while traveling.** Sleep loss on the journey can make jet lag symptoms worse. The Yellow Book suggests avoiding too much alcohol on the flight.
- **Time your light.** Getting daylight at the right times helps your body clock shift. The Yellow Book also notes that a jet lag calculator can help you plan around your trip.
- **Plan your first days.** Expect to feel off for a few days, and schedule important events later in the trip where you can.

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine's options for jet lag include timed melatonin and timed light exposure, as the Yellow Book summarizes. Talk to a clinician before trying melatonin or sleep medicines, especially if you take other medicines or have health conditions.

## FAQs

### How Long Does Jet Lag Last?

It depends on how many time zones you cross and which direction you fly. The CDC Yellow Book gives average adjustment rates of about 1 hour per day after flying east and 1.5 hours per day after flying west. So a 6-hour eastward trip may take about 6 days.

### Is Jet Lag Worse Flying East or West?

Most people find flying east harder. Your body clock has to move earlier, against its natural tendency to drift later. The CDC Yellow Book puts the average adjustment at about 1 hour per day eastward and 1.5 hours per day westward.

### Can I Prevent Jet Lag Before My Trip?

You can reduce it. The CDC Yellow Book suggests shifting your sleep 1 hour a day toward your destination's time for 2 to 3 days before you fly. Earlier for eastward trips and later for westward ones may shorten how long you take to adjust.
