The Navy body fat method estimates body fat percentage from a few tape measurements and your height. It uses the circumference equations developed by Hodgdon and Beckett in 1984 for the US Navy. This Navy body fat calculator applies those equations: neck and waist for men, plus hips for women. All you need is a tape measure.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Choose the equation: male or female.
2. Enter your height, neck and waist. Women also enter their hips.
3. Use the same unit, inches or centimeters, for every measurement.
4. Read your estimated body fat percentage.

## How to Measure

- **Neck:** Just below the larynx (Adam's apple), with the tape sloping slightly down toward the front.
- **Waist, men:** Around the abdomen at the level of the navel.
- **Waist, women:** At the narrowest point of the waist.
- **Hips, women:** Around the widest part of the buttocks.
- **Height:** Standing straight, without shoes.

Take each measurement two or three times and use the average.

## How the Calculator Works

All measurements are in inches. Centimeters are converted at 2.54 cm per inch.

| Sex | Equation |
|---|---|
| Men | % body fat = 86.010 × log10(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log10(height) + 36.76 |
| Women | % body fat = 163.205 × log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log10(height) − 78.387 |

### A Worked Example

A man who is 70 inches tall with a 15-inch neck and a 34-inch waist:

1. **Waist − neck:** 34 − 15 = 19; log10(19) = 1.2788
2. **Height:** log10(70) = 1.8451
3. **Body fat:** 86.010 × 1.2788 − 70.041 × 1.8451 + 36.76 = **17.5%**

A woman who is 64 inches tall with a 13-inch neck, 28-inch waist and 38-inch hips gets 163.205 × log10(53) − 97.684 × log10(64) − 78.387 = **26.6%**.

## How Accurate Is It?

Tape methods are quick and cheap, but they're estimates. A 2023 study of people in eight weeks of military training found that circumference-based assessments did not accurately capture the body composition changes measured by a reference method. Use the Navy method to track trends with careful, consistent measuring, not as a precise number.

## Limitations

- **Small measuring errors move the result.** A half-inch difference at the waist can change the estimate noticeably.
- **It may not suit everyone,** such as people with very muscular necks or unusual body shapes.
- **It's not a DEXA scan.** For a precise measure, ask about lab methods.

## FAQs

### How Accurate Is the Navy Body Fat Calculator?

It's a reasonable estimate for tracking change, but not precise for an individual. Research has found circumference methods can miss real changes in body composition that lab methods detect.

### What Measurements Do I Need for the Navy Method?

Men need height, neck and waist at the navel. Women need height, neck, waist at the narrowest point and hips at the widest point.

### Why Do Women Need a Hip Measurement?

The female equation was built using waist plus hips minus neck, because women tend to store more fat around the hips. The male equation uses waist minus neck only.

### Is My Information Stored?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. It needs no account, doesn't save your entries and doesn't send them anywhere. Closing or refreshing the page clears everything you entered.
