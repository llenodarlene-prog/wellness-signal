Hiking burns more than walking the same distance on a sidewalk. The difference comes mostly from two things: the climbing and the pack on your back. This calculator accounts for both.

Enter your weight, your pack, the trail's distance and elevation gain, and your usual moving pace. It uses a published equation from the US Army Research Institute of Environmental Medicine, built from measured oxygen use while people walked with backpacks.

## How the Calculator Works

The estimate comes from the LCDA backpacking equation, published by Looney and colleagues in 2022 in *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*. It has three parts.

| Part | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Walking cost | Energy to walk at your speed on flat, firm ground |
| Grade cost | Extra energy to climb, or energy saved descending |
| Load factor | How much a backpack multiplies the cost |

The equation works in watts per kilogram of body weight. We convert that to calories using your weight and moving time. The load factor grows faster than the pack weight, so heavy packs cost much more than light ones.

### How We Handle Elevation

Trail apps usually give one elevation gain figure for the whole hike. We assume you climb that amount over half the distance and come back down over the other half, as on a loop or an out-and-back trail. The average grade is the gain divided by half the distance.

Descending still costs energy, just less than climbing. The equation handles this, so a steep downhill never counts as zero or negative calories. A point-to-point hike that only climbs, or only descends, will differ from this estimate.

### A Worked Example

Take a 160 lb hiker carrying a 15 lb daypack on a 6-mile loop with 1,200 ft of climbing, moving at 2 mph.

1. **Grade:** 1,200 ft over 3 miles of climbing is an average grade of about 7.6%.
2. **Time:** 6 miles at 2 mph is 3 hours of moving time.
3. **Intensity:** about 4.7 METs on the climb and 2.1 METs on the descent, for an average of 3.4.
4. **Result:** roughly 750 calories, or about 250 an hour.

Without the pack, the same hike comes to about 710 calories. On flat ground with no pack, it would be about 600.

## What Changes the Estimate Most

Elevation usually matters more than pack weight for a typical day hike. In the example above, climbing adds about 110 calories over a flat walk. The 15 lb pack adds about 40 more.

That changes on steep trails and with heavy packs.

- **More climbing:** raising the gain to 2,000 ft on the same 6 miles lifts the estimate to about 920 calories.
- **A heavier pack:** doubling the pack to 30 lb on the original hike lifts it to about 810.
- **Slower pace:** going slower burns fewer calories per hour, but the hike takes longer, so the total changes less than you might expect.

The calculator shows your average intensity on a MET scale. One MET is your energy use at rest. The CDC classes 3 to 5.9 METs as moderate activity and 6 or more as vigorous.

## How Accurate Is the Estimate?

Treat it as a planning number, not a measurement. The equation was developed from treadmill walking at about 2.5 to 4.0 mph. The adults in the study were fit and mostly male, carrying military packs of up to 62% of their body weight. Many hikers move slower than that range, especially on climbs.

Real trails also add costs the calculator can't see. A 2018 Army study found that existing equations underestimated energy use over mixed, complex terrain. Rocks, roots, mud, sand and snow all make each step harder. Heat, altitude and frequent stops change things too.

The figure is also gross calories. It includes the energy you'd burn at rest, which we set at 1 MET, or about 1.2 watts per kilogram.

## FAQs

### How many calories does hiking burn per hour?

It depends on your weight, pack, pace and the climbing. For a 160 lb hiker with a 15 lb pack on a 6-mile loop with 1,200 ft of gain at 2 mph, the estimate is about 250 calories an hour. Steeper trails and heavier packs push that higher.

### Does a heavier backpack burn more calories?

Yes. The equation multiplies your walking and climbing cost by a load factor. That factor rises faster than the pack weight itself, so a 30 lb pack costs more than twice the extra energy of a 15 lb one.

### Does hiking downhill burn calories?

Yes, but fewer than hiking uphill. On the example trail, the descent averages about 2.1 METs compared with 4.7 on the climb. Your muscles still work to control each step down. The calculator never lets a descent fall below your resting energy use.

### Why is my fitness watch number different?

Watches estimate energy from heart rate, motion and your profile, and each brand uses its own method. This calculator uses a published equation and the trail figures you enter. Neither is exact, so compare hikes using the same tool rather than mixing the two.

### Is my information stored?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. It needs no account, does not save your entries and does not send them anywhere. Closing or refreshing the page clears everything you entered.
