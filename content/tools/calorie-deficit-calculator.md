A calorie deficit means eating fewer calories than your body uses. This calorie deficit calculator subtracts the deficit you choose from your maintenance calories to show the daily intake that implies. It's simple arithmetic, not a prediction of how much weight you'll lose or how fast.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter your maintenance calories. If you don't know them, estimate them with the [TDEE Calculator](/tdee-calculator/).
2. Choose a deficit of 250, 500 or 750 calories a day, or enter your own.
3. Read your daily target and weekly deficit.

## How the Calculator Works

| Step | Formula |
|---|---|
| Daily target | Maintenance calories − daily deficit |
| Weekly deficit | Daily deficit × 7 |

### A Worked Example

Say your estimated maintenance is 2,300 calories a day and you choose a 500-calorie deficit.

1. **Daily target:** 2,300 − 500 = **1,800 calories a day**
2. **Weekly deficit:** 500 × 7 = 3,500 calories

## Why We Don't Predict Weight Loss

Many calculators turn a deficit into a promised number of pounds a week. We don't. Real weight change depends on your starting point, body composition, how your body adapts and how closely you track intake. The CDC notes that people who lose weight at a gradual, steady pace of about 1 to 2 pounds a week are more likely to keep it off.

## Safety Notes

- **Very low intakes need supervision.** NIDDK describes a very low-calorie diet as about 800 calories a day or less, supervised by a health care professional. The calculator warns you if your target falls that low.
- **Check with a clinician first** if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, have diabetes or another health condition, take medicines that affect weight or appetite, or have a history of disordered eating.

Once you have a target, the [Macro Calculator](/macro-calculator/) splits it into protein, carbs and fat, and the [Protein Calculator](/protein-calculator/) shows sourced protein amounts for your weight.

## Limitations

- **Maintenance is an estimate.** If your maintenance number is off, your target will be too.
- **Intake tracking is imprecise.** Food labels and portion estimates carry their own errors.
- **Your body adapts.** Energy use can change as your weight and activity change, so revisit your numbers over time.

## FAQs

### How Do I Calculate a Calorie Deficit?

Subtract the deficit you want from your maintenance calories. For example, a 2,300-calorie maintenance and a 500-calorie deficit gives a target of 1,800 calories a day. Use the TDEE Calculator if you need a maintenance estimate.

### How Big Should My Calorie Deficit Be?

There's no single right size, and it depends on your health and goals. The CDC notes that gradual loss of about 1 to 2 pounds a week is more likely to last. Talk to a clinician or registered dietitian before a large deficit, especially if you have a health condition.

### Is 1,200 Calories a Day Safe?

It depends on the person, and it isn't right for everyone. NIDDK describes diets of about 800 calories a day or less as very low-calorie diets that need health care supervision. For any low target, check with a clinician first.

### Is My Information Stored?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. It needs no account, doesn't save your entries and doesn't send them anywhere. Closing or refreshing the page clears everything you entered.
