Your basal metabolic rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body uses at rest to keep you breathing, circulating blood and maintaining temperature. This BMR calculator estimates it with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, from your age, sex, height and weight. It's an estimate of resting energy use, not your total daily calories.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Choose the sex used in the equation, since it has separate male and female versions.
2. Enter your age, weight and height, in either unit.
3. Read your estimated BMR in calories a day.

To estimate your total daily calories including activity, take your BMR to the [TDEE Calculator](/tdee-calculator/).

## How the Calculator Works

The calculator uses the simplified Mifflin-St Jeor equations, published in the *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* in 1990.

| Sex | Equation (weight in kg, height in cm, age in years) |
|---|---|
| Men | 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5 |
| Women | 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age − 161 |

The researchers built the equation from 498 healthy adults aged 19 to 78, whose resting energy use was measured with indirect calorimetry. They found the older Harris-Benedict equations overestimated measured values by about 5%.

### A Worked Example

Take a 35-year-old woman who is 5 feet 6 inches (167.6 cm) and weighs 160 pounds (72.6 kg).

1. **10 × weight:** 10 × 72.57 = 725.7
2. **6.25 × height:** 6.25 × 167.64 = 1,047.8
3. **5 × age:** 5 × 35 = 175
4. **BMR:** 725.7 + 1,047.8 − 175 − 161 = about **1,437 calories a day**

## BMR vs TDEE

BMR is only your resting energy use. Your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) adds everything you do on top: walking, working, exercising. For most people TDEE is roughly 1.4 to 2.4 times BMR. Our [TDEE Calculator](/tdee-calculator/) applies those activity levels, and the [Calorie Deficit Calculator](/calorie-deficit-calculator/) works from there.

## Limitations

- **It's a prediction.** The equation estimates an average for people like you. Your measured value could be higher or lower.
- **Body composition matters.** The same paper found fat-free mass was the best single predictor of resting energy, which the standard equation doesn't use. Very muscular people may burn more than predicted.
- **Age range.** The study included adults aged 19 to 78. Results outside that range are rougher.
- **Special situations.** Pregnancy, illness, some medicines and thyroid conditions change energy needs.

## FAQs

### What Is a Normal BMR?

There's no single normal number. BMR depends mainly on your body size, age and sex. Using Mifflin-St Jeor, a 35-year-old woman who is 5 feet 6 inches and 160 pounds has an estimated BMR of about 1,437 calories a day. Larger and younger people tend to have higher BMRs.

### Is BMR the Same as Calories Burned in a Day?

No. BMR is what your body burns at complete rest. Your total daily calories, or TDEE, also include activity and digesting food. For most people, total daily burn is about 1.4 to 2.4 times their BMR, depending on how active they are.

### Which BMR Formula Is Most Accurate?

No equation is exact for everyone. In the 1990 study that produced it, Mifflin-St Jeor predicted measured resting energy better than the older Harris-Benedict equations, which overestimated by about 5%. A lab test with indirect calorimetry is the way to measure your own rate.

### Is My Information Stored?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. It needs no account, doesn't save your entries and doesn't send them anywhere. Closing or refreshing the page clears everything you entered.
