Treadmill consoles show a calorie number, but each brand uses its own formula. This treadmill calorie calculator uses the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) walking and running equations. They estimate oxygen use from speed and incline, then convert it to calories for your body weight.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter your body weight.
2. Choose walking or running.
3. Enter your speed in mph, the incline and how many minutes you exercised.
4. Read your gross calories, plus net calories above what you'd burn at rest.

## How the Calculator Works

| Step | Formula |
|---|---|
| Walking | VO2 = 0.1 × speed + 1.8 × speed × grade + 3.5 |
| Running | VO2 = 0.2 × speed + 0.9 × speed × grade + 3.5 |
| Calories per minute | VO2 × weight (kg) ÷ 1,000 × 5 |

Speed is in meters per minute (mph × 26.82) and grade is a decimal, so 5% is 0.05. The 3.5 is resting oxygen use; removing it gives net calories.

### A Worked Example

A 160-pound person (72.6 kg) walks at 3 mph on a flat treadmill for 30 minutes.

1. **Speed:** 3 × 26.82 = 80.5 m/min
2. **VO2:** 0.1 × 80.5 + 3.5 = 11.5 mL/kg/min
3. **Per minute:** 11.5 × 72.6 ÷ 1,000 × 5 = 4.2 calories
4. **Total:** 4.2 × 30 = about **126 calories**, or about 88 net

## Getting a Better Estimate

A 2021 study in *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise* found the ACSM walking equation is most accurate at about 1.9 to 3.7 mph. A 2013 study in *Hippokratia* found the running equation inaccurate for predicting VO2 max in trained athletes. Don't hold the handrails, since that lowers your real effort. ## FAQs

### How Many Calories Does 30 Minutes on a Treadmill Burn?

It depends on your weight, speed and incline. A 160-pound person walking at 3 mph on a flat treadmill burns about 126 calories in 30 minutes. Adding incline or speed raises it.

### Does Incline Burn More Calories?

Yes. In the walking equation, each percent of grade adds oxygen cost on top of speed. Walking at 3 mph on a 5% incline uses about 63% more oxygen than walking on the flat.

### Is My Information Stored?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. It needs no account, doesn't save your entries and doesn't send them anywhere. Closing or refreshing the page clears everything you entered.
