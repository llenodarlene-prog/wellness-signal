Total daily energy expenditure (TDEE) is the number of calories you burn in a day, including rest, movement and exercise. This TDEE calculator estimates it by multiplying your basal metabolic rate by an activity level from the FAO/WHO/UNU report on human energy requirements. It shows a typical value and a range, because activity estimates are broad.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Enter the sex used in the equation, your age, weight and height.
2. Pick the activity level that best matches a typical week.
3. Read your estimated TDEE and the range for that activity level.

## How the Calculator Works

The calculator takes two steps.

1. **BMR.** It estimates your resting calories with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the same one used in our [BMR Calculator](/bmr-calculator/).
2. **Activity.** It multiplies BMR by a physical activity level (PAL). The FAO/WHO/UNU report defines PAL as total daily energy expenditure divided by BMR.

| FAO/WHO/UNU lifestyle | PAL range | Example used |
|---|---|---|
| Sedentary or light activity | 1.40 to 1.69 | 1.53 |
| Active or moderately active | 1.70 to 1.99 | 1.76 |
| Vigorous or vigorously active | 2.00 to 2.40 | 2.25 |

The example values come from the report's own worked tables of daily routines.

### A Worked Example

Take a 35-year-old woman who is 5 feet 6 inches and 160 pounds, with a BMR of about 1,437 calories a day.

1. **Sedentary or light:** 1,437 × 1.53 = about **2,199 calories a day**, with a range of 2,012 to 2,429
2. **Moderately active:** 1,437 × 1.76 = about 2,530 calories a day
3. **Vigorously active:** 1,437 × 2.25 = about 3,234 calories a day

## Using Your Result

Your TDEE is the rough number of calories that would keep your weight steady. To plan a smaller intake, use the [Calorie Deficit Calculator](/calorie-deficit-calculator/). To split calories into protein, carbs and fat, try the [Macro Calculator](/macro-calculator/).

The best check is your own data. If your weight holds steady over several weeks at a given intake, that intake is close to your real TDEE.

## Limitations

- **Activity bands are broad.** The FAO report designed them to estimate the needs of population groups, so one person can sit anywhere in a band, or outside it.
- **Two estimates stack up.** Errors in the BMR equation and in the activity level multiply together.
- **Exercise varies.** A few hard sessions a week and an active job can land in different bands, even if they feel similar.
- **Special situations.** Pregnancy, breastfeeding, illness and some medicines change energy needs.

## FAQs

### What Does TDEE Mean?

TDEE stands for total daily energy expenditure, the calories your body uses in a full day. It includes your resting metabolism plus everything you do, from walking to exercise. The FAO/WHO/UNU report expresses it as BMR multiplied by a physical activity level.

### Which Activity Level Should I Choose?

Pick the one that matches a typical week, not your best one. Most people with desk jobs and little exercise fit the sedentary or light band, with a PAL of 1.40 to 1.69. Regular daily activity fits the active band, and heavy physical work or hard training fits the vigorous band.

### How Accurate Is a TDEE Calculator?

It's an estimate that can be off by a few hundred calories a day. Both the BMR equation and the activity level are averages. Tracking your weight and intake for a few weeks gives a more personal number.

### Is My Information Stored?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. It needs no account, doesn't save your entries and doesn't send them anywhere. Closing or refreshing the page clears everything you entered.
