Most people use far less sunscreen than the label's SPF assumes. That SPF number comes from lab tests using a set amount: 2 milligrams of sunscreen for every square centimeter of skin. Apply less, and you get less protection than the bottle promises.

This sunscreen amount calculator works out how much that is for your body size and what you're wearing. It also tells you how much you'll need for a full day outside.

## How the Calculator Works

The calculator takes three steps.

1. **Body surface area.** It estimates your skin area from your height and weight with the Mosteller formula: the square root of height in cm times weight in kg, divided by 3,600.
2. **Exposed skin.** It applies the "rule of nines," a standard way to divide the body into areas. The head is 9%, each arm 9%, each leg 18%, the front and back of the trunk 18% each, and the groin 1%.
3. **Amount.** It multiplies exposed skin by 2 mg per square centimeter, the amount used in FDA SPF testing.

| Clothing preset | Skin exposed |
|---|---|
| Head and arms (short sleeves, long pants) | 27% |
| Shorts and tee (head, arms, lower legs) | 45% |
| Swimsuit (all but the groin and about half the trunk) | 81% |

### A Worked Example

Take an adult who is 5 feet 6 inches (167.6 cm) and 160 pounds (72.6 kg).

1. **Body surface area:** √(167.6 × 72.6 ÷ 3,600) = 1.84 square meters
2. **In a swimsuit:** 81% of that is about 14,900 square centimeters
3. **Sunscreen:** 14,900 × 2 mg = about 30 grams, or 30 mL, which is about 1 fluid ounce

That matches the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) advice that most adults need about 1 ounce, roughly a shot glass full, to cover skin not covered by clothing. In shorts and a tee, the same person needs about 17 mL per application.

## How Much for a Whole Day?

The AAD says to reapply every 2 hours, and right after swimming or sweating. The calculator counts your first application plus one every 2 hours you're outside. For 4 hours in shorts and a tee, that's 2 applications, or about 33 mL.

## Tips for Using Sunscreen

- **Apply early.** The AAD says to put sunscreen on about 15 minutes before going outside.
- **Choose the right type.** The AAD recommends broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher.
- **Don't forget easy-to-miss spots.** The AAD reminds you to cover your neck, face, ears and the tops of your feet.
- **Use lip balm too.** The AAD suggests a lip balm with SPF 30 or more.

## FAQs

### How Much Sunscreen Should I Use on My Body?

The AAD says most adults need about 1 ounce, roughly a shot glass full, to cover skin not covered by clothing. Larger people need more. SPF testing uses 2 mg per square centimeter of skin, which this calculator uses to estimate your amount.

### How Much Sunscreen Do I Need for My Face?

The head, including the face and neck, makes up about 9% of your skin under the rule of nines. For an average adult that works out to roughly 3 mL at the 2 mg/cm² used in SPF testing. Use the "head and arms" preset as a starting point.

### How Often Should I Reapply Sunscreen?

The AAD advises reapplying every 2 hours when you're outdoors, and right after swimming or sweating. Water-resistant sunscreen still needs reapplying. Check your product's label for its water-resistance time.
