A step count tells you how much you moved, but not how much energy it took. This calculator converts steps into an estimate of calories burned, using your weight, your walking pace and your step length. It also shows the distance, the walking time and the calories for every 1,000 steps.

Enter your steps and weight, pick a pace, and open the advanced options if you want to use your height or a measured step length.

## How to Use the Steps to Calories Calculator

1. **Enter your steps** or drag the slider.
2. **Enter your weight** and choose pounds or kilograms.
3. **Pick a walking pace** from the cards.
4. **Optional:** open Advanced options to set your step length from height or a measurement.

The chart shows the same step count at four paces, so you can see how speed changes the estimate.

## How Steps Become Calories

The calculator works in three stages.

| Stage | Method |
|---|---|
| Distance | Steps × step length |
| Walking time | Distance ÷ pace |
| Calories | ACSM walking equation on flat ground × weight × time |

The ACSM walking equation estimates oxygen use from speed: 0.1 × speed (meters per minute) + 3.5 on level ground. Each liter of oxygen corresponds to about 5 calories. The result is a gross figure that includes resting energy.

### A Worked Example

A 160 lb person who is 5 feet 6 inches tall takes 10,000 steps at 3.0 mph. Their estimated step length is about 27.3 inches, so they cover about 4.3 miles in about 1 hour 26 minutes. The equation gives roughly 360 calories, or about 36 calories per 1,000 steps.

## Why Pace Changes the Estimate

At a faster pace you burn more calories per minute, but you finish the same distance sooner. Because this estimate is gross, it also counts resting energy for every minute you walk. On flat ground that makes the per-step figure slightly higher at slower paces, which is what the comparison bars show. Hills, stairs and carrying a load have a larger effect, and step counts alone cannot capture them. ## How Accurate Is a Steps to Calories Estimate?

Every stage adds uncertainty. Step length is estimated unless you measure it, trackers can miscount steps, and the ACSM equation itself has known error. A 2021 study by Moore and colleagues found that the ACSM equation is most accurate at about 1.9 to 3.7 mph and was biased by around 0.6 to 0.8 METs in their sample.

The estimate is best used for comparing days or tracking trends rather than exact calorie counting. If your tracker also reports calories, it may use heart rate or its own algorithm, so the numbers will not match exactly.

## Making Steps Count

Steps are an easy way to track daily movement, but activity guidelines are written in minutes. The CDC says adults need 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, and it lists walking briskly, at 2.5 mph or faster, as moderate activity. Steps taken at a brisk pace count toward that goal more clearly than slow, incidental steps around the house.

## FAQs

### How many calories do 10,000 steps burn?

It depends on your weight, step length and pace. For a 160 lb person with a 27-inch step walking at 3.0 mph, 10,000 steps burns roughly 360 calories, including resting energy. Heavier people burn more, and hills or stairs raise the figure further.

### How many calories are burned per 1,000 steps?

Using this calculator's method at 3.0 mph with a 27-inch step, the estimate ranges from about 27 calories per 1,000 steps at 120 lb to about 56 at 250 lb. Body weight and step length drive most of the difference. The calculator shows your own figure based on the details you enter.

### Does walking faster burn more calories per step?

Not in this estimate. Faster walking burns more per minute, but you cover the distance sooner, and the gross figure includes resting energy for every minute walked. On flat ground slower walks therefore show slightly more calories per step. Hills, stairs and carrying weight have a much bigger effect.

### Why is my fitness tracker's calorie number different?

Trackers use their own algorithms, often combining heart rate, movement and personal details. They may also count steps differently. Both figures are estimates, so comparing trends over time is more useful than matching exact numbers.

### Is my information saved?

No. All calculations run in your browser. Your steps, weight and height are not stored, sent to analytics or shared with anyone.
