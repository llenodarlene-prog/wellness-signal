Heart rate recovery is how much your heart rate falls in the first minute after you stop exercising hard. This heart rate recovery calculator subtracts your one-minute heart rate from your peak. It compares the result with the 12 beats per minute cut-off used in a landmark 1999 study in the *New England Journal of Medicine*.

## How to Use This Calculator

1. Exercise hard, then note your heart rate at the moment you stop: your peak.
2. Note your heart rate exactly one minute later.
3. Enter both, and read your one-minute recovery.

## How the Calculator Works

Heart rate recovery = heart rate at peak exercise − heart rate one minute after stopping.

### A Worked Example

Your heart rate is 160 bpm when you stop, and 135 bpm one minute later.

1. **Recovery:** 160 − 135 = **25 bpm**
2. **Compare:** 25 bpm is above the 12 bpm cut-off.

## What the Research Found

Cole and colleagues followed 2,428 adults for six years after symptom-limited exercise testing. They defined heart rate recovery as the drop from peak to one minute after exercise. A drop of 12 bpm or less was defined as abnormal, and 26% of patients fell into that group. A low value was strongly linked to death from any cause, even after adjusting for age, sex, workload and other risk factors.

These were adults referred for heart imaging, not a general fitness population, so the cut-off is a research marker rather than a fitness score. ## Limitations

- **It's not a medical test.** Clinical testing follows set protocols.
- **Readings depend on effort and cooldown.** Standing still, walking or lying down after exercise all change the result, so measure the same way each time.
- **Some medicines affect heart rate,** including beta blockers.

## FAQs

### What Is a Good Heart Rate Recovery?

In the 1999 NEJM study, a drop of 12 bpm or less in the first minute was defined as abnormal. A bigger drop is generally a sign of quicker recovery, but there's no single "good" number for everyone.

### How Do I Measure Heart Rate Recovery?

Note your heart rate the moment you stop hard exercise, then again exactly one minute later. Subtract the second from the first.

### Should I Worry About a Low Heart Rate Recovery?

A single low reading can come from measuring differences. If your recovery is repeatedly 12 bpm or less, or you have symptoms such as chest pain or faintness, talk to a clinician.
