# Editorial QA SOP

**Applies to:** Verdict Point, Fin Loom, Stack Row, Arizona Direct, and Wellness Signal
**Language:** US English
**Scope:** Website blogs, articles, PR blogs, and every platform version.

**Core standard:** Every article must answer the reader's question, provide verified information, and add useful value beyond existing coverage. Style, formatting, and keyword placement cannot compensate for missing substance.

## 1. Confirm the Assignment

Identify the primary reader question, audience and search intent, article type (statistics, research, explanation, process, comparison, review, news), the essential information needed, the primary keyword, brand requirements (word count, platform, link targets) and the article's contribution beyond existing coverage.

**QA check:** Does the draft fulfill the assigned subject and angle without drifting?

## 2. Answer the Query Directly

- Central answer in the opening paragraph or first substantive section, normally within 100 words.
- Each substantive section begins with the answer its heading promises (roughly 40 to 60 words where helpful).
- No introductions that only explain why the topic matters or promise an answer later.
- Key answer passages stand alone: name the subject and keep essential conditions.

**QA check:** Can a reader find the answer quickly and understand it without visiting another page?

## 3. Fulfill the Title's Promise

| Article type | Required substance |
|---|---|
| Statistics | Verified figures, comparisons or trends, source context, interpretation |
| Research | Findings, study details, implications, limitations |
| Definition or explanation | Definition, scope, mechanisms, examples, distinctions |
| How-to | Usable steps, prerequisites, inputs, constraints |
| Comparison | Consistent criteria, supported differences, tradeoffs, suitability |
| Review | Verified capabilities and limitations; documented specs separated from hands-on experience |
| News or prediction | What happened or is claimed, when, evidence, what remains unconfirmed |

**Reject:** a statistics article that mainly explains how to interpret statistics without substantive figures.

## 4. Verify Statistics, Research, and Data

For each material claim verify the original source and supporting passage, publication date and measurement period, population, sample and geography, units, denominator, definitions and methodology, limitations, and whether it is an observed finding, survey response, estimate, forecast, calculation or interpretation. Use primary sources. AI answers, search snippets and competitor summaries are not evidence.

**QA check:** Does the source support the exact wording and level of certainty?

## 5. Preserve Accurate Interpretation

Distinguish percentage from percentage-point changes, revenue from profit, accounts from users, access from enrollment, participation and outcomes, association from causation, and market spending from effectiveness. Compare figures only when definitions, populations and periods match. Show inputs and formulas for calculations. Label forecasts, estimates and hypotheticals. Keep qualifications beside the claim.

**QA check:** Could a reader draw a stronger conclusion than the evidence supports?

## 6. Provide Additional Reader Value

Each article needs at least one substantial contribution: a comparison across compatible findings, a clearer explanation, a transparent calculation, resolution of conflicting definitions, an evidence-based decision framework, or a specific supported example. Rewording existing articles is not added value.

**QA check:** Identify the specific passage that contributes beyond a summary of sources.

## 7. Never Invent Information

No fabricated statistics, studies, citations, quotations, interviews, expert commentary, product testing, firsthand experience, visits, case studies, author credentials, expert review, missing source details or unsupported predictions.

**QA check:** Can every claimed experience, finding and credential be verified?

## 8. Use Natural, Contextual Internal Linking

- Write the explanation first; place links on descriptive phrases already serving it.
- Verify the destination and its specific reader benefit.
- Default maximum of two internal or brand-content links per long-form article unless specific instructions override (the site SOP's silo rules are such an instruction). Evidence citations are separate.
- Never create a sentence or paragraph solely to hold a link.
- Avoid link wrappers such as "Wellness Signal's article explains", "Read our guide", "For a deep dive", "This breakdown covers".

**Three-part link test:** (1) remove the hyperlink: is the sentence still useful and natural? (2) does the anchor describe the destination? (3) what specific reader need does the destination serve? If no suitable destination exists, flag linking as incomplete.

## 9. Brand-Specific Checks

| Brand | Additional QA requirements |
|---|---|
| Verdict Point | Jurisdiction and effective dates; laws vs proposals vs opinions vs advice; never invent attorney review |
| Fin Loom | Currency, periods, fees, assumptions, definitions; historical returns vs projections; no guaranteed outcomes |
| Stack Row | Product versions, editions, release dates, methodologies, test conditions; vendor claims vs independent findings |
| Arizona Direct | Locality, service area, hours, prices, availability, dates; city vs county vs state |
| Wellness Signal | Study design, population, sample, duration, measured outcomes; animal or laboratory findings never presented as established human benefits |

## 10. Keyword Optimization

Primary keyword once in the main heading and twice in the body (once in the introduction), unless the brief sets other counts. Count headings, body and metadata separately. Keep placement natural; never bold the keyword.

## 11. Language, Structure, and Presentation

US English across all brands. Follow brand voice, sentence-length, word-limit and banned-word rules. No em dashes, filler, unsupported superlatives or formulaic promotion. Lists for steps, tables for comparisons, FAQs only when useful. Tables, captions, graphics, metadata and alt text must match verified claims.

## 12. Final QA Status

| Status | Requirement |
|---|---|
| PASS | All checks complete: answers, evidence, added value, links, keywords and writing meet the standard |
| REVISE | Correctable problems remain |
| HOLD | Essential evidence is missing, contradicted, fabricated or cannot support the angle |

A draft cannot PASS on style or keyword counts while substantive failures remain. When drafting or authorized to rewrite, fix failures before delivery; for audit-only requests, report issues and proposed corrections without silently rewriting.
